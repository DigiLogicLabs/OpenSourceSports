Section 1: Introduction

Craps is a dice game in which players bet on the outcome of a roll, or a series of rolls, of two dice. One player — the shooter — throws, but everyone at the table bets, and most bets are on the shooter succeeding.

The game itself is traditional and in the public domain. What varies is the house: individual casinos set their own table limits, odds multiples and which side bets are offered.

Section 2: Components

  • Two six-sided dice.
  • A marked craps table with betting areas, in the casino form.
  • Chips or an agreed stake. Street craps needs nothing but the dice and a wall.

Section 3: Setup

  • Players gather at the table; one is the shooter, and the role passes clockwise.
  • Before the first roll, players place a pass line or don't pass bet.
  • The shooter must have a pass-line bet down to throw.

Section 4: Gameplay

The Come-Out Roll

The shooter's first roll of a round:

  • 7 or 11 — "natural": pass line bets win immediately.
  • 2, 3 or 12 — "craps": pass line bets lose. (On 12, don't-pass usually pushes rather than wins.)
  • 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10: that number becomes the point.

Chasing the Point

Once a point is set, the shooter keeps rolling until one of two things happens:

  • The point is rolled again: pass line wins, and the shooter starts a new come-out.
  • A 7 is rolled ("seven out"): pass line loses and the dice pass to the next shooter.

Note the reversal: a 7 wins instantly on the come-out and loses instantly afterwards. That single inversion is the whole structure of the game.

Other Bets

  • Come / don't come: the same structure, started on any roll after the point.
  • Odds: an additional bet behind the pass line, paid at true odds — the only bet on the table with no house edge.
  • Place, field, hardways, proposition: single-roll or standing side bets, generally at worse odds.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

There is no cumulative score — each bet resolves in chips.

Common Payouts

  • Pass / don't pass: even money.
  • Odds behind the point: 2:1 on 4 or 10, 3:2 on 5 or 9, 6:5 on 6 or 8 — true odds.
  • Field, hardways, proposition bets: vary widely by house; the posted table is authoritative.

Because 7 is the most probable total with two dice, bets that win on 7 before a point resolve favourably and those that need the point first do not — which is why pass-line-plus-odds is the standard low-edge line.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

  • Street craps: no table and no house — players bet against each other, and the shooter covers the wagers.
  • Bank craps: the casino form described above, played against the house.
  • Crapless craps: 2, 3 and 12 become points instead of instant losses — sounds friendlier, is worse value.
  • High Point / New York craps: older regional forms with different come-out resolutions.
