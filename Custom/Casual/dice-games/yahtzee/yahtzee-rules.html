Section 1: Introduction

Yahtzee is a dice game in which players roll five dice up to three times a turn, then commit the result to one of thirteen boxes on a scorecard. Each box may be used exactly once, so the real decision is rarely what to roll — it is which box to spend.

The game was published in 1956 and is owned by Hasbro. Like UNO, it has no governing body — no federation issues its rules or sanctions competition — so it is catalogued here as a community entry despite being commercially published.

What this page is. An independent description of play, not a reproduction of Hasbro's rule sheet. The publisher's own rules are linked above and are authoritative where the two differ. Its public-domain ancestor Yacht uses much the same structure with a simpler scorecard.

Section 2: Components

  • Five six-sided dice.
  • A scorecard per player, with an upper and lower section.
  • A cup for rolling (optional).

Section 3: Setup

  • Each player takes a scorecard. Any number can play; the game is also perfectly playable solo.
  • Highest single roll goes first; play passes clockwise.
  • The game lasts exactly thirteen rounds — one per scorecard box.

Section 4: Gameplay

A Turn

  1. Roll all five dice.
  2. Set aside any you want to keep and reroll the rest.
  3. Repeat once more — three rolls maximum per turn.
  4. Enter a score in exactly one box. You may stop rolling early, and you may re-include previously set-aside dice in a reroll.

The Forced Zero

You must fill a box every turn. If nothing fits, you are forced to enter zero somewhere — usually a high-value box you have given up on. Managing which box you will eventually sacrifice is most of the game's skill.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

Upper Section

Six boxes — Aces through Sixes — each scoring the sum of dice showing that number. Score 63 or more across the upper section and earn a 35-point bonus, which is roughly three of each number.

Lower Section

  • Three of a kind: sum of all five dice
  • Four of a kind: sum of all five dice
  • Full house (three + two): 25 points
  • Small straight (four consecutive): 30 points
  • Large straight (five consecutive): 40 points
  • Yahtzee (five of a kind): 50 points
  • Chance: sum of all five dice, always legal

Yahtzee Bonus

A second or later Yahtzee, once the Yahtzee box is filled with 50, scores a 100-point bonus and may then be used as a joker in another box. If the Yahtzee box holds a zero, no bonus applies.

Highest total after thirteen rounds wins.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

  • Yacht: the public-domain ancestor — same three-roll structure, simpler scorecard, no bonuses.
  • Triple Yahtzee: three scorecards played simultaneously, with column multipliers.
  • Solitaire Yahtzee: the same game played alone against a target score.
  • Generala, Kismet, Yatzy: regional relatives with different category sets and bonuses.
  • Free joker: a common house rule loosening where a bonus Yahtzee may be scored.
