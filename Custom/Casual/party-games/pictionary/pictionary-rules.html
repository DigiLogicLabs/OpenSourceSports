Section 1: Introduction

Pictionary is a team guessing game in which one player communicates a secret word or phrase by drawing while teammates try to identify it before time expires. The drawer may turn an idea into pictures, symbols, diagrams, or a sequence of images, but may not communicate through speech, writing, gestures, or other prohibited clues.

These rules describe a self-contained recreational points game. They do not depend on a particular commercial edition, and no governing or sanctioning organization controls them. Before play begins, the group should agree on the winning score, the turn time, the prompt difficulty, and any house rules that will apply.

Object of the Game

A team earns points by correctly guessing prompts drawn by its own players. The first team to reach the agreed winning score wins, provided every team has received the same number of ordinary turns. Accuracy, quick visual thinking, and teamwork matter more than artistic skill.

Standard Match

  • Divide the players into two or more teams.
  • Use a 60-second limit for each ordinary turn.
  • Award one point for each prompt correctly identified within the limit.
  • Play to 10 points unless the group chooses another target before starting.
  • Rotate drawers so every team member draws before anyone takes a second drawing turn.

Section 2: Equipment

The following equipment is required for this version of the game. Teams should inspect all materials before play so that prompts remain secret, drawings remain visible, and timing is consistent.

Required Equipment

  • Cards: A supply of cards bearing age-appropriate words or phrases. Each card should present one clearly selectable prompt, or use numbered categories that identify which prompt the drawer must use. Players may prepare homemade cards as long as nobody sees the prompts before the game.
  • Markers: At least one visible marker for drawing. Provide additional markers if teams will draw simultaneously. All markers used in a round should offer comparable visibility.
  • Timer: A timer capable of measuring the agreed turn length and giving a clear signal when time expires. The standard turn lasts 60 seconds.
  • Score sheets: Sheets large enough to serve as the drawing surface, with a separate portion or additional sheet used to record team points and completed turns. Start each turn with a clean drawing area.

Prompt Preparation

Mix the cards thoroughly and keep their prompt sides concealed. Prompts should be understandable to the participating age group and should avoid private information, slurs, or material likely to embarrass a player. If players created the cards, one neutral person should combine and randomize them.

Equipment Equality

Every team must receive equivalent access to drawing space, markers, and viewing distance. Replace a dry marker or an illegible score sheet immediately. If equipment failure materially interrupts a turn, stop the timer and restart that turn with the same prompt once the problem is corrected.

Section 3: Playing Area

Play in a well-lit area where each team can see its drawing surface without crowding the drawer. Position teams far enough apart that private guesses cannot easily be overheard, while keeping all players close enough to hear the timer and any ruling made by the scorekeeper.

Drawing Station

  • Place the drawing surface where it remains visible to every eligible guesser on the active team.
  • Keep the card supply face down or otherwise concealed from all guessers.
  • Set the timer where the timekeeper can operate it reliably.
  • Keep the score record visible so teams can confirm the score and number of turns played.
  • Remove earlier drawings before the next turn so they cannot supply accidental clues.

Team Separation

During an ordinary turn, only the active team may guess. Other teams should remain quiet and avoid signaling, distracting the drawer, or reading the prompt card. A team may use its own drawing station, or all teams may share one central station and take turns approaching it.

Accessibility

Adjust the height and placement of the drawing surface so every drawer can use it comfortably. Players may draw while seated. The group may enlarge prompt text, improve lighting, or allow an assistant to position materials, provided the assistant does not interpret the prompt or guide the drawing.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Pictionary works best with four or more players divided into teams of similar size. Two players may compete individually by alternating drawing and guessing, but team play creates the intended cooperative experience.

Player Roles

  • Drawer: Secretly reads the selected prompt and draws clues for the active team. The drawer must obey all communication restrictions.
  • Guessers: Call out possible answers during their team's active turn. Guessers may make any number of attempts before time expires.
  • Opposing players: Observe silently, help confirm fair play, and prepare for their next turn.

The drawing role rotates within each team in a fixed order. Late-arriving players join at the end of their team's rotation. A player who cannot draw during an assigned turn may exchange places with the next teammate, but should return to the rotation at the next reasonable opportunity.

Officials

No formal official is required. The group should designate a timekeeper and scorekeeper; one person may perform both duties. A non-playing host may also serve in these roles. The timekeeper announces the start and end of each turn, while the scorekeeper records points and tracks whether teams have received equal numbers of turns.

Disputes

Players should settle disagreements promptly and in good faith. The scorekeeper may ask the drawer to reveal the card after the turn and compare it with the accepted guess. If the group cannot agree whether a guess was close enough, a simple majority of non-drawing players decides. A tied vote results in no point unless the group agreed on another method before play.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Starting the Game

  1. Form teams, choose team names, and establish the drawing order within each team.
  2. Agree on the turn length, winning score, acceptable-answer standard, and any variations.
  3. Randomly select the first team. Turns then proceed in the agreed team order.
  4. The first drawer takes a concealed card and privately reads the prompt.
  5. When the drawer is ready, the timekeeper starts the timer and announces the beginning of play.

Drawing and Guessing

The drawer may create any nonverbal picture that does not reproduce letters, words, numbers, or conventional written abbreviations. The image may be literal or may break a phrase into several pictured ideas. The drawer may add to, erase, cross out, circle, or point at parts of the drawing using the marker.

The drawer may not speak, mouth words, hum, make sound effects, act out the answer, trace letters in the air, indicate the number of letters or syllables with fingers, or signal whether a guess is close. Facial reactions should be kept neutral. The drawing itself must carry the clue.

Guessers may call out continuously and do not need to wait for permission. Multiple guesses can overlap. A correct answer must identify the prompt before the timer's expiration signal begins. Ordinary differences in capitalization, singular or plural form, tense, or minor pronunciation are acceptable when they preserve the prompt's meaning.

Completing a Turn

The turn ends immediately when the active team gives an accepted answer or when time expires. The drawer then reveals the card, the scorekeeper records the result, and play passes to the next team. A used card is set aside until the supply is exhausted, after which the group may reshuffle it if continued play is necessary.

Common House Rules and Variations

  • All-play round: Every team receives the same prompt and draws simultaneously. The first team to call out the exact answer earns the point.
  • Steal chance: If the active team fails, each opposing team receives one guess in turn. The first correct steal earns one point.
  • Rapid draw: Use 30-second turns for a faster and more demanding game.
  • Relaxed play: Use 90-second turns, simpler prompts, or category hints for younger or less experienced players.
  • Three-clue round: The drawer may create no more than three separate pictured elements.
  • Relay drawing: Teammates switch drawers at fixed intervals during one prompt without speaking to one another.
  • Cooperative mode: All players share a score and try to solve a set number of prompts within a total time limit.

Section 6: Scoring

In the standard points game, the active team earns one point when it identifies the complete prompt within the turn limit. An unsuccessful turn earns no point. There are no partial points for identifying only part of a phrase unless the group adopted a partial-credit variation before play.

Accepted Answers

An answer is correct when it communicates the same word or phrase shown on the card. Harmless grammatical differences may be accepted, but a broader related concept is not enough. For example, if the prompt is “running shoe,” the answer “shoe” identifies only part of the prompt and does not score.

Winning the Game

The usual winning target is 10 points. Reaching the target does not always end play immediately: every team must receive the same number of ordinary turns. If one team alone has the highest score after those turns are equalized, that team wins.

Ties

If two or more teams are tied for the lead, play sudden-death rounds among the tied teams. Each tied team receives one turn per round. Continue until, after an equal number of turns, one team has earned more additional points than every other tied team.

Alternative Scoring

  • Timed match: Play for a fixed number of rounds; the highest score wins.
  • Speed bonus: Award two points if a prompt is solved within the first half of the turn and one point thereafter.
  • Difficulty levels: Award one point for easy prompts, two for medium prompts, and three for difficult prompts.
  • All-play scoring: Award one point only to the first team that provides the correct answer.

Alternative scoring must be announced before the game and applied equally to every team.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Penalties should preserve fair play without turning a casual game into an argument. When possible, stop an improper clue immediately and explain the ruling briefly.

Drawing Violations

  • Writing a word, letter, number, initial, abbreviation, or other deliberate piece of language.
  • Speaking, whispering, mouthing, humming, or making a sound associated with the prompt.
  • Acting out the answer or using gestures away from the drawing surface.
  • Showing the prompt card or its reflection to a guesser.
  • Indicating the number of letters, words, or syllables through fingers, blank spaces, or similar codes.
  • Giving deliberate confirmation such as nodding, shaking the head, or signaling that a guess is close.

A clear drawing violation ends the turn and the active team receives no point. Accidental marks that merely resemble letters or numbers may be erased without penalty if they convey no useful information. Repeated or intentional violations may cause the offending player to lose the next drawing turn.

Guesser and Opponent Violations

A guesser who secretly views the card makes the prompt ineligible for scoring; discard it and use a new card. An opposing player who calls out clues, distracts the drawer, interferes with the equipment, or reveals a prompt gives the active team a replacement turn. If the interference occurs after an otherwise correct guess, the point still counts.

Timing and Procedural Errors

If the timer starts before the drawer has read the prompt, reset and restart the turn. If the timer fails or the end signal cannot be determined, replay the turn with the same prompt unless that prompt was exposed; if exposed, draw a replacement. A guess begun before the signal counts only if the complete answer is spoken before the signal starts.

Prompt Problems

A drawer may reject a card before the timer starts if it is illegible, duplicated in the same game, outside the agreed content limits, or genuinely unknown to the drawer. Replace it without penalty. Once drawing begins, the prompt normally cannot be exchanged merely because it is difficult.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Pictionary is a low-contact indoor game, but a few precautions keep energetic play comfortable and safe. Arrange the playing area so drawers can move their arms without striking furniture, walls, lights, or nearby players.

Physical Safety

  • Keep floors, aisles, and the route to the drawing station clear of bags, cords, drinks, and loose materials.
  • Place the drawing surface on a stable support and do not allow players to climb on chairs or furniture to reach it.
  • Use markers only on the intended surface; cap them when not in use and follow any product safety instructions.
  • Allow seated drawing, extra space, rest breaks, or slower rotations for anyone who needs them.
  • Do not run to the drawing station or grab equipment from another player.

Inclusive and Appropriate Play

Select prompts suitable for the players' ages, cultures, language skills, and comfort levels. Remove prompts involving private information, traumatic subjects, discriminatory stereotypes, or content a participant has asked to avoid. Artistic ability should never be mocked; unusual drawings are part of the fun.

Hygiene and Shared Materials

Clean shared markers and frequently handled materials when appropriate, especially during large gatherings. Players should not place markers in their mouths. Anyone with a relevant allergy or sensitivity should be offered suitable materials and adequate ventilation.

Managing Excitement

Guessers should remain far enough from the drawing surface to avoid bumping the drawer. Keep shouting at a comfortable volume for the room, and use turn-taking accommodations if overlapping guesses create sensory or communication difficulties. Stop play immediately if someone is injured, distressed, or unable to participate safely.
