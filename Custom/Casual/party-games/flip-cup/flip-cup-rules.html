Section 1: Introduction

Flip Cup is a team relay game in which players empty a small, equally measured serving from a cup and then try to flip that cup from the edge of a table so that it lands upside down. Once a player succeeds, the next teammate begins. The first team to complete its entire line wins the round.

The game is commonly associated with alcoholic drinks, but alcohol is not required. Water, juice, soda, or another mutually agreed beverage may be used, and an alcohol-free game follows exactly the same competitive rules. Players must never be pressured to consume alcohol or any other beverage.

Flip Cup has no single governing body or universal official code. These rules describe a fair, widely recognizable recreational format. Before play, participants should settle the team size, beverage, serving amount, match length, starting signal, and any house variations.

Objective

Each team attempts to move through its relay order faster than the opposing team. A player completes a leg by finishing the assigned serving and making the cup come to rest inverted on the tabletop after a legal flip. Speed matters, but a team cannot advance until each leg has been legally completed.

Spirit of Play

Good play depends on honest starts, equal servings, clear turns, and respect for every participant's limits. Disputes should be resolved quickly and consistently so that the social, fast-paced character of the game is preserved.

Section 2: Equipment

Required Equipment

  • Cups: Each active player requires one lightweight, unbreakable cup capable of resting upright and inverted. Identical disposable or reusable plastic cups are preferred. A common recreational size is approximately 16 to 18 US fluid ounces (473 to 532 millilitres), although smaller cups may be used when every player receives the same type.
  • Table: One stable, level table is required. It must have an unobstructed edge from which the cups can be flipped and enough length for both teams to stand in opposing lines.
  • Beverage: Every cup must contain the same agreed serving at the start of a round. The beverage may be non-alcoholic. A modest fill of approximately 1 to 3 US fluid ounces (30 to 90 millilitres) is typical, but participants should choose an amount appropriate to the group and circumstances.

Equipment Preparation

Cups should be clean, undamaged, and dry on the outside before the round begins. Cracked glass, ceramic vessels, metal cups with sharp edges, and containers that could shatter or injure a player are unsuitable.

Each team's cups should be of equivalent size, shape, weight, and condition. Servings should be measured or visually checked for equality. The table must be wiped when spills make its surface or the surrounding floor slippery.

Optional Match Aids

A score sheet, scoreboard, or timer may help organize a longer match, but none is required to play. Towels and a water container may be kept nearby for cleanup and hydration, away from the flipping area.

Section 3: Playing Area

Table Layout

The two teams form parallel lines along opposite long sides of the same table. Players on each team stand shoulder to shoulder in their declared relay order. The first players should be positioned at the same end of the table, with each subsequent pair aligned as evenly as practical.

Each cup begins upright directly in front of its player and far enough from the edge to avoid accidental falls. A clear strip of tabletop must remain between the cup positions and the edge used for flipping.

Flipping Zone

When attempting a flip, the active player places the empty cup upright near the edge so that part of its base projects beyond the tabletop. The player strikes or lifts the exposed base with the fingers, causing the cup to rotate toward the table. The cup must finish supported by the tabletop and resting upside down.

A cup that lands on the floor, on another cup, in a player's hand, or upright does not complete the leg. A cup hanging over the edge is valid only if it has stopped moving, is inverted, and remains supported without being held.

Clearance and Spectators

The playing area should provide at least an arm's length of clear space behind each team where practical. Spectators, bags, cords, chairs, and breakable objects must be kept away from players and from places where missed cups are likely to fall.

Alternative Layouts

When a long table is unavailable, teams may use opposite ends or separate equal tables, provided the surfaces are comparable and every player has a safe flipping edge. The layout must be announced before the round and must not give either team a material advantage.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Teams

Standard recreational play uses two teams with the same number of players. Four to six players per team is common, but any equal team size of at least two works. Teams declare their relay order before each round and may change that order between rounds unless the match format says otherwise.

If team sizes are unequal, the preferred solution is to rebalance the teams. As a house rule, the smaller team may designate enough players to take an additional leg, but those players must use a fresh, equally filled cup for every leg and must return to the declared position before beginning again.

Player Responsibilities

  • Remain behind the table edge and out of the active player's arm space.
  • Begin only after the preceding teammate's cup has legally settled upside down.
  • Use only the assigned cup and serving.
  • Make all flip attempts personally without physical assistance.
  • Call attention to spills, damaged equipment, or unsafe conditions immediately.

Officials and Captains

No official is required for casual play. The teams may appoint one neutral starter to verify equal servings, give the starting signal, judge close finishes, and record results. If there is no starter, the two lead players should confirm readiness and use an agreed countdown.

For organized recreation, each team may name a captain. Captains confirm the lineup and settle routine disagreements. If observers cannot agree on a close or disrupted result, replaying the affected round is the fairest remedy.

Participation and Substitutions

A player may decline a beverage, request a non-alcoholic substitute, or leave the game at any time. Substitutions may be made between rounds. During a round, a substitute should enter only for injury, illness, or another genuine inability to continue; the affected leg should then restart with a fresh equal serving.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Starting a Round

  1. Teams take their declared positions, with one filled cup placed upright in front of every player.
  2. The starter checks that the teams have equal numbers of players and comparable servings.
  3. The first player from each team raises or touches the assigned cup to show readiness.
  4. On the agreed signal, both first players may begin. A common social start is for the opposing lead players to touch cups, touch the table, touch cups again, and then drink, but a spoken countdown is equally valid.

Completing a Relay Leg

  1. The active player consumes the assigned serving. Pouring it out, transferring it to another container, or having another person consume it is not permitted.
  2. The player places the empty cup upright at the table edge with a portion of the base extending beyond the edge.
  3. Using one hand, the player propels the cup by contacting its base or lower portion so that it rotates in the air.
  4. The player may make unlimited attempts until the cup comes to rest upside down on the table.
  5. After a failed attempt, the player retrieves and resets the cup. Teammates may offer verbal encouragement but may not position, catch, or flip it.
  6. As soon as the cup has visibly settled in a valid inverted position, the next teammate begins the same sequence.

Legal Completion

The cup must make a distinct airborne rotation and settle with its open rim against the tabletop. Brief contact with the table during the rotation is allowed. A player may stop a cup only after it has already settled legally; touching a moving cup before it settles cancels that attempt.

Round Completion

The round ends when the final player on one team legally lands the cup. The opposing team stops immediately. If both final cups appear to settle at the same time and no neutral observer can distinguish them, the round is replayed unless the group agreed beforehand to use a tiebreaker.

Common House Variations

  • Survivor: After each round, the winning team selects one opponent to leave the opposing lineup. The remaining players continue until one team has no players left.
  • Relay return: After the final player finishes, the relay reverses direction. Every player completes a second leg, and the original starter must finish last.
  • Two-cup relay: Each player must complete two separate cups in sequence before passing the turn.
  • Perfect flip: A successful cup must land cleanly on its rim without first striking another object.
  • Non-drinking challenge: Cups begin empty, and each active player performs only the flipping portion of the relay.

House variations must be announced before the match. They should apply equally to both teams and must never override the safety and consent provisions in this rulebook.

Section 6: Scoring

Winning a Round

The first team whose final player completes a legal flip wins the round and earns one point. There are no partial points for individual cups, faster intermediate legs, or a lead that existed before the final legal landing.

Winning a Match

Before play, teams choose one of the following common match formats:

  • Single round: One round determines the winner.
  • Best of three: The first team to win two rounds wins the match.
  • Best of five: The first team to win three rounds wins the match.
  • Race to a target: The first team to reach an agreed number of round wins takes the match.
  • Timed session: Teams play until an agreed time expires, and the team with more completed round wins is the winner.

Tied Matches

If a timed match ends level, the teams may declare a draw or play one full deciding round. A shorter tiebreaker may use one nominated player per team, each with an equal serving and one cup. The first legal completion wins.

Recordkeeping

A scorekeeper may record only completed rounds. A round replayed because of simultaneous finishes, external interference, or unsafe conditions does not count. Teams should alternate table sides between rounds when lighting, surface condition, or surrounding space creates a noticeable advantage.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Minor Turn Violations

The following actions normally require the offending player to correct the problem and continue the same leg:

  • Beginning a flip before completely consuming the agreed serving.
  • Touching or catching the cup while it is still moving after an attempted flip.
  • Claiming completion when the cup is upright, on its side, off the table, or supported by another object.
  • Receiving physical help to retrieve, position, or flip the cup.

For a minor violation, the cup is returned upright to the active player's flipping position. If any of the serving was deliberately poured out, the cup must be refilled to the original amount before that player restarts the leg.

Relay Violations

  • Early start: If a player begins drinking before the preceding cup has legally settled, that player's cup is refilled to the original level and the leg restarts after the preceding leg is complete.
  • False start by the lead player: Stop both teams, reset the cups and servings, and restart the round. Repeated deliberate false starts may result in loss of the round.
  • Wrong order: Stop the offending team and return play to the first player whose proper turn was skipped. Any later flips do not count.
  • Interference: A player who intentionally blocks, touches, distracts, or knocks away an opponent's cup causes their team to lose the round.

Equipment and Spill Problems

An accidental spill caused solely by the active player is treated as an incomplete serving: refill the cup and continue that leg. If an opponent, spectator, unstable table, or defective cup caused the spill, pause play, restore equal conditions, and resume from the affected leg. Replay the round when the original state cannot be restored fairly.

Unsporting Conduct

Bullying, dangerous behavior, intentional cheating, harassment, or pressuring anyone to drink warrants immediate removal from the game. A removed player's team may use an agreed substitute; otherwise the teams should rebalance before continuing.

Dispute Resolution

A neutral starter's immediate ruling controls when one was appointed. Without a neutral starter, team captains should seek agreement based on what was plainly observed. When evidence is inconclusive and the disputed action decided the round, replay the round rather than inventing an advantage.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Alcohol and Consent

Alcohol is optional and should never be treated as a condition of participation. Follow all applicable age restrictions, venue policies, and local laws. Anyone who does not wish to drink alcohol must be allowed to use water or another non-alcoholic beverage without penalty or attention.

Do not use large servings, high-proof alcohol, forced-drinking penalties, speed-drinking pressure, or rules that punish a player for stopping. Participants should pace themselves, eat appropriately, hydrate, and stop immediately if they feel unwell. No one who has consumed alcohol should drive, cycle in traffic, operate machinery, swim, or undertake another hazardous activity.

Choking and Medical Risk

Drinking quickly can cause choking, vomiting, or aspiration. Use small servings and allow each participant to drink at a comfortable pace. Players with swallowing difficulties, relevant medical conditions, medication interactions, pregnancy-related concerns, or other risks should use the non-drinking variation or sit out.

Stop play for confusion, inability to remain awake, repeated vomiting, seizure, slow or irregular breathing, pale or bluish skin, or loss of consciousness. Seek emergency medical help immediately. Keep an unconscious person on their side if it is safe to do so, monitor breathing, and do not leave them alone.

Physical and Venue Safety

  • Use only lightweight, unbreakable cups; never use glass.
  • Place the table on a level surface and confirm that it does not wobble or collapse under ordinary contact.
  • Wipe spills promptly and keep floors, walkways, and exits clear.
  • Keep electrical devices and cables away from liquids.
  • Do not climb, sit, dive across, or lean heavily on the table.
  • Provide enough space for each player to flip without striking a teammate, opponent, wall, light, or spectator.

Hygiene and Allergies

Assign a separate clean cup to every player and do not share drinking vessels. Wash reusable cups before and after play. Clearly identify beverages and ingredients, accommodate allergies and dietary restrictions, and prevent accidental substitution of alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks.

Stopping Play

Any participant may call for a pause to clean a spill, replace damaged equipment, check on another person, or address an unsafe condition. Safety pauses carry no competitive penalty. The game should resume only when the hazard has been removed and all affected players freely agree to continue.
