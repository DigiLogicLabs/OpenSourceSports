Section 1: Introduction

Jenga is a turn-based dexterity game in which players remove blocks from a standing tower and place them on top. Every successful turn makes the structure taller, less stable, and more entertainingly precarious. The object is to complete a legal move without causing the tower to fall.

This rulebook describes the familiar casual form of the game. Jenga has no governing body for recreational play, so players should settle any desired house rules before building the tower. Once play begins, the same standards should apply to everyone.

Key Terms

  • Block: One rectangular playing piece used to build the tower.
  • Layer: A horizontal row of three adjacent blocks.
  • Complete layer: A layer containing all three blocks.
  • Active player: The person currently removing and placing a block.
  • Tower fall: A collapse in which the tower, or blocks other than the block being actively moved, falls because of a player's action.

The standard game produces one winner: the last player to complete a turn successfully before another player causes the tower to fall. Groups may instead play several rounds and use the scoring options in Section 6.

Section 2: Equipment

Required Playing Pieces

A standard set contains 54 rectangular wooden blocks used as the game's playing pieces. The blocks are intended to be similar in size but may have slight natural variations in thickness, grain, friction, and weight. Those small differences are part of the challenge.

A common full-size block is approximately 7.5 centimeters long, 2.5 centimeters wide, and 1.5 centimeters high. Sets made for travel, children, or oversized outdoor play may use different dimensions. All players in a game must use the same complete set.

Inspection and Preparation

  • Count the blocks before play. A 54-block set forms 18 complete starting layers.
  • Remove any block that is cracked, splintered, wet, sticky, or dangerously damaged.
  • Do not wax, sand, mark, weight, or otherwise alter selected blocks to change their behavior during a game.
  • If a set has fewer or more than 54 usable blocks, players may still play by agreement, provided the starting tower consists entirely of complete three-block layers.

No timer, score sheet, stacking sleeve, or other accessory is required for ordinary play. A stacking sleeve may help construct the initial tower, but it must be removed before the first turn.

Section 3: Playing Area

Build the tower on a flat, rigid, stable surface with enough open space for every player to reach it safely. A sturdy table or a smooth floor works well. Avoid soft carpet, flexible trays, uneven ground, vibrating surfaces, and locations where wind can move the tower.

Starting Tower

  1. Place three blocks side by side to form the bottom layer. Their long sides should be parallel.
  2. Place the next three blocks on top at a right angle to the layer below.
  3. Continue alternating direction through all 18 layers.
  4. Align the blocks into a reasonably straight tower without deliberately wedging them together.
  5. Remove any stacking sleeve carefully and allow the tower to settle before choosing the first player.

Players should remain clear of the surface except when taking a turn. Leaning on, bumping, shaking, tilting, or intentionally vibrating the playing surface is not allowed. Drinks, loose objects, pets, fans, and trailing cords should be kept away from the tower.

All players should have a fair line of sight. If the tower is difficult to reach, players may change their own position between turns, but the tower or its supporting surface should not be rotated unless everyone agrees to a rotation procedure before the game.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Jenga may be played by two or more individuals. It also works as a team game when teammates alternate turns and may not physically assist one another. For very large groups, divide into smaller games so turns remain easy to follow.

Turn Order

Choose the first player by mutual agreement, a random method, or by assigning the player who built the tower to go first. Play then proceeds in a fixed clockwise or agreed order. A player who leaves temporarily may be skipped with the group's consent; changing the order to avoid a difficult tower position is not permitted.

Officials and Disputes

No official is required. The non-active players jointly observe each turn and should call violations promptly and consistently. If the group appoints a referee for a tournament or party event, that person should remain neutral, determine whether a move was legal, and rule on the cause of a collapse.

When observers disagree and no referee is present, settle the issue by unanimous agreement where possible. Otherwise, replay the round for an unclear external disturbance or apply the interpretation announced before play. Players should not touch the tower while discussing a ruling.

Conduct

  • Players may talk and react, but may not touch, shove, startle, or obstruct the active player.
  • Advice is allowed only if the group agrees before play; the active player must still perform the move alone.
  • Players should disclose accidental contact with the tower or playing surface.
  • Respect reasonable accessibility adjustments, such as additional reach time or seated positioning.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Basic Turn

  1. Select one block from any layer below the highest completed layer.
  2. Using only one hand at a time, remove the selected block from the tower.
  3. Place that same block on the top of the tower.
  4. Position it at a right angle to the blocks in the layer immediately below.
  5. Complete the top layer with three blocks before beginning another layer above it.
  6. Release the block and allow the tower to remain standing to complete the turn.

Selecting and Testing Blocks

The active player may gently touch or nudge blocks to find a loose one. Only one hand may contact the tower at any given moment. The player may switch hands, but must remove one hand before the other touches the tower.

If testing moves a block out of position, the player should restore that block before touching another candidate. A clearly displaced block may be realigned with the same one-hand restriction. Players may not hold the tower steady with one hand while moving a block with the other.

Legal Source Layers

A block may be removed from any layer below the highest complete layer. If the top layer is complete, no block may be taken from that layer; the layer immediately below it is also unavailable because it is then the highest complete layer. If the top layer is incomplete, blocks may not be taken from that incomplete layer or from the complete layer directly beneath it.

Placing the Block

The removed block must be placed flat on the top level, following the three-block pattern and alternating orientation. If the current top layer has one or two blocks, the new block must continue that layer. Players may gently align the top blocks, but may not use this adjustment to brace lower layers.

End of Turn

A common timing standard treats the turn as complete ten seconds after the player releases the placed block, or as soon as the next player touches the tower, whichever happens first. For untimed casual play, the group may simply require the tower to settle visibly before the next player begins.

If the tower falls during the active player's turn or during the agreed settling period because of that player's move, that player loses the round. If only the block currently being moved falls without disturbing the rest of the tower, the player may retrieve it and continue unless the group adopted a stricter house rule.

Common Variations

  • Timed turns: Give each player a fixed limit, commonly 30 or 60 seconds, beginning with the first touch.
  • Two-hand beginner play: Permit two hands for young children or accessibility needs, while still prohibiting support of the tower during extraction.
  • Team relay: Teams alternate players for every move and earn one team result per round.
  • Truth or challenge blocks: Use pre-marked blocks carrying harmless prompts; complete the prompt after a successful placement.
  • Reverse direction: Change turn direction after each successfully completed top layer.
  • No testing: Once a player deliberately moves a candidate block, that block must be removed.

Variations should be announced before construction or before the first move. A variation never overrides basic safety expectations.

Section 6: Scoring

Standard Result

A single round normally has no numerical score. The player who causes the tower to fall loses, and the player who completed the immediately preceding legal turn wins. If the first player topples the untouched starting tower, the last successful player does not exist; the group may record only the first player's loss or rebuild and replay.

Match Scoring Options

For a longer session, choose one system before the first round:

  • Round wins: Award one point to each round's winner. The first player to three or five points wins the match.
  • Penalty total: Give the player who topples the tower one penalty point. After a fixed number of rounds, the lowest total wins.
  • Survival scoring: Award one point for every legal turn completed. The highest total after the agreed number of rounds wins.
  • Team scoring: Award one point to the team whose member made the final successful move.

When using round wins, rotate the starting player so no participant repeatedly receives the same position. If scores are tied at the end of a scheduled match, play additional rounds until one tied player earns a clear advantage, or conduct a shared-tower playoff among the tied players.

Records and Tower Height

Groups may record the number of successful blocks placed before collapse. Count only blocks legally removed and added during the round, not the 54 blocks in the original tower. Tower height is a fun secondary achievement, but it does not replace the chosen win condition unless announced beforehand.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Casual games work best when minor mistakes are corrected immediately and deliberate unfair play carries a stronger consequence. Agree before play whether the group will use friendly corrections or strict tournament-style penalties.

Correctable Violations

  • Using both hands on the tower at the same time.
  • Removing a block from a prohibited upper layer.
  • Starting a new top layer before the layer below is complete.
  • Placing a block in the wrong orientation.
  • Leaving a tested block substantially out of position.
  • Beginning a turn before the previous player's settling period has ended.

If the tower remains standing, stop play and require the active player to correct the mistake using one hand. The turn is not complete until the block is legally placed. Under strict play, the first violation may receive a warning and a repeated violation may count as a loss of the round.

Round-Losing Violations

  • Causing the tower to collapse during one's turn or settling period.
  • Deliberately supporting the tower while extracting or placing a block.
  • Intentionally shaking, striking, or tilting the playing surface.
  • Knowingly altering or substituting a block during the round.
  • Interfering physically with another player in a way that affects the tower.

If a non-active player causes a collapse through interference, that person loses the round under individual play. In a team game, that person's team loses. If an uninvolved person, pet, wind gust, unstable furniture, or other outside event clearly causes the fall, declare the round void and rebuild the tower.

Ambiguous Falls

A few loose blocks dropping from the tower ordinarily count as a collapse if they were not the block actively being moved. If the tower remains upright after an unclear minor displacement, the group may allow careful restoration. In strict play, any fallen non-active block ends the round. Apply the chosen interpretation consistently.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Standard tabletop Jenga is low risk when played carefully, but falling blocks can strike faces, hands, feet, drinks, or fragile objects. Set up the game where a collapse has room to spread without causing injury or damage.

Safe Setup and Play

  • Use a stable surface at a comfortable height and test it for wobble before building.
  • Keep faces away from the tower, especially when inspecting lower layers.
  • Do not place fingers beneath a leaning tower or attempt to catch a collapse.
  • Let falling blocks land, then collect them after all movement stops.
  • Keep food, drinks, glassware, electronics, candles, and sharp objects outside the fall area.
  • Inspect wooden blocks for splinters and retire damaged pieces.
  • Supervise young children and keep small blocks away from anyone likely to place them in the mouth.

Oversized and Outdoor Sets

Giant sets require extra precautions because their blocks may be heavy and can fall from greater height. Build them no higher than the manufacturer's intended limit, keep spectators beyond the likely fall zone, and never construct the tower above a player's safe reach. Closed-toe footwear is advisable for oversized play, though it is not required for the standard tabletop game.

Stop outdoor play during strong wind, rain, lightning, or whenever the supporting ground becomes uneven or slippery. Do not throw blocks, climb on the tower, or use blocks as stools, steps, or striking objects.

Accessibility

Players may agree to seated play, extended turn time, assistance positioning a chair, or another reasonable modification. Any helper should assist with access rather than selecting or moving the block unless the group has explicitly adopted cooperative play.
