Section 1: Introduction

Euchre is a fast partnership card game in which four players compete as two teams. Partners sit opposite one another. Each hand has five tricks, and one suit becomes trump. The team that chooses trump, called the makers, tries to win at least three tricks; the opposing team, called the defenders, tries to stop them.

A standard game is played to 10 points. Because hands are short and trump is powerful, success depends on timing, cooperation, memory, and sensible risk-taking. Partners may communicate through their legal plays, but they may not reveal cards, use secret signals, or make comments intended to suggest what another player should play.

Players and Objective

  • Four players form two fixed partnerships.
  • Partners occupy opposite seats, so turns alternate between the two teams.
  • The makers score by taking three or more of the five tricks.
  • The defenders score by taking at least three tricks and thereby euchring the makers.
  • The first team to reach 10 or more points wins unless a different target was agreed before play.

Essential Terms

Trump
The suit selected for the hand. Trump cards outrank every card belonging to a non-trump suit.
Right bower
The jack of the trump suit and the highest card in the hand.
Left bower
The jack of the same color as trump. It becomes a trump card and is the second-highest card in the hand.
Kitty
The four undealt cards placed in the center after each player receives five cards.
Upcard
The top card of the kitty, turned face up during the first round of choosing trump.
Going alone
A maker's choice to play the hand without that player's partner in an attempt to earn a larger reward.
Euchre
The result when the makers fail to take at least three tricks.

These rules describe the widely played four-player form. Since casual Euchre traditions differ by region, players should settle optional rules—especially stick the dealer, farmer's hand, and scoring for a lone defender—before the first deal.

Section 2: The Deck

Required Equipment

Euchre requires one deck containing 24 cards. Make the Euchre deck from a standard French-suited pack by retaining the ace, king, queen, jack, ten, and nine of clubs, diamonds, hearts, and spades. Remove every other card before play. The cards should have indistinguishable backs and be complete enough that no card can be identified while face down.

Trump Ranking

In the trump suit, cards rank from highest to lowest as follows:

  1. Right bower: jack of the trump suit
  2. Left bower: jack of the other suit of the same color
  3. Ace of trump
  4. King of trump
  5. Queen of trump
  6. Ten of trump
  7. Nine of trump

The left bower changes suit for the entire hand. If hearts are trump, for example, the jack of diamonds is a heart, not a diamond. A player holding it must treat it as a heart when following suit. The diamond suit then contains only its ace, king, queen, ten, and nine.

Non-Trump Ranking

In every non-trump suit, cards ordinarily rank ace, king, queen, jack, ten, nine from highest to lowest. The exception is the suit that supplied the left bower: its jack has moved into trump and is no longer available in its printed suit.

A trump card beats every non-trump card. If no trump is played to a trick, the highest card of the suit led wins. Cards from other non-trump suits cannot win, regardless of rank. For example, an ace of clubs cannot beat a nine of hearts when hearts were led unless clubs is trump.

Card Care and Preparation

Count the deck before beginning and whenever an irregularity is suspected. There must be exactly six printed ranks in each of the four suits. Shuffle thoroughly between hands so that previous combinations do not remain together. Any replacement deck should be introduced between hands, not during an active hand unless the current cards are unusable.

Section 3: Setup & Deal

Seating and First Dealer

Choose partners and sit opposite them. Select the first dealer by any fair method, such as drawing cards from the shuffled deck; the player drawing the lowest card deals first, with aces treated as high. If players tie, only the tied players draw again. After every completed hand, the deal passes one seat to the left.

Shuffle and Cut

The dealer shuffles the full 24-card deck. The player to the dealer's right may cut by lifting a packet and placing it beside the remainder; the dealer completes the cut by placing the original lower packet on top. A player who declines the cut should do so clearly, after which the deal begins.

Dealing the Hand

Starting with the player on the dealer's left and proceeding clockwise, the dealer gives each player five cards face down. Cards are traditionally dealt in packets of two and three, with the packet sizes alternated or otherwise distributed so everyone receives five. The exact two-and-three pattern is not strategically important, provided no player receives extra information and every player receives exactly five cards.

The four remaining cards form the kitty. The dealer turns the top kitty card face up without exposing the other three. Players may pick up and arrange their own hands but must keep every card concealed from opponents and partner.

Correcting Deal Errors

  • If a card is exposed during the deal, a player receives the wrong number of cards, or the deal proceeds in the wrong direction, stop before trump is chosen and redeal with the same dealer.
  • If the deck is discovered to contain the wrong cards before play begins, correct the deck, reshuffle, and redeal.
  • If the wrong player deals and the mistake is noticed before trump is established, cancel the deal and give it to the proper dealer.
  • If the wrong dealer's hand has already entered play, complete the hand and resume the normal rotation from the seat that should deal next. Do not score an extra penalty unless the group adopted one in advance.

Readiness

Before the first player acts on the upcard, everyone should confirm the partnership arrangement, target score, dealer, direction of play, and any house rules. Once trump has been established, players should avoid redealing for a correctable misunderstanding that does not affect the cards.

Section 4: Gameplay

First Round of Choosing Trump

The player to the dealer's left acts first, followed clockwise by the other players, with the dealer acting last. On a turn, a player either passes or orders the upcard's suit as trump. A player other than the dealer says “order it up” or otherwise clearly names the choice. The dealer selects that suit by accepting or picking up the upcard.

When the upcard's suit is selected, the dealer takes the upcard into hand and discards any one card face down to the kitty, restoring a five-card hand. The dealer may discard the upcard itself after picking it up, although doing so is rarely useful. The player who caused trump to be selected and that player's partner are the makers.

Second Round of Choosing Trump

If all four players pass in the first round, the dealer turns the upcard face down. Beginning again with the player to the dealer's left, each player may name any of the other three suits as trump or pass. The rejected upcard suit cannot be chosen in this round.

If everyone passes again, the hand is thrown in and the next player deals under the basic rules. Many groups instead use “stick the dealer,” under which the dealer must choose one of the three available suits rather than pass. Whichever method is chosen should remain in effect for the whole game.

Going Alone

When a player makes trump, that player may announce an intention to go alone before the opening lead. The maker's partner places all five cards face down and takes no part in the hand. The remaining three active players continue in their usual seating order, skipping the inactive partner's seat.

A player may go alone whether that player ordered the dealer, named a suit in the second round, or is the dealer accepting the upcard. The announcement must be clear and timely; once the first card is led, a maker may not newly declare a lone hand.

Playing Tricks

The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick unless that player is the inactive partner of a lone maker, in which case the next active player to the left leads. A lead may be any card. Each remaining active player contributes one card clockwise.

  • A player who holds at least one card of the suit led must follow that suit.
  • A player unable to follow suit may play any card, including trump.
  • The left bower belongs to trump, not to the suit printed on the card.
  • The highest trump played wins the trick. If no trump is played, the highest card of the suit led wins.
  • The winner of a trick leads the next trick.

Keep completed tricks face down in front of one member of the team, with each trick separated enough to count. Players may review the current trick until it is gathered, but previously completed tricks should remain face down. After five tricks, count each team's tricks and score the hand.

Irregular Play

Playing out of turn should be corrected as soon as it is noticed. If no later player has exposed a card, return the premature card and restore the proper turn. If later cards have been exposed, casual groups should reconstruct the trick when everyone can agree; otherwise cancel and redeal the hand without score.

Failure to follow suit while holding that suit is a revoke. If discovered before the trick is gathered, take back the cards played after the error, correct the play, and continue. If discovered later, a practical casual penalty is two points for the non-offending team, or four points if the offending side was attempting a lone hand. Do not also score the ordinary result of that hand. Agree on the revoke penalty before play because local customs vary.

Players must not expose cards deliberately, signal strength, identify a partner's mistake before the hand ends, or suggest a play through words, gestures, or unusual emphasis. For an accidental exposure, leave the card visible until legally played or redeal if the exposure gives a major unfair advantage; use the remedy the group considers fairest and apply it consistently.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

Standard Hand Scores

Result Points
Makers win three or four tricks 1 point to the makers
Makers win all five tricks with both partners active 2 points to the makers
Defenders win three or more tricks, euchring the makers 2 points to the defenders
Lone maker wins three or four tricks 1 point to the maker's team
Lone maker wins all five tricks 4 points to the maker's team
Defenders euchre a lone maker 2 points to the defenders

A lone maker earns the four-point bonus only by winning all five tricks. Winning three or four tricks is worth one point, just as it is for a partnership that makes its bid without a sweep. A team cannot combine multiple scoring rows from the same hand.

Keeping Score

Any clear running record may be used. A traditional method uses spare cards that are not part of the active 24-card deck: one scoring card covers or uncovers suit symbols on another to display the total. Players may instead track the score verbally or with any mutually understood method. Announce and confirm the score after each hand.

Winning the Game

The first team to reach at least 10 points wins immediately after the hand is scored. The winners do not need a two-point margin, and a team may win with any scoring result, including a euchre of the opponents. For a longer match, play an agreed best-of-three or best-of-five series of games, beginning each new game with a fresh selection of the first dealer.

Ties and Disputed Scores

A game cannot end tied because only one team scores on a hand. If the recorded total is disputed, reconstruct it from the hands already scored and any visible scoring record. When reconstruction is impossible, use the last score on which all players agree and continue from there. Good practice is to have both teams acknowledge every scoring change before the next deal.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

Euchre has many regional traditions. A house rule is valid when everyone understands it before play begins, but it should not be introduced to settle a hand already in progress. The following options are common and may be adopted separately.

Stick the Dealer

If all players pass in the first round and the first three players pass in the second round, the dealer must name one of the three eligible suits as trump. This prevents thrown-in hands and keeps the game moving. The dealer still may not choose the rejected upcard suit.

Screw the Dealer

This name is often used interchangeably with stick the dealer. In some groups it also means the dealer must choose trump whenever everyone else passes in the second round, without receiving an additional chance to pass. Clarify the intended meaning before starting.

Farmer's Hand

A player dealt an unusually weak hand may request a redeal. A common definition is a hand containing only nines and tens, while another permits a hand containing three nines or three tens. Because definitions vary widely and repeated redeals affect strategy, specify the exact qualifying hand and whether it must be revealed. Unless adopted in advance, a weak hand receives no special treatment.

Ace-No-Face

Under this option, a player whose five-card hand contains one ace and no kings, queens, or jacks may reveal it and call for a redeal. Some groups omit the ace requirement and allow any hand with no face cards. Decide which version applies and whether the dealer remains the same.

Defending Alone

Some groups allow either defender to play alone against a lone maker. The defender announces this before the opening lead, and the defender's partner sits out. A common bonus awards the defenders 4 points if the lone defender takes at least three tricks, though other groups award 4 only for all five tricks. This option changes risk substantially and must include a clearly agreed scoring schedule.

Partner's Best

In this variation, a lone maker may exchange one unwanted card for a card chosen and passed face down by the inactive partner. The inactive partner does not disclose the rest of the hand. This makes lone attempts stronger and is not part of the basic game.

Going Under

A player with a qualifying weak hand may place three cards face down and exchange them for the three face-down cards in the kitty, leaving the upcard untouched. A common qualification is holding three nines or tens. The exchange usually occurs before bidding, and the player must reveal enough of the original hand to prove eligibility. Define the qualification and timing before play.

No Trump or High/Low Options

Some versions permit a player in the second round to call “no trump,” making the led suit control each trick with aces high. Another option allows “low,” in which lower ranks beat higher ranks. These calls require a complete ranking agreement, especially for jacks, and should not be added casually during a standard game.

Three-Player Cutthroat Euchre

Three players may play without fixed partners. Deal five cards to each player and place the remaining nine cards in the kitty, turning one up. The player who makes trump plays against the other two for that hand. A simple scoring system gives the maker 1 point for three or four tricks and 3 points for all five; each defender receives 2 points if the maker is euchred. Set a winning target such as 10 points.

Two-Player Euchre

For two players, deal five cards to each and use the normal two-round trump selection. The non-dealer leads. Each player competes individually, and standard maker scoring can be used: 1 point for three or four tricks, 2 points for a sweep, and 2 points to the defender for a euchre. With no partner to sit out, lone-hand bonuses do not apply.

Expanded Decks

Some groups add sevens and eights for a 32-card deck, especially with more than four players. Added ranks fall below the nine unless the group agrees otherwise. Increasing the deck changes the deal, kitty size, and card-counting strategy, so establish those details before shuffling.

Recommended Pre-Game Agreement

  • Target score and number of games in the match
  • Whether stick the dealer is active
  • Whether weak-hand redeals or going under are allowed
  • Whether defenders may go alone and how that scores
  • The penalty for a completed revoke
  • Whether table talk receives a warning, a point penalty, or a redeal

For a straightforward first game, use the 24-card deck, four fixed partners, thrown-in hands after two rounds of passing, standard scoring to 10, and no optional weak-hand or lone-defender rules.
