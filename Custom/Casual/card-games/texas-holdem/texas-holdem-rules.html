Section 1: Introduction

Texas hold'em is the most widely played form of poker. Each player receives two private cards and shares five community cards dealt face up in the middle; the best five-card hand wins the pot — or the last player left after everyone else folds does.

Why this is a community entry. Poker has major tournaments and long-settled cardroom conventions, but no single sanctioning body issues its rules the way FIDE does for chess. House rules genuinely differ between rooms, so this describes standard practice rather than a governed law.

Section 2: The Deck

  • One standard 52-card deck, no jokers.
  • Cards rank ace high down to two. The ace also plays low in a 5-4-3-2-A straight.
  • No suit outranks another — suits never break a tie in hold'em.
  • Chips for betting, and a dealer button marking position, which moves one seat clockwise each hand.

Section 3: Setup & Deal

  • The two players left of the button post forced bets: the small blind and the big blind.
  • Each player is dealt two private cards ("hole cards") face down.
  • The first betting round begins with the player to the left of the big blind.

Section 4: Gameplay

The Four Betting Rounds

  • Pre-flop: after the hole cards are dealt.
  • The flop: three community cards dealt face up, then betting.
  • The turn: a fourth community card, then betting.
  • The river: a fifth community card, then a final round.

Actions

  • Check: pass, only when no bet is outstanding.
  • Bet / raise: put chips in, or increase an existing bet.
  • Call: match the current bet.
  • Fold: surrender the hand and any chips already committed.

In no-limit hold'em a player may bet their entire stack at any point. A player who cannot cover a bet may go all-in and remains eligible for the portion of the pot they matched; further betting forms a side pot.

Making a Hand

Your hand is the best five cards from your two hole cards plus the five community cards. You may use both, one, or neither — playing the board is legal, and when the board is the best hand available the pot is split.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

There are no points. Hands rank, highest first:

  • Royal flush — A-K-Q-J-10, one suit
  • Straight flush — five in sequence, one suit
  • Four of a kind
  • Full house — three of a kind plus a pair
  • Flush — five of one suit
  • Straight — five in sequence, mixed suits
  • Three of a kind
  • Two pair
  • One pair
  • High card

At showdown the best hand takes the pot. Identical hands split it — suits never break the tie.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

  • Limit hold'em: bet sizes are fixed, with a cap on raises per round.
  • Pot-limit: the maximum bet is the current size of the pot.
  • Omaha: four hole cards, and you must use exactly two of them.
  • Short-deck (six-plus): twos through fives removed, which reorders some hand rankings — a flush beats a full house.
  • Tournament vs cash: tournaments use rising blinds and chips with no cash value; cash games use fixed blinds and chips you can walk away with.
