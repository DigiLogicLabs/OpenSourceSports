Section 1: Introduction

Blackjack is a comparing card game in which each player tries to finish with a hand closer to 21 than the dealer’s hand without exceeding 21. Players compete independently against the dealer, not against one another. A player can therefore win even when another player loses during the same round.

This rulebook presents a complete recreational version suitable for homes, clubs, and casual events. Because blackjack has many established local variations, the dealer must announce the table rules before play begins. Once a round has started, those rules remain in effect until the round is settled.

Basic objective

  • Build a hand whose value is 21 or as close to 21 as possible without going over.
  • Win when the dealer busts while the player remains at 21 or below.
  • Win by holding a higher valid total than the dealer after both hands are complete.
  • Avoid busting: a hand over 21 loses immediately, even if the dealer later busts.

A two-card hand consisting of an ace and any ten-value card is a blackjack, also called a natural. It is different from a hand totaling 21 with three or more cards and normally receives special priority and payout treatment.

Recommended casual format

For a simple game, use one standard deck, allow one to five players, have the dealer stand on all 17s, permit doubling on any initial two cards, permit one split per hand, and pay a blackjack at 3 to 2. Points, counters, or a written score may replace money. No purchase or wager is necessary to enjoy the game.

Section 2: Equipment

Required equipment

Blackjack requires a standard 52-card deck containing four suits, with thirteen cards in each suit. Jokers and advertising cards are removed. One deck is sufficient for recreational play; multiple combined decks are a common variation but are not required.

The deck must be complete, distinguishable, and capable of being randomized fairly. Cards with marks, bends, tears, transparent backs, or other identifying features should be replaced before play. If multiple decks are used, their backs should match so that individual cards cannot be identified while face down.

Card composition

  • Number cards 2 through 10 have their printed values.
  • Jacks, queens, and kings are each worth 10.
  • Aces may count as either 1 or 11, whichever gives the hand its best legal value.
  • Suits have no rank and do not affect scoring.

Optional accessories

Players may use chips, tokens, a score sheet, or another agreed scoring method, but none is necessary to play. A discard holder or dealing shoe may help organize multi-deck games. These accessories do not alter the rules or determine the winner.

Deck preparation

Before the first round, verify the deck’s contents and shuffle thoroughly. The dealer may offer another participant an opportunity to cut the deck. When too few cards remain to complete a likely round, reshuffle before dealing rather than during an active hand.

Section 3: Playing Area

Blackjack may be played on any clean, stable surface with enough room to keep every hand separate. Arrange the players along one side or around the surface and designate a clear dealer position. Each player receives a distinct playing spot directly in front of them.

Suggested layout

  • Keep undealt cards near the dealer and away from completed hands.
  • Place the dealer’s hand in a central area visible to every participant.
  • Keep each player’s cards within that player’s own space.
  • Create a separate discard area so used cards cannot be confused with the live deck.
  • If wagers or scoring counters are used, place them clearly with the corresponding hand before the deal.

Cards may be dealt face up for a simple recreational game. If a face-down format is chosen, everyone must understand who may touch the cards and how decisions will be signaled. Face-up dealing is recommended because it reduces handling errors and makes totals easy to verify.

Seat order

Play proceeds from the dealer’s left to right, or in another fixed direction announced before the first deal. A player’s position does not change the value of a hand. However, the order must remain consistent throughout each round because cards are drawn sequentially from the same shuffled deck.

The playing area should remain free of unrelated cards, drinks that might spill, and objects that obscure cards or wagers. Spectators must remain far enough away that they cannot see concealed information or interfere with decisions.

Section 4: Players/Officials

One participant serves as dealer, and one or more others serve as players. For comfortable single-deck play, one to five players is recommended. A participant may rotate into the dealer role after a fixed number of rounds, after each shuffle, or according to a schedule agreed before play.

Dealer responsibilities

  • Shuffle, deal, and protect the deck.
  • Announce the applicable house rules before play.
  • Expose and complete the dealer hand according to the fixed drawing rule.
  • Resolve each player’s result separately and accurately.
  • Correct dealing or scoring errors openly and consistently.

Player responsibilities

  • Act only when it is their turn.
  • State or signal decisions clearly.
  • Keep cards and any score markers associated with the correct hand.
  • Avoid touching the deck, dealer’s cards, discards, or another player’s hand.
  • Check the settlement of their hand before the next round begins.

Officials

No separate official is required for casual blackjack. The dealer administers the game and applies the announced rules. For an organized recreational event, a neutral host may settle disputes, verify the deck, and record scores. The host should not change a valid result merely because a different house rule is common elsewhere.

Advice and cooperation

Players may discuss strategy only if the group allows it. The acting player always makes the final decision for their own hand. No player may reveal a concealed card, manipulate the draw order, or coordinate decisions to gain access to unavailable information.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Starting a round

  1. Players enter the round and, if applicable, commit their wager or scoring stake before cards are dealt.
  2. The dealer gives two cards to each participating player and two cards to the dealer, dealing one card at a time in the established direction.
  3. In the common face-up format, both player cards are exposed. One dealer card is exposed as the upcard, and the other remains face down as the hole card.
  4. If the dealer’s upcard is an ace or ten-value card, the dealer may check the hole card immediately for blackjack. If the game uses no-hole-card dealing, the dealer receives or reveals the second card only after players act.

Player decisions

Beginning with the first player in order, each player completes their hand before the next player acts. The usual choices are:

  • Hit: Take one additional card. The player may continue hitting until standing, busting, or reaching a total that ends the hand.
  • Stand: Take no more cards and keep the current total.
  • Double down: Increase the initial stake by an amount up to the original stake, receive exactly one additional card, and then stand. In a non-wagering game, record the result as double points instead.
  • Split: When the first two cards have the same value, separate them into two hands, supply an equal stake for the new hand if wagers are used, and deal an additional card to each hand. Each hand then plays separately.
  • Surrender: If this optional rule was announced, surrender the initial two-card hand before taking another card and lose half the stake. Late surrender is normally available only after the dealer has checked for blackjack.

Aces and soft hands

A hand is soft when an ace currently counts as 11 without making the total exceed 21. It is hard when no ace counts as 11. Ace values adjust automatically: for example, ace-6 is soft 17, while ace-6-10 becomes hard 17 because the ace changes from 11 to 1.

Split-hand details

Unless a different rule is announced, each split hand may be hit, stood on, or doubled in the same way as an ordinary hand. Split aces commonly receive only one additional card per hand. An ace and a ten-value card formed after a split normally counts as 21 rather than as a blackjack. The group should announce whether resplitting and doubling after a split are allowed.

Dealer’s turn

After every active player has completed a hand, the dealer reveals the hole card. The dealer must hit totals of 16 or less and stand on totals of 17 or more. Under the recommended rule, the dealer also stands on soft 17. A variation may require the dealer to hit soft 17, but this must be announced in advance.

Completing the round

After the dealer stands or busts, compare the dealer’s hand separately with every player hand that has not already busted or surrendered. Settle blackjacks, wins, losses, and pushes, then collect all used cards into the discard area before beginning the next round.

Common house rules and variations

  • Dealer hits soft 17: The dealer draws on an ace-6 or equivalent soft total.
  • Six-to-five blackjack: A natural pays 6 to 5 rather than 3 to 2. This is less favorable to players and must be disclosed clearly.
  • Five-card Charlie: A player holding five cards without busting wins automatically, unless the group gives dealer blackjack priority.
  • Spanish-style deck: All tens are removed while face cards remain. This changes probabilities and should be treated as a separate announced variation.
  • Continuous dealer rotation: The dealer role passes after every round so all participants have equal turns administering the game.
  • Tournament points: Players begin with equal points and play a fixed number of rounds; the highest remaining score wins the session.

Section 6: Scoring

Hand values

Add the values of all cards in the hand. Number cards count at face value, face cards count as 10, and each ace counts as 11 unless that would make the hand exceed 21, in which case it counts as 1. With multiple aces, use the combination of 1s and 11s that produces the highest total not exceeding 21.

  • Ace-king is a blackjack: a two-card total of 21.
  • 7-7-7 totals 21 but is not a blackjack under the standard game.
  • Ace-ace-8 totals 20 because one ace counts as 11 and the other as 1.
  • King-6-8 totals 24 and is a bust.

Result priority

  1. A player who busts loses immediately.
  2. An unsplit two-card blackjack beats any dealer hand other than a dealer blackjack.
  3. If both the player and dealer have blackjack, the result is a push.
  4. If the dealer busts, every remaining player hand wins.
  5. If neither hand busts, the higher total wins.
  6. Equal totals are a push unless an announced variation says otherwise.

Standard recreational payouts

  • An ordinary winning hand pays 1 to 1.
  • A blackjack pays 3 to 2.
  • A push returns the player’s stake with no profit or loss.
  • A surrendered hand returns half the stake and loses the other half.
  • A doubled hand settles at the doubled amount.
  • Each split hand is settled independently.

For example, a 10-point stake returns 20 points in total after an ordinary win: the original 10 plus 10 profit. A blackjack on a 10-point stake returns 25 points in total: the original 10 plus 15 profit.

Insurance and even money

If the dealer shows an ace, a table may offer insurance before checking for blackjack. Insurance is a separate wager of no more than half the original stake. It pays 2 to 1 if the dealer has blackjack and loses otherwise. Even money is the equivalent settlement sometimes offered when a player has blackjack against a dealer ace: the player accepts a guaranteed 1-to-1 profit instead of waiting for the dealer check. Insurance and even money are optional rules and should be omitted from beginner games unless explained beforehand.

Non-gambling scoring

A family-friendly game may award 1 point for an ordinary win, 1.5 points for a blackjack, 0 points for a push, and deduct 1 point for a loss. Alternatively, begin each player with equal counters that have no cash value. Agree on the number of rounds and tie-breaking method before play.

Section 7: Violations/Penalties

Casual blackjack works best when mistakes are corrected consistently rather than punished harshly. The dealer should pause play as soon as an error is noticed, preserve the known card order when practical, and choose the remedy that restores the intended state without giving anyone a strategic advantage.

Common errors and remedies

  • Acting out of turn: Pause the game. If no card was exposed and the action can be withdrawn cleanly, restore the proper turn. If a card was exposed, keep it visible and apply an agreed neutral correction.
  • Taking a card after standing: If the card has not been exposed, return it to the top of the deck. If exposed, the group may require the player to accept it or void the hand; use the same remedy for every participant.
  • Incorrect number of initial cards: Correct the hand before decisions begin. If substantial play has occurred, finish unaffected hands and void only the compromised hand when a fair reconstruction is impossible.
  • Exposed dealer hole card: Continue with the card visible only if every player agrees no one has yet acted; otherwise redeal the round. Never secretly substitute another card.
  • Misread total: The actual card values control. Correct the total immediately. A hand that truly exceeded 21 is busted even if it was initially announced otherwise.
  • Illegal double or split: If discovered before another card is dealt, undo the action. If a card has already been exposed, apply the announced house remedy, normally treating the action as a hit and returning any added stake.
  • Premature dealer draw: Preserve the card order and complete all outstanding player decisions without revealing additional information when possible. If the exposed card materially influenced a decision, the affected hand may be voided by agreement.

Unfair conduct

Marking cards, stacking or controlling the deck, peeking at concealed cards, secretly changing a stake, deliberately misreporting a hand, or signaling hidden information is cheating. Stop the round, return unsettled stakes when the fair result cannot be established, remove the participant from the game if appropriate, and replace or reshuffle compromised cards.

Disputes

First consult the rules announced before the round. If the situation was not covered, the dealer or neutral host should select the fairest consistent remedy. A majority vote may be used in a friendly game, but participants directly benefiting from the disputed outcome should disclose that interest. Record any new house rule and apply it only to future rounds.

Dead hands and redeals

A hand should be declared dead only when it cannot be reconstructed fairly or when deliberate misconduct has compromised it. A full redeal is appropriate when the wrong deck was used, the deck was not randomized, multiple opening cards were exposed incorrectly, or the integrity of the entire round is uncertain.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Responsible play

Blackjack does not require gambling. For casual and youth play, use points or valueless counters. Where money is involved, every participant must satisfy local age and legal requirements. Set firm spending and time limits before starting, never borrow money to continue, and stop when play is no longer enjoyable.

  • Do not chase losses or pressure anyone to increase a stake.
  • Allow any participant to leave between rounds without criticism.
  • Keep essential expenses and shared funds completely separate from game money.
  • Do not play for money while impaired by alcohol, medication, fatigue, or distress.
  • Seek support from an appropriate responsible-gambling service if play becomes difficult to control.

Physical safety

Use a stable, well-lit playing surface and supportive seating. Keep walkways clear and place drinks away from the deck to prevent spills and slips. Take regular breaks during long sessions to rest the eyes, hands, neck, and back.

Cards can cause minor paper cuts. Deal smoothly across the surface rather than throwing cards toward faces or hands. Replace badly damaged cards and collect dropped cards without reaching under unstable furniture.

Health and accessibility

Participants should wash or sanitize hands as appropriate when sharing a deck. Do not share food residue, smoke, or other contaminants through frequently handled cards. Anyone who is unwell may use a separate dealing method or postpone play.

Use large-print cards, high-contrast markings, verbal announcements, additional decision time, or another reasonable accommodation when needed. Confirm that all players can distinguish suits and values, understand the chosen signals, and follow the action comfortably.

Respectful environment

Keep the game welcoming and free from harassment, intimidation, and deceptive pressure. Explain unfamiliar options before play, protect personal financial information, and do not photograph participants, hands, or scores without consent. A host should end the session if conflict, unsafe behavior, or suspected exploitation cannot be resolved calmly.
