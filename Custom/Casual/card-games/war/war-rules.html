Section 1: Introduction

War is a two-player game of pure chance. The deck is split evenly, both players flip their top card, and the higher rank takes both. Equal ranks trigger a war: cards are laid face down and the next flip decides the whole pile.

It is worth being honest about what this game is: there are no decisions. Once the deck is shuffled and dealt, the entire outcome is determined. That makes it a good first card game for a small child and a poor one for anybody else.

Section 2: The Deck

  • One standard 52-card deck, no jokers.
  • Cards rank ace high down to two.
  • Suits are irrelevant — they never break a tie, which is why ties become wars.

Section 3: Setup & Deal

  • Shuffle and deal the entire deck — 26 cards to each player, face down.
  • Players do not look at their cards. Each keeps a face-down stack.

Section 4: Gameplay

The Basic Turn

  • Both players simultaneously flip their top card face up.
  • The higher rank wins both cards, which go to the bottom of the winner's stack.

War

If the flipped cards are equal, a war begins:

  1. Each player lays three cards face down.
  2. Each then flips a fourth card face up.
  3. The higher fourth card takes every card on the table — ten cards in a single war.
  4. If those are equal too, the war repeats with another three-down, one-up.

Running Low

A player with fewer than four cards during a war plays what they have, using their last card as the face-up one. A player who cannot produce a card at all has lost.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

There is no score. A player wins by capturing all 52 cards.

In practice games can run very long or fail to terminate, because the order cards return to the bottom of the stack can cycle. Most households cap it:

  • Play for a fixed time and whoever holds more cards wins, or
  • Play to a set number of wars, or
  • Shuffle captured cards before returning them, which makes cycling far less likely.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

  • One card down: wars use a single face-down card instead of three — noticeably shorter.
  • Three or four players: deal the deck evenly; a war involves only the tied players.
  • Casino War: the house version, played against a dealer with a bet and a surrender option on ties.
  • Suit tiebreak: assigning suit order removes wars entirely — faster, but it removes the only dramatic moment the game has.
