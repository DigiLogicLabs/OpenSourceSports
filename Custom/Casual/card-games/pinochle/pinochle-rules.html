Section 1: Introduction

Pinochle is a trick-taking and melding card game in which players earn points in two distinct ways: by displaying valuable card combinations before play and by capturing counting cards during play. This rulebook presents a complete casual format for four players in fixed partnerships, commonly called partnership pinochle.

The four players form two teams of two. Partners sit opposite each other. Each hand consists of a deal, an auction, declaration of trump, melding, and twelve tricks. The auction winner commits the partnership to earning at least the final bid through meld and trick points.

Objective

A partnership wins by reaching or exceeding 150 points after a completed hand, provided it successfully made the contract on that hand. If both teams reach the target during the same hand, the successful declaring partnership wins. Groups should agree before play whether a different target will be used.

Players and communication

  • The standard game uses exactly four players divided into two partnerships.
  • Partners may communicate through legal bids and card play, but they may not reveal cards, name hidden holdings, use private signals, or advise each other what to play.
  • All cards should remain visible when meld is displayed, and all players may verify the meld before the cards return to their owners' hands.
  • A player must follow the agreed procedures consistently. If a local custom differs from this rulebook, announce it before the first deal.

Key terms

Bid
A player's proposed contract value.
Declarer
The highest bidder, who chooses the trump suit.
Meld
A scoring combination displayed from cards in a player's hand.
Trump
The suit that outranks every non-trump suit during trick play.
Trick
One card played by each player, won by the highest card under the rules of play.
Set
Failure by the declaring partnership to earn at least its bid.

Section 2: The Deck

Required equipment

Play requires one pinochle deck. A standard partnership pinochle deck contains 48 cards: two identical copies of each ace, ten, king, queen, jack, and nine in each of the four suits. A commercial pinochle deck may be used, or the required cards may be assembled from two ordinary decks.

Suits and ranks

The suits are clubs, diamonds, hearts, and spades. Within every suit, cards rank from highest to lowest as follows:

Ace, ten, king, queen, jack, nine.

The ten therefore ranks between the ace and king; it is not a low card. Because the deck contains duplicate cards, two physically different cards can have equal rank and suit.

Duplicate-card priority

If two identical cards are played to the same trick, the copy played first ranks higher for that trick. For example, if one ace of hearts is already winning, a later ace of hearts does not overtake it. A later player who holds only an identical card is nevertheless considered able to follow suit, but not able to beat the card already winning.

Preparing the deck

  • Confirm that all 48 cards are present before starting.
  • Remove all twos through eights and any jokers when constructing the deck from ordinary playing cards.
  • Shuffle thoroughly before each hand.
  • Keep cards face down during the deal and conceal each hand from all other players.

Card uses

The same card may participate in certain melds and later be played to a trick. Displaying a card for meld does not remove it from the hand. Meld scores and trick scores are separate, and neither changes the rank of a card.

Section 3: Setup & Deal

Seating and first dealer

Choose partners by agreement or by drawing cards. Partners sit opposite one another. Select the first dealer at random; thereafter, the deal passes clockwise after every hand, including a hand thrown in because everyone passed.

Shuffle and cut

The dealer shuffles the complete deck. The player to the dealer's right may cut, after which the dealer distributes the cards clockwise. No player may inspect the cut or undealt cards.

The deal

Deal every card so that each player receives twelve. A convenient procedure is to deal three cards at a time in four rounds, although packets of another agreed size are acceptable if used consistently. There is no widow, kitty, stock, or undealt card in this four-player format.

If a card is exposed during the deal, a player receives the wrong number of cards, or the deck is discovered to be incomplete before bidding ends, gather the cards and redeal with the same dealer. If the problem is discovered later, stop the hand and use the correction policy agreed by the table; for casual play, a redeal is usually fairest unless the result can be repaired without revealing hidden information.

Auction

The player to the dealer's left bids first, and bidding continues clockwise. The minimum opening bid is 20. On a turn, a player must either bid a whole number higher than the current bid or pass. A bid normally rises by at least one point.

  • A player who passes may not reenter the auction during that hand.
  • Bidding continues until three players have passed.
  • The last active bidder becomes declarer and the final bid becomes the contract.
  • If the first three players pass, the dealer must bid 20 under this rule set. This avoids a thrown-in hand and is often called “stick the dealer.”

Declaring trump

After winning the auction, the declarer names clubs, diamonds, hearts, or spades as trump. The choice is final once announced. Players then arrange and display their meld.

Displaying meld

All players lay their scoring combinations face up, grouped clearly enough to verify. Teammates add their meld values together. After every player has had a reasonable opportunity to check the display, each player returns the cards to hand. The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick.

Section 4: Gameplay

Playing a trick

The opening leader plays any card. The other players play one card each in clockwise order. The suit of the first card is the suit led. Once all four cards have been played, determine the winner, gather the trick for that partnership, and have the winner lead the next trick.

Obligations when playing

  1. Follow suit: If able, a player must play a card of the suit led.
  2. Beat the winning card: When following suit, a player must play a card high enough to beat the highest card of that suit already played if able to do so.
  3. Trump when void: If unable to follow the suit led but holding trump, the player must play a trump.
  4. Overtrump when able: If the trick has already been trumped, a player required to play trump must beat the highest trump already played if able.
  5. Discard when free: A player with neither the suit led nor any trump may play any card.

These duties apply even when a player's partner is currently winning the trick. A player may not intentionally underplay when holding a card that is legally required to beat the current winner.

Determining the winner

  • If one or more trumps were played, the highest trump wins.
  • If no trump was played, the highest card of the suit led wins.
  • Cards of other non-trump suits cannot win.
  • Between identical cards, the copy played earlier wins.

Legal play and corrections

A card is played when released face up into the trick. If a player notices an illegal play before the trick is gathered, restore the cards and require a legal card; information revealed by the exposed card remains known. If a revoke is discovered after later play has begun, casual groups should correct the hand when practical or apply a previously agreed penalty. A simple house remedy is to award the non-offending team the hand's last-trick point and enough additional trick points to cancel any advantage gained.

Completing the hand

Play continues for twelve tricks, leaving every player with no cards. Partnerships keep their captured tricks separate until the trick-point count has been checked. Meld cards do not belong to a partnership's captured pile unless that partnership actually wins them in tricks.

Good table practice

  • Play one card at a time in proper clockwise order.
  • Leave each completed trick face up until its winner is clear.
  • Do not review earlier tricks after they have been gathered unless correcting an error.
  • Announce totals clearly and allow both teams to verify the score before the next deal.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

Meld values

Meld belongs to the partnership whose players hold the cards. Each player scores only combinations actually present in that player's own hand; partners may not combine cards to create a meld.

Meld Required cards Points
Run A, 10, K, Q, J in trump 15
Trump marriage K and Q of trump 4
Common marriage K and Q of the same non-trump suit 2
Dix 9 of trump 1
Aces around One ace in each suit 10
Kings around One king in each suit 8
Queens around One queen in each suit 6
Jacks around One jack in each suit 4
Pinochle Q of spades and J of diamonds 4

Multiple melds and card reuse

A card may count once in each different meld category. For example, the king and queen of trump in a run also count as a trump marriage, and the queen of spades in that run may also belong to queens around or pinochle. A single card may not stand for two copies within the same combination.

Separate physical copies can create repeated melds. Two matching marriages score twice their ordinary value. Two complete pinochles score 30 points rather than 8. Two complete around combinations score 100 for aces, 80 for kings, 60 for queens, or 40 for jacks. A double run containing two of every required trump rank scores 150 points and includes the two trump marriages; groups should decide before play whether those marriages are scored separately. Under this rulebook, they are included in the 150 and are not added again.

Trick points

After the final trick, each partnership counts the valuable cards it captured:

Captured item Value
Each ace 1 point
Each ten 1 point
Each king 1 point
Each queen, jack, or nine 0 points
Winning the final trick 1 point

There are 24 card points in the deck and one point for the last trick, so the two partnerships' combined trick score must total 25.

Making or failing the contract

Add each partnership's meld and trick points. If the declaring partnership's total equals or exceeds the contract, it scores its full meld and trick total. The defenders also score their full meld and trick total.

If the declaring partnership earns less than its bid, it is set. Subtract the amount of the contract from its running score and do not add any meld or trick points from that hand. The defending partnership still scores its meld and trick points normally. Running scores may fall below zero.

Minimum trick requirement

To make a contract, the declaring partnership must capture at least one trick. A team that takes no tricks is set even if its meld alone equals or exceeds the bid. The defending team must also capture at least one trick to record its meld; otherwise its score for the hand is zero.

Winning the game

The first partnership to reach at least 150 points at the end of a hand wins, but a partnership may claim victory only after making a contract. If the defenders pass 150 while the declarers also reach 150 and make their bid, the declaring partnership wins. If the declarers are set, a defending partnership that reaches 150 wins.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

Pinochle has many regional traditions. Agree on all variations before dealing, especially the auction floor, winning score, meld requirements, and obligation to beat a card. The following are common alternatives rather than additions that should be introduced during a hand.

Alternative auctions

  • Throw in an all-pass hand: If all four players pass, redeal with the next dealer instead of forcing the dealer to bid 20.
  • Higher opening bid: Begin at 25, 30, or another agreed value to create a more aggressive auction.
  • Bid by fives: Require every bid to be a multiple of five.
  • Dealer privilege: Permit the dealer to take an existing minimum bid without raising it when all other players have passed.
  • Pass with help: Allow a passed player to give a narrowly defined signal, such as announcing a fixed meld amount. This substantially changes bidding and must be defined precisely before play.

Alternative meld rules

  • Minimum meld: Require a partnership to have at least 20 meld points before any meld can be recorded.
  • Run required to name trump: Require the declarer to hold a run, a trump marriage, or another agreed minimum trump meld.
  • Double-run treatment: Score a double run as 150 plus two trump marriages instead of treating the marriages as included.
  • Multiple meld schedule: Score repeated combinations by simple multiplication instead of using the premium double-meld values.
  • Roundhouse: Award an agreed bonus for holding a king and queen in every suit. Values vary widely, so record the chosen value before play.

Simplified trick play

For an easier introductory game, require players to follow suit but remove the obligation to beat the current winning card. If void in the led suit, players may either be required to trump or allowed to discard freely. Because this changes strategy considerably, use the same rule for the entire game.

Alternate winning totals

A short game may end at 100 points, while a longer match may use 250 or 500. For a match consisting of several games, award one game point to each winning partnership and play to a predetermined number of game points.

Three-player cutthroat pinochle

Three-player pinochle is played without partners and normally uses a different deal, bidding structure, and scoring target. A common casual setup deals fifteen cards to each player and places three cards face down as a widow. The highest bidder takes the widow, discards three cards, names trump, and plays against the other two players. Because scoring customs differ, decide whether defenders score independently and whether the declarer must meet the contract using only personal meld and captured tricks.

Two-player pinochle

Two-player forms commonly use a stock from which players draw after tricks during an opening phase, followed by strict follow-suit play after the stock is exhausted. This is a distinct format and should not be mixed casually with the partnership procedures in this rulebook.

Double-deck pinochle

A popular larger-group variation uses two 48-card pinochle decks combined, usually with higher bidding and scoring values. It is often played by four players with larger hands or by additional players in partnerships. Duplicate-card ties, special meld values, and the number of cards dealt must be settled before starting.

Friendly-play options

  • Allow new players to keep a printed rank and meld reference beside them.
  • Use open scoring so both teams can confirm every calculation.
  • Permit one corrected bid per player if the correction is made immediately and no later player has acted.
  • Give a warning for the first accidental revoke and apply an agreed penalty to later violations.
  • Set a reasonable turn pace, but allow extra time for players learning meld and mandatory-play rules.
