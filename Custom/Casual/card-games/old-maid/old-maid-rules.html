Section 1: Introduction

Old Maid is a simple matching game in which players discard pairs and draw hidden cards from one another. Every card can eventually be paired except one unmatched card, traditionally called the Old Maid. Players try to empty their hands without being left holding that card.

The game is best suited to three or more players, although two people can play. It requires memory, observation, luck, and a little bluffing, but players may never secretly inspect another person’s hand. A typical round lasts about five to fifteen minutes.

Objective

Make and discard all possible pairs, then avoid holding the unmatched Old Maid when every other card has been paired. Players who empty their hands are safe for the remainder of the round. The last player holding the Old Maid loses the round.

General Conduct

  • Keep card faces hidden from opponents unless a pair is being revealed and discarded.
  • Offer cards so the drawing player cannot identify them by their faces or marked backs.
  • Do not bend, mark, expose, or deliberately arrange cards to reveal the Old Maid’s location.
  • Resolve accidental exposure fairly by reshuffling the affected hand before play continues.

Section 2: The Deck

Required Equipment

Old Maid requires one deck. The deck may be a purpose-made Old Maid deck containing matching picture pairs and one uniquely unmatched Old Maid, or it may be prepared from an ordinary fifty-two-card deck.

Preparing a Standard Deck

When using a standard deck, remove exactly one queen before dealing. The remaining fifty-one cards contain twenty-five rank pairs and one unmatched queen. That lone queen is the Old Maid. Which queen is removed does not matter, but the choice should be made before the deck is shuffled.

Pairs are formed by cards of the same rank and the same color: hearts pair with diamonds, and clubs pair with spades. For example, the seven of hearts pairs with the seven of diamonds, while the seven of clubs pairs with the seven of spades. Under this method, a red seven and a black seven do not form a pair.

Purpose-Made Decks

In a commercial or homemade Old Maid deck, each ordinary image must have exactly one matching partner. One additional card must have no partner and serves as the Old Maid. The pictures, names, or symbols should make every valid pair clear to all players before the round begins.

Count or inspect an unfamiliar deck before play. Missing cards can create extra unmatched cards and prevent the round from ending correctly. Jokers, advertising cards, instruction cards, and other unrelated cards are removed unless a chosen variation specifically uses one as the unmatched card.

Section 3: Setup & Deal

Choose the Dealer

Select the first dealer by agreement or at random. For a series of rounds, the deal may pass clockwise after each round so every player has an equal opportunity to deal.

Shuffle and Deal

  1. The dealer thoroughly shuffles the complete prepared deck.
  2. Starting with the player to the dealer’s left, the dealer distributes one card at a time, face down, moving clockwise.
  3. Continue until every card has been dealt. Some players may receive one more card than others; this is normal and requires no adjustment.
  4. Each player picks up and privately examines their hand.

Discard Initial Pairs

Before drawing begins, every player removes all valid pairs already present in their hand. Each pair is placed face up where everyone can verify that the two cards match. Discarded pairs take no further part in the round.

If a hand contains all four suits of one rank in a standard deck, the player discards two pairs: the two red cards as one pair and the two black cards as the other. Players should finish removing every initial pair before the first turn begins.

Starting Player and Direction

Normally, the player to the dealer’s left takes the first turn, and play proceeds clockwise. The group may choose another starting method before dealing, but the drawing direction must remain consistent throughout the round unless a variation says otherwise.

Section 4: Gameplay

Taking a Turn

  1. The active player draws one card at random from the hand of the next eligible player in the chosen direction.
  2. The player offering cards holds them face down and spreads or fans them enough to permit a free choice.
  3. The active player adds the selected card to their own hand without showing it to anyone else.
  4. If the drawn card completes a valid pair, the active player immediately reveals and discards that pair face up.
  5. If no pair is completed, the card remains concealed in the active player’s hand.
  6. The turn then passes to the next eligible player.

Players Who Empty Their Hands

A player with no cards leaves active play and cannot draw or be drawn from. When such a player would normally be selected, skip that player and continue in the established direction to the next person who still holds cards.

If the active player has cards but the next seated player does not, the active player draws from the next eligible hand beyond that player. If only one player has cards remaining, no further draw is possible and the round ends.

Handling the Cards Fairly

The offering player may rearrange their hand before presenting it, but all card faces must remain hidden. They may use expressions or playful comments to bluff, provided they do not reveal card faces, mark cards, refuse a legal selection, or physically guide the drawing player’s hand.

A drawn card cannot be returned or exchanged merely because the player dislikes it. If more than one card is accidentally pulled, return all of those cards unseen when practical, reshuffle the offered hand, and draw again.

Ending the Round

Turns continue until every matching pair has been discarded. At that point, one player will hold the sole unmatched card. That player is the Old Maid for the round and loses; all other players have successfully escaped.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

Single-Round Play

The simplest game consists of one round. Every player who empties their hand avoids defeat, while the player left holding the Old Maid loses. Because Old Maid is traditionally an avoidance game, it does not require a separate point winner.

Multiple-Round Play

For a longer session, agree on the number of rounds before beginning. A common method gives the Old Maid holder one penalty point after each round. All other players receive zero penalty points. After the agreed number of rounds, the player with the fewest penalty points wins.

If two or more players share the lowest total, they may share the victory or play an additional round as a tiebreaker. When using a tiebreaker, continue until only one tied player finishes a round without receiving the decisive penalty.

Elimination Option

For elimination play, a player who accumulates an agreed number of penalties—commonly three—is removed from the series. Continue playing rounds among the remaining participants until only one player remains. This option works best with a larger group because eliminated players may otherwise wait a long time.

Recording Results

If the group records scores, note one penalty beside the Old Maid holder’s name after each completed round. Disputes should be resolved before the next deal. A round affected by an irreparable deck error, such as discovering a second unmatched card, should be canceled and replayed with the corrected deck.

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

Old Maid has many informal versions. Players should announce all variations before shuffling so that everyone uses the same pairing method, drawing direction, and scoring system.

Common Variations

  • Single Joker: Use a standard fifty-two-card deck plus one joker. Keep all four queens, pair cards by rank and color, and treat the lone joker as the Old Maid.
  • Black Peter: Remove one card from a chosen matching pair and use its remaining partner as the unwanted card. The group may rename that card to suit the theme of the game.
  • Rank Pairs: With a standard deck, allow any two cards of the same rank to form a pair regardless of suit or color. When three equal-ranked cards are held, discard only two and keep the third. Remove one card before play so exactly one card remains unmatched.
  • Smaller Deck: For younger players or a faster round, use selected matching pairs plus one unmatched card. Shuffle only after confirming that every ordinary card has exactly one partner.
  • Reverse Direction: Conduct all drawing counterclockwise instead of clockwise. The direction remains fixed for the whole round.
  • Winner by Exit Order: The first player to empty their hand wins the round, and later players are ranked in the order they go out. The final Old Maid holder still finishes last.

Optional House Rules

  • Silent Play: Players may not comment on the location or identity of any card while a choice is being made.
  • One Shuffle of the Hand: Before offering cards, a player mixes their hand once and then keeps the fan still until a card is selected.
  • Penalty Score: The Old Maid holder receives two points, while the first player out subtracts one point. The lowest total after the agreed number of rounds wins.
  • Rotating Unmatched Card: Before each round, secretly remove a different card from a complete set of pairs. Players know the unmatched card may change but do not learn its identity until play reveals it.
  • Team-Friendly Play: Adults may help younger players recognize valid pairs, but helpers must not identify which hidden card should be drawn.

Adapting the Game

For very young players, use a small picture deck with obvious pairs and allow hands to be placed face up with adult guidance. For experienced groups, increase suspense by using more pairs, concealing which card was removed, or tracking penalties over several rounds.

Whatever variation is chosen, preserve the core structure: ordinary cards form pairs, exactly one card has no partner, players draw without seeing card faces, and the person holding the unmatched card at the end receives the loss or agreed penalty.
