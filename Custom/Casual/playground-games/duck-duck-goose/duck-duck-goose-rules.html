Section 1: Introduction

Duck Duck Goose is a traditional playground game enjoyed by children and adults of all ages. It combines elements of tag, circle games, and chase mechanics into a fast-paced social activity. The game is often played during recess at school, birthday parties, or family gatherings where a group of people needs an active game that requires minimal setup.

The core mechanic involves one person chasing a circle of players, tapping them on the head while reciting the phrase "Duck Duck Goose." The tapped players remain seated, but if they stand up to chase the chaser, they become "Gooses." The game continues until everyone in the circle has been tagged as a Goose, at which point roles are reversed or a new game begins.

This version of the rules is designed for casual, recreational play without official governing bodies or standardized equipment. Variations exist in different regions and cultures, but the fundamental structure remains consistent across most communities.

Section 2: Equipment

Duck Duck Goose is a game of people and movement, requiring no physical equipment to play. The only necessary components are the players themselves and a flat surface.

Mandatory Items

  • Players: A minimum of three participants is required to start the game. One player acts as the chaser, while at least two players sit in a circle.
  • Space: An open area is required for players to form a circle and for the chaser to move around them.

Optional Items

While not required for the standard game, some groups may choose to add optional elements to increase fun or complexity:

  • Musical Instrument: A bell, whistle, or music player can be used to signal when the chaser must stop and select a new victim.
  • Props: Players may wear hats or headbands to make the "head tapping" mechanic more obvious.

Note: No balls, bats, or other sports equipment are required for this game.

Section 3: Playing Area

The playing area for Duck Duck Goose is flexible and can be adapted to various environments, from a living room floor to an outdoor playground.

Indoor Requirements

  • Floor Space: Ensure there is enough room for players to sit in a circle with space between them (approximately one arm's length apart).
  • Clearance: The player standing outside the circle needs enough room to walk around the perimeter without hitting walls or furniture.

Outdoor Requirements

  • Surface: A flat, even surface is preferred to prevent tripping. Grass or pavement works well.
  • Safety Perimeter: Ensure the circle is not too close to obstacles like trees, benches, or other hazards where players might run into them while chasing.

Circle Formation:

  1. All seated players must sit in a continuous circle.
  2. Players should hold hands or link pinkies to maintain the chain connection.
  3. The circle should be large enough so that no player is more than one person away from the chaser.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Duck Duck Goose is a self-regulated game with no referees or officials. All participants are players, and they enforce the rules among themselves.

Minimum Players

You need a minimum of three players to begin:

  • The Chaser (Gooser): One player stands outside the circle. This is the person who taps heads and chases others.
  • The Ducks: At least two players sit in the circle. They are initially "safe."

Recommended Players

For a better experience, 5 to 10 players are recommended. This allows for more "Ducks" in the circle and keeps the game moving at a good pace.

Roles

  • Duck: A player currently sitting in the circle. They are "safe" until tagged.
  • Goose: A player who was previously a Duck but stood up to chase the chaser. They are now "out" of the circle and must wait for a new round.
  • New Chaser: When the game ends, one of the Gooses is selected to become the new Chaser for the next round.

Section 5: Rules of Play

The following rules describe the standard flow of a game of Duck Duck Goose.

Setup

  1. All players except one sit in a circle on the ground or chairs. They should hold hands to form a continuous chain.
  2. The remaining player stands outside the circle, facing them. This is the Chaser.

The Chase

The Chaser begins by walking around the outside of the circle. The seated players remain quiet and still.

Tapping Mechanics

  1. The Chaser taps a player on the head and says "Duck."
  2. The Chaser taps a second player on the head and says "Duck."
  3. The Chaser taps a third player on the head and says "Goose."

The Reaction

This is the most critical part of the game:

  • If you are a "Duck": You must stay seated and hold your hands. If you stand up, you are out.
  • If you are a "Goose": You must immediately stand up and chase the Chaser around the circle.

Catch and Switch

If a Goose catches the Chaser before they complete their lap, the roles switch. The Goose becomes the new Chaser, and the old Chaser sits down as a Duck.

Ending the Round

The game continues until every player in the circle has been tagged as a Goose. At this point, all players stand up and choose who will be the Chaser for the next round. The game then restarts with a new configuration.

Section 6: Scoring

In traditional casual play, Duck Duck Goose is not a scored game. The objective is simply to be the last person remaining in the circle or to successfully catch the chaser. However, for competitive play (e.g., at a party with prizes), players may use the following scoring methods:

Rounds Won

  • The Chaser: If the Goose catches the Chaser, the Goose gets a point (or wins the round).
  • The Ducks: If no one catches the Chaser before they complete a full circle, all remaining Ducks get a point (or win the round).

Time Limit

A timer can be set for a specific duration (e.g., 5 minutes). The player who is still in the circle as a Duck when time runs out wins. If they were tagged as a Goose, they lose.

Last Duck Standing

In a long version of the game, play continues until only one Duck remains. That player is declared the winner.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Since there are no referees, players must police themselves. Violations can lead to disqualification from the current round.

Duck Violations

  • Standing Up: If a player tagged as "Duck" stands up, they are immediately out of the game for that round. They must sit back down and stay seated until their turn comes again.
  • Breaking the Chain: Players must hold hands or link pinkies. If a player lets go, they are out.
  • Talking: Ducks must remain silent. If a Duck speaks, they are out.
  • Blocking: Ducks cannot stand up to block the Chaser. They must stay seated.

Chaser Violations

  • Tapping the Wrong Person: The Chaser must tap a player who is currently in the circle. Tapping someone already outside (a previous Goose) results in a penalty.
  • Tapping Twice: The Chaser must tap each player only once per round. Tapping the same person twice is a violation.
  • Entering the Circle: The Chaser must stay outside the circle. If they jump in or sit down, they are out.
  • Taking Too Long: If the Chaser takes too long to complete their lap (e.g., more than 10 seconds), they may be forced to switch roles.

Penalties

If a violation occurs, the offending player is out of the current round. In some variations, they must sit on a chair outside the circle for the next round before rejoining.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Duck Duck Goose is generally safe, but head tapping and chasing can pose risks if not played carefully.

Head Tapping

  • Gentle Touch: The Chaser should tap the head lightly. Hitting or slapping is dangerous and can cause injury.
  • Consent: Players should agree to the head tapping mechanic before starting. If a player is uncomfortable, they can opt out of that specific variation.
  • Head Protection: If playing in a rough environment, players might consider wearing soft hats or beanies to protect their heads.

Chasing

  • Clear Space: Ensure the area is clear of obstacles. Players running around a circle can trip over furniture or uneven ground.
  • No Rough Play: The Goose should not tackle or trip the Chaser. Use speed and agility, not physical contact.
  • Age Appropriateness: This game is best for children and adults. It may be too rough or confusing for very young toddlers.

Hygiene

If playing with a large group, ensure players are clean and not sick to prevent spreading germs through hand-holding or close proximity.
