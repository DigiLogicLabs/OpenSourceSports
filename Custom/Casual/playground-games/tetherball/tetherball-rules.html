Section 1: Introduction

Tetherball is a classic playground game where the objective is to wrap a tethered ball around a vertical pole. It combines elements of hand-eye coordination, strategy, and physical exertion. Players take turns hitting the ball against each other or into a designated wall to force it around the pole. The game ends when one side successfully wraps the rope around the pole a specific number of times, typically 21 turns for doubles or 6 turns for singles.

The game is simple enough to play anywhere with a sturdy pole, but it is most commonly found in playgrounds and parks. It can be played by children or adults, making it a versatile activity for all ages. The excitement comes from the tension of trying to outlast your opponent while managing the momentum of the ball and rope.

Section 2: Equipment

To play a standard game of tetherball, you will need three specific items. These are the only mandatory pieces required to start a match.

The Ball

You need a ball that is soft enough to hit safely but heavy enough to have momentum. A rubber or plastic tetherball is ideal, often featuring a textured surface for better grip and aerodynamics. The ball should be roughly the size of a tennis ball or slightly larger, depending on the age group playing. It must be securely attached to one end of the rope.

The Rope

A strong rope connects the ball to the top of the pole. The rope must be long enough so that when it is fully wrapped around the pole, there is still some slack or tension. Standard ropes are usually 20 to 30 feet long, though shorter lengths can be used for smaller poles or younger players. The rope should be durable to withstand the force of being hit repeatedly.

The Pole

A vertical pole is the central anchor of the game. It must be sturdy and securely anchored to the ground so it does not tip over when hit hard. The pole should be straight and vertical, with a smooth surface to allow the rope to wrap easily without snagging. The height of the pole is critical for gameplay balance.

Section 3: Playing Area

The playing area is defined by the pole and a designated court space around it. While tetherball can be played on any flat surface, having a defined area ensures fair play and safety.

Court Dimensions

The court is typically a square or rectangular area centered around the pole. For standard play, the distance from the center of the pole to the boundary lines should be at least 10 feet. This gives players enough room to move and hit the ball without being too close to the pole or the sidelines. The court can be marked with chalk, tape, or painted lines on concrete or grass.

Pole Height

The height of the pole determines the length of the rope and the difficulty of the game. For children, a pole height of 8 to 10 feet is recommended. For adult or competitive play, the standard pole height is often 12 to 15 feet. The rope should be attached near the top of the pole, leaving a small amount of slack at the bottom when the ball is first served.

Surface

The surface should be flat and non-slip to prevent players from slipping while moving. Hard surfaces like concrete or asphalt are common in parks, but grass or artificial turf is preferred for safety to reduce the impact of falls. If playing on a hard surface, players should wear appropriate footwear with good traction.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Tetherball can be played in two main formats: singles or doubles. The number of players determines the rules for serving and scoring.

Singles

In singles, there are two players on the court. Each player stands on opposite sides of the pole. The game is played one-on-one, with each player trying to wrap the rope around the pole before their opponent does.

Doubles

In doubles, there are four players on the court. Two players stand together on one side of the pole, and two players stand together on the opposite side. They work as a team to hit the ball and wrap the rope around the pole.

Officials

While casual play often goes without referees, it is highly recommended to have an official for organized matches. The referee's role is to ensure players stay within the court boundaries, call fouls, and keep track of the number of wraps around the pole. The referee should be positioned near the center of the court or behind one side to get a good view of both players.

Section 5: Rules of Play

The rules govern how the game is played from start to finish. These ensure fairness and prevent dangerous play.

Serving

The game begins with a serve. The player on the left side of the court serves first (in singles) or Team A serves first (in doubles). The server must hit the ball into their opponent's side of the court. A serve is valid only if it lands within the boundaries and does not hit the pole directly.

Hitting the Ball

Players must hit the ball with their hands or arms. Using feet, knees, elbows (below the elbow), or any other body part to hit the ball is a foul. Players cannot catch, hold, or throw the ball; they must keep it in play by hitting it. The ball must be hit with a swinging motion, not just pushed or thrown.

The Wrap

The goal is to wrap the rope around the pole. Each time the ball hits the pole and wraps the rope once, it counts as a point or turn. The rope must wrap completely around the pole to count. If the ball hits the pole but does not wrap (e.g., it bounces off), no point is awarded.

Movement

Players are allowed to move around the court freely, but they must stay within the boundary lines. Stepping on or over the line is considered out of bounds. Players cannot touch the pole, the rope, or the net (if used) with their hands or body. They can only touch these objects when hitting the ball.

The Wall

In some variations, a wall is placed behind the court. If the ball hits the wall and bounces back into play, it is still in play. However, if the ball hits the wall and stays out of bounds, the point is awarded to the opponent. The wall should be positioned so it does not interfere with the pole or the players' movement.

Section 6: Scoring

Scoring in tetherball is based on the number of times the rope wraps around the pole. The first team or player to reach a specific number of wins takes the match.

Winning a Point

A point is scored when the rope wraps around the pole a full 360 degrees. In singles, each wrap counts as one point for that player. In doubles, each wrap counts as one point for the team.

Winning a Match

The standard match length is 21 points for doubles and 6 points for singles. The first player or team to reach the required number of points wins the match. If a player or team is ahead by two points, they must win two consecutive points to secure the match (deuce rule), though this is often omitted in casual play.

Changing Ends

If the match goes beyond a certain number of points (e.g., 15 in doubles), players or teams should switch sides of the court to ensure fairness. This is done every 6 points in doubles or at regular intervals depending on the agreed-upon rules.

Match End

The match ends immediately when one player or team reaches the winning score. The game does not continue if a player is injured or leaves early, unless agreed upon beforehand. The winner is declared by the referee or the players themselves.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Fouls and violations can occur during play. These are handled according to standard penalty rules to maintain the integrity of the game.

Fouls

A foul is any action that violates the rules of play. Common fouls include stepping on or over the boundary lines, hitting the ball with a foot or knee, touching the pole or rope with hands, or catching the ball. A player who commits a foul immediately loses their turn to hit the ball.

Fouls in Doubles

In doubles, if one player on a team commits a foul, the entire team loses their turn. The opposing team then gets to hit the ball for the remainder of their point or until they also commit a foul. This ensures that both players on the team are responsible for their actions.

Penalties

If a player commits a serious foul (e.g., using hands to catch the ball or intentionally hitting with feet), they may be given a penalty. A common penalty is losing one point for the opponent or being disqualified from the match if they commit multiple serious fouls. The referee has the final say on penalties.

Time Violations

If a player takes too long to hit the ball (e.g., more than 5 seconds), they may be called out. This prevents players from stalling or delaying the game unnecessarily. The referee will warn the player first, and a second violation results in a point for the opponent.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Safety is paramount in tetherball due to the nature of hitting a ball against a pole and rope. Players should follow these guidelines to prevent injury.

Pole Padding

The pole should be padded or covered with soft material to prevent injury if the ball hits it hard or if a player accidentally touches it. The padding should be secure and not interfere with the rope wrapping.

Rope Tension

The rope should be checked regularly for wear and tear. A frayed or loose rope can snap or whip unpredictably, causing injury to players' hands or faces. The rope should be kept taut but not too tight, allowing for a natural wrap.

Clear Area

The area around the court should be clear of obstacles, spectators, or other players. Only authorized players should be on the court during play. Spectators should stand behind a designated line to avoid being hit by the ball.

Fall Prevention

Players should be careful when running and hitting the ball. They should avoid running into each other or the pole. If a player falls, they should get up safely and not continue playing if injured.

Age Appropriateness

Tetherball can be dangerous for very young children. The ball should be soft and lightweight, and the pole height should be adjusted to match the players' size. Supervision is recommended for children under 10 years old.
