Section 1: Introduction

KanJam (also written "Kan Jam") is a fast-paced, two-on-two flying disc and target game invented in Auburn, New York in 2004 and popularized as a backyard, beach, tailgate, and campground game. The object of the game is simple to explain but surprisingly skillful to master: one player throws a flying disc toward a slotted metal or plastic can, while that player's partner — standing beside the can — tries to redirect or "deflect" the disc so it strikes or enters the can. Teams alternate throws, accumulate points, and race to reach an exact score of 21, or end the game instantly with a spectacular "Bucket" or "Slam Dunk" shot.

Part of KanJam's enduring appeal is that it rewards teamwork over raw individual skill — a great deflector can turn a wild throw into a game-winning play, and communication between partners (calling "off!" or "leave it!") is as important as the throw itself. The game requires only a flat patch of ground, two cans, and a disc, which makes it a staple of tailgates, cookouts, campsites, dorm quads, and beaches.

This rulebook reflects the standard rules published with the official Kan Jam game set, supplemented with widely observed house rules and casual variations. Because KanJam is played overwhelmingly in recreational and social settings rather than sanctioned leagues, groups are encouraged to agree on any local variations (see Section 5) before starting play.

Section 2: Equipment

  • Cans (Goals): Two matching cylindrical cans/goals, each standing approximately 28 inches (71 cm) tall with a diameter of about 15.75 inches (40 cm). Official Kan Jam cans are made of durable plastic with a weighted or wide base for stability on grass or sand.
  • Slot: Each can has a rectangular slot cut into the top rim, roughly 9 inches wide by 4 inches tall, through which a disc can pass to score a "Bucket" or "Slam Dunk." The slot always faces the thrower on the opposite end of the field.
  • Flying Disc: One official flying disc, typically a 175-gram, 10.75-inch (27 cm) diameter disc similar to a regulation Ultimate disc. Softer, lighter "beach" or "mini" discs are common variants for casual or wind-heavy play.
  • Optional accessories: A carrying/travel bag, a scorecard or portable scoreboard, and stakes or sandbags to anchor cans on windy days or soft/sandy surfaces.
  • Number of sets: Only one set (two cans, one disc) is required per game; tournament or "round robin" setups may use multiple sets across several fields simultaneously.

Section 3: Playing Area

KanJam is played on any flat, open area of grass, sand, or hard-packed ground roughly 60–70 feet long and at least 15–20 feet wide, though the game is forgiving of imperfect or irregular terrain.

  • Can placement: The two cans are set up facing each other, with their slots oriented toward the opposing thrower, at a standard distance of 50 feet (15.24 m) apart, measured center-to-center.
  • Throwing position: Each thrower stands directly beside — or just behind — their own team's can and throws from that spot toward the opposing can. A player does not need a fixed "throwing line," but must keep at least one foot in contact with the ground within a reasonable distance of the can when releasing the disc (see Section 7 for foot-fault house rules).
  • Buffer zone: Because the deflector stands immediately next to the target can, a buffer of at least 5–10 feet on either side and behind each can is recommended to give the deflector room to move and to keep spectators safely clear of errant discs.
  • Surface considerations: Level, mowed grass or firm sand is ideal. Sloped or uneven ground can cause cans to tip; on windy days, stake or weight the cans, particularly the goal facing directly into the wind.

Section 4: Players/Officials

  • Team size: KanJam is played by two teams of two players each (four players total). Larger groups commonly rotate players in and out between games or organize a round-robin bracket.
  • Player roles: On any given throw, one player from each team acts as the "Thrower" and their partner, stationed at the target can, acts as the "Deflector" (sometimes called the "Catcher" or "Goalie"). Roles alternate between partners throughout the game (see Section 5, Rotation).
  • Starting order: Teams determine which team throws first by mutual agreement, a coin flip, or a disc flip (spin the disc; whichever team calls the correct side it lands on throws first).
  • Officials: KanJam is self-officiated in nearly all recreational and tournament settings. Players call their own violations and agree on ambiguous scoring calls; casual house-league or bar/backyard tournaments may designate one neutral scorekeeper to track points and settle disputes.
  • Substitutions: In casual "winner stays" or rotating-partner formats, substitutions may occur between games but not mid-game, to preserve fair alternating throws.

Section 5: Rules of Play

5.1 Turn order. Teams alternate throws. On a given turn, the throwing team's Thrower releases the disc from beside their own can, aiming for the opposing team's can 50 feet away. That team's Deflector, standing beside the target can, may redirect the disc with their hands, arms, or body to help it strike or enter the can, or may choose to let it fly past without touching it ("leave it").

5.2 Deflecting, not catching. A Deflector may tip, slap, punch, kick, or otherwise redirect the disc in-flight, but may not cleanly catch, trap, or cradle the disc and then place or drop it into the can. Any disc that is caught and controlled before being directed toward the can is a dead throw and scores no points (see Section 7).

5.3 Rotation of roles. After each player has thrown once — meaning all four players (two Throwers, two Deflectors) have completed one turn — the round is complete. Partners then swap roles: the player who threw becomes the Deflector at their own can, and the player who deflected becomes the Thrower, aiming at the opposite can. This rotation repeats every round so that each player alternates between throwing and deflecting on consecutive turns.

5.4 Live disc. A thrown disc remains live and in play until it comes to rest on the ground, strikes the can, passes through the slot, or is caught/trapped by the Deflector. Once dead, the disc is retrieved and handed to the next Thrower for the following turn.

5.5 Communication. Because Deflectors decide in real time whether to redirect or let a disc pass, teams are encouraged to communicate clearly and immediately (e.g., "off!" to leave it, or nonverbal signals) to avoid confusion, especially on borderline throws near the can.

5.6 Common variations and house rules.

  • Team size variants: Singles ("1v1") play has each player manage both throwing and deflecting duties for their own can, alternating solo turns.
  • Distance adjustment: Casual or youth games often shorten the 50-foot distance (e.g., to 30–40 feet) for smaller yards or younger players.
  • "No deflect" mode: A simplified variant for beginners in which only direct hits and Buckets count, and deflecting is disallowed, used to teach throwing accuracy first.
  • Best-of series: Casual tournaments often play best-of-three games to a match win rather than a single game to 21.
  • Wind/weather calls: Groups frequently agree in advance that a gust-blown disc knocking over an empty can (with no player contact) results in a re-throw rather than a scored or dead play.

Section 6: Scoring

Points are scored only by the throwing team, based on how the disc contacts the target can. There are four scoring outcomes:

  • Deflection (1 point): The Deflector touches or redirects the disc in-flight and it subsequently strikes any part of the can.
  • Direct Hit (2 points): The disc strikes the can directly, without being touched by the Deflector at all.
  • Bucket — Instant Win (disc thrown directly into the slot): If the Thrower's disc goes directly into the can's slot without any deflection by the opposing player, that team wins the game immediately, regardless of the current score.
  • Slam Dunk — Instant Win (disc deflected into the slot): If the Deflector redirects the disc into their own team's can through the slot, that team wins the game immediately, regardless of the current score. (Note: the Deflector is scoring for their own team by guiding the disc into their own can — this is the signature highlight play of KanJam.)

6.1 Winning condition. Aside from an instant-win Bucket or Slam Dunk, a team wins the game by reaching a total score of exactly 21 points. If a scoring throw would push a team's total beyond 21, those points do not count and the team's score remains unchanged for that turn (commonly called "busting"). Play continues until a team lands on exactly 21 or achieves an instant win.

6.2 Score tracking. Either team may keep score verbally, on a scorecard, or on a portable scoreboard. Because reaching exactly 21 matters, teams should track running totals carefully as the score approaches the target.

6.3 Match/series scoring. In tournament settings, a "match" is commonly best-of-three individual games, with the first team to win two games taking the match.

Section 7: Violations/Penalties

  • Catch/trap violation: If the Deflector catches, traps, or cleanly cradles the disc (rather than deflecting it in continuous motion) before it strikes the can or passes through the slot, no points are scored on that throw. The disc is dead and play moves to the next turn.
  • Foot fault: A Thrower who steps well beyond their own can's throwing area at the moment of release (commonly enforced as moving noticeably closer to the target can to gain an unfair distance advantage) forfeits that throw, per house-rule agreement among most casual groups; there is no universal, precisely codified foot line in casual play, so groups should agree on an acceptable release zone before starting.
  • Can displacement: If a Deflector's body or an illegal action (rather than the disc itself) knocks the can over or out of position, any resulting "hit" is voided and does not score; the can is reset and play continues on the next turn.
  • Out-of-turn throw: A disc thrown out of the correct rotation order is a dead throw with no points scored; the correct Thrower repeats the turn.
  • Interference: Deliberate interference by a non-participating player (e.g., a teammate or bystander touching the disc) voids the throw with no points scored, and the disc is re-thrown by the same Thrower.
  • Delay of game: In timed or tournament formats, teams that take an excessive amount of time between throws may be assessed a warning or a forfeited turn at the discretion of the group or event organizer, since there is no official shot clock in standard recreational play.
  • Disputes: Because KanJam is self-officiated, any disputed call (e.g., whether a deflection or a catch occurred) should be resolved by mutual agreement between all four players before the next throw; a neutral bystander may be asked to make the call if the players cannot agree.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

  • Playing surface: Choose a flat, open area free of holes, roots, sprinkler heads, rocks, or other tripping hazards, since Deflectors often move quickly and unpredictably to redirect an incoming disc.
  • Clear zone: Keep spectators, pets, and other bystanders well clear of the throwing lanes and the area behind each can, as missed discs and deflections can travel unpredictably in any direction.
  • Disc impact awareness: A hard plastic flying disc traveling at speed can cause facial or eye injury if it strikes a player who is not expecting it; Deflectors should keep their eyes on the disc at all times and be ready to duck or step aside rather than forcing a risky deflection.
  • Can stability: Secure cans on windy days or soft/uneven ground (sand, mulch, slopes) with stakes, sandbags, or by choosing a more sheltered location, to prevent tipping that could cause a trip hazard or an unfair bounce.
  • Weather and hydration: As an outdoor game often played at tailgates, beaches, or in direct sun, players should wear appropriate footwear, use sunscreen, and stay hydrated during extended play sessions, especially in hot weather.
  • Alcohol and judgment: KanJam is frequently played in social settings where alcohol is present; groups should exercise caution, as impaired throwing and deflecting increases the risk of accidental disc strikes to players or bystanders.
  • Youth play: When children are playing, consider using a softer or lighter "beach"-style disc, shortening the throwing distance, and providing adult supervision, particularly around the deflecting position closest to the incoming disc.
