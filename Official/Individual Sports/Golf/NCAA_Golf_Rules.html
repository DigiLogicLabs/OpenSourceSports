Section 1: Introduction

NCAA Golf is the collegiate variant of golf contested by NCAA Division I, II, and III institutions across both men's and women's brackets, with regular-season tournaments and conference championships culminating in the NCAA Golf Championship in late May. NCAA Golf uses the Rules of Golf (jointly maintained by the USGA and The R&A) as the playing-rule baseline with NCAA-specific modifications around team format, tournament structure, accommodation rules, local rules, scoring, and pace of play. The current edition is the 2025-26 NCAA Golf Rules Book, built on the 2023 Edition of the Rules of Golf.

Effective January 1, 2026, the USGA and The R&A issued Additional Clarifications of the 2023 Rules of Golf that amend how a moved ball at rest is penalized (Rule 9.4b, see Section 5) and how a broken or significantly damaged club may be replaced during a round (Rule 4.1b(4), see Section 2), and introduced a new Model Local Rule (MLR F-2.3) expanding pitch-mark relief under Rule 16.3b that Committees may choose to adopt (see Sections 2 and 5). Separately, the NCAA Division I Board of Directors finalized an age-based eligibility model in summer 2026 that governs how many seasons a golfer may compete (see Section 4). As of this verification, the NCAA had not yet published a 2026-27 edition of the Golf Rules Book incorporating these changes; this entry applies them against the 2025-26 book pending that release. For underlying playing rules see the sibling R&A Rules of Golf entry; for USGA championship application see USGA Championship Golf; for PGA TOUR see Golf-PGA TOUR; for KPMG Women's PGA see KPMG Women's PGA Championship.

Section 2: Equipment

  • Conforming clubs (USGA/R&A List of Conforming Golf Clubs)
  • Conforming golf balls (USGA/R&A List of Conforming Golf Balls)
  • 14-club limit per stipulated round (Rule 4.1b)
  • Updated for 2026: if a club is broken or significantly damaged in the normal course of play and taken out of play, Rule 4.1b(4) now permits the player to build a replacement club using components carried by or for the player or by or for any other player playing on the course, provided the replacement fills the gap left by the removed club and no complete finished club is borrowed from another player. The other restrictions of Rule 4.1b(4) (e.g., the club may not have been discarded or given away, and the replacement must not unduly delay play) continue to apply.
  • Distance-only DMD permitted; slope/wind/elevation functions NOT permitted at NCAA championship (per typical NCAA Notice to Players)
  • One-Ball Rule (Model Local Rule G-4) applies at NCAA championship
  • New for 2026 (optional): Model Local Rule F-2.3 lets a Committee grant free relief under Rule 16.3b when a referee determines a player's ball lies below ground level in an unrepaired pitch-mark made by any player's stroke (not only the player's own), rather than only their own pitch-mark. The MLR is restricted to competitions with on-site referees able to make that determination, and it applies only where and if the NCAA Championship Committee adopts it for a given event; check the year's Notice to Players before relying on it.

  • Daily hole locations published before each round

Section 4: Players & Officials

  • NCAA team: 5 players per team in stroke play; lowest 4 scores count per round
  • NCAA Championship match play: 5-player team; 5 individual matches per dual contest
  • Caddies: NCAA-specific accommodation rules (some venues / divisions allow caddies; others do not)
  • Officials: NCAA Tournament Director, rules officials on each hole, video review at championship-level

Eligibility (updated for 2026-27)

In summer 2026, the NCAA Division I Board of Directors finalized an age-based eligibility model that replaces the prior "four seasons of competition within five calendar years" structure with up to five seasons of competition, eliminating sport-specific season-of-competition limits, redshirt bookkeeping, and eligibility-extension waivers for golfers governed by the new model. The five-year clock starts the academic year after a student-athlete turns 19, or upon first full-time college enrollment, whichever comes first. Recruits first enrolling in fall 2027 or later are governed solely by the age-based model; golfers who enrolled in fall 2026, and current student-athletes with remaining eligibility from prior seasons, are governed by whichever of the prior rules or the age-based model produces the more favorable outcome for that individual. Golfers with grounds for a waiver or extension under the prior system needed to file by July 31, 2026, before that option was retired.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Format

  • Regular-season tournaments: typically 54-72 hole stroke play; team scoring
  • Conference championship: same stroke-play format, with conference-defined scoring
  • NCAA Championship: 4-day stroke play (72 holes) reduces to top 8 teams + top individuals; the top 8 teams advance to match play bracket

Ball Moved at Rest (updated for 2026)

Rule 9.4b now distinguishes penalty severity by the player's awareness. If a player causes their ball at rest to move and fails to replace it before their next stroke, but was neither aware nor should reasonably have been aware that the ball had moved or might have moved, the player incurs only the one-stroke penalty under Rule 9.4b if it later becomes known that they caused the movement — they are not treated as having played from a wrong place. If the player was aware their ball at rest might have moved and still failed to replace it before their next stroke, the general penalty (two strokes / loss of hole) for playing from a wrong place under Rule 14.7a applies instead once it becomes known they caused the movement.

Pace of Play

  • NCAA pace-of-play policy similar to USGA: each group has a timing par; bad-time accumulation triggers stroke penalties

Suspension of Play

  • 1-tone air horn: normal suspension; 3-tone: immediate suspension (lightning); short+steady: resumption

Section 6: Scoring

  • Stroke play: lowest cumulative score wins individual; team score = sum of best 4 of 5 individual scores per round
  • Match play: holes won determines match outcome; team match = best of 5 individual matches
  • Tiebreakers: aggregate strokes; sudden-death playoff at championship

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

  • Standard Rules of Golf penalties (1 stroke, 2 strokes, loss of hole, DQ)
  • Updated: a ball moved at rest and not replaced before the next stroke draws a 1-stroke penalty under Rule 9.4b if the player had no awareness of the movement, or the general penalty under Rule 14.7a if the player was aware the ball might have moved — see Section 5
  • One-Ball Rule breach: 2 strokes per hole, max 4 strokes/round
  • 14-club limit breach: 2 strokes per hole, max 4 strokes/round
  • Equipment standards breach (non-conforming club or ball, including an improperly built Rule 4.1b(4) replacement club): DQ
  • Pace-of-play: warning → 1 stroke → 2 strokes → DQ
  • NCAA Sportsmanship Code violations: ejection + disciplinary review

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Lightning detection halts outdoor play; 30-30 rule supplemented by professional detection. WBGT-based heat thresholds; mandatory water/cooling stations on course. Course safety: spectator management, sight-line management, "Fore!" warning. Medical: certified athletic trainer + EMT on site at championship-level; AED + emergency action plan at every venue.
