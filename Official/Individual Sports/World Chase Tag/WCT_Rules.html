Section 1: Introduction

World Chase Tag (WCT) is competitive tag played by parkour-style athletes on a purpose-built obstacle arena. Athletes alternate as Chaser and Evader in 20-second chases. This entry follows the WCT8 Sporting Regulations, updated July 23, 2026, which expressly supersede prior versions and apply throughout the WCT8 season. WCT8 adds a club and A-/B-team structure, athlete licensing, ranking-based championship qualification, revised roster rules, a disciplinary-card system, and an experimental extended match format.

Section 2: Equipment

  • Athletic apparel: official competition kit without loose or unsafe items.
  • Footwear: suitable athletic shoes that do not damage or leave residue on the Quad.
  • Permitted protection: soft gel, cloth, or flexible-plastic padding for knees, shins, groin, and elbows; mouthguards, athletic tape, kinesiology tape, and non-residue gloves are permitted.
  • Prohibited: hard plastic protection other than permitted shin/groin guards, chalk, chemical grip enhancers, or anything that changes traction or leaves debris.

Section 3: Playing Area

  • The Quad: 12 m × 12 m WCT-standard arena with 20+ obstacles (walls, vaults, rails, pyramids, ramps)
  • Padded competition floor; padded outer perimeter
  • Standardized obstacle layout for international competition; minor variations for showcase events
  • Camera positions overhead + corner for broadcast coverage

Section 4: Players & Officials

  • Club roster: WCT8 permits up to 10 registered athletes, with six—the Super Six—declared for an event.
  • On the Quad: one Chaser competes against one Evader in each chase.
  • Divisions: Open and Women's divisions have separate leaderboards. Official title-event athletes must be at least 16.
  • Licensing: every athlete in a ranking-bearing event must hold a current WCT athlete license.
  • Officials: the Head Referee has final event-level authority for situations not expressly covered by the regulations.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Standard Match

  • The standard WCT8 match consists of 16 chases.
  • Each chase lasts no more than 20 seconds.
  • A successful tag ends the chase; an Evader who remains untagged for the full time records an evasion.
  • Match-start roles are selected by a one-round Rock-Paper-Scissors decision. The winning team chooses whether to chase or evade first.
  • In a tied match, Sudden Death role choice belongs to the team that did not win the match-start role selection.

Boundary and Tag Clarifications

  • An airborne athlete remains in play until first contacting an out-of-bounds surface.
  • If athletes exit simultaneously, the one who touches the ground last wins the chase; an indistinguishable result is replayed.
  • A tag made before the Chaser subsequently exits the Quad remains valid.

Experimental Extended Format

WCT8 permits designated matches to trial an extended format alongside the standard 16-chase match. An extended-format squad may field up to eight athletes; WCT may consider it for wider adoption from WCT9.

Section 6: Scoring

  • In round-robin competition, a regulation win awards 3 points, a draw 1 point to each team, and a loss 0.
  • If a drawn match is followed by a Sudden Death Chase-Off, the winner receives one additional point, for 2 total; the losing team keeps 1.
  • Group-stage ties are broken in order by head-to-head result, evasion difference, total evasions, then disciplinary cards.
  • WCT8's official Open and Women's leaderboards use ELO ratings and determine ranking-based invitations to title events; wild cards are discontinued.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

  • Yellow card: formal warning. Two yellows in one match, or three across one event, trigger suspension under the WCT8 thresholds.
  • Red card: sanction for dangerous or seriously unsporting conduct and suspension for the remainder of the match and following match.
  • Match flow: a sent-off athlete cannot contest the next chase as Evader; that chase is recorded as a No Contest Tag.
  • Eligibility: fielding an unlicensed athlete can cause forfeiture and club discipline.
  • Anti-doping: the WADA Prohibited List applies; testing may be conducted at WCT events.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Athlete protection is the controlling principle of the WCT8 regulations and companion Code of Conduct. Athletes must inspect and adapt to the Quad, report hazards, maintain their own physical readiness, and use only permitted protective equipment. Referees may inspect equipment, stop competition for medical or technical incidents, and sanction dangerous conduct. WCT8's Code of Conduct applies at sanctioned venues, training and accreditation areas, official hospitality and media spaces, and relevant participant conduct online or away from the venue.
