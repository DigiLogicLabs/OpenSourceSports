Section 1: Introduction

Rainbow Six Siege is a 5v5 first-person tactical shooter in which two teams compete on various maps across multiple rounds by attacking and defending objectives. All competitions of BLAST R6 are played on PC (BLAST R6 Global Rulebook Season 2026, Section 5.1).

This entry is based on the BLAST R6 Global Rulebook, Season 2026 (cover marked "Last update – March 2026"), the umbrella rulebook for the official Rainbow Six Siege competitive circuit run by Ubisoft and BLAST. It applies to BLAST R6 and any affiliated tournament that references it (Introduction). Each Tournament Organizer may also issue "Specific Rules" for its competition; where the Specific Rules conflict with the Global Rulebook, the Global Rulebook prevails, but not retroactively (Section 5.3).

Participation is conditioned on acceptance of the Rulebook, the Specific Rules, the Ubisoft Code of Conduct, the Rainbow Six: Siege Code of Conduct and the Ubisoft Terms of Use (Introduction; Section 1.1.1). Ubisoft and/or BLAST may amend the Rules with or without prior notice (Section 1.2.1).

Season structure (Section 2). BLAST R6 Season 2026 is made up of five Regions: APL, CNL, EML, NAL and SAL (Section 2.1). The Season begins in March with the Off-Season Transfer Window and ends after the Six Invitational 2027 (Section 2.2). Global competitions include the BLAST R6 Majors in May and November 2026 (Section 2.3.1.1) and the Esports World Cup (EWC 2026) in August 2026 (Section 2.3.1.2). Twenty Teams in total attend each Major (Section 2.3.1.1). The CNL Region is operated by Tencent under its own rules, though the Global Rulebook applies to CNL Teams in global competition (Section 2.1).

Season 2026 changes listed by the rulebook include a new 120-second Role Swap Timeout at the end of Round 6 (Section 5.1.5), an adjusted map ban process (Section 5.1.3.2), and a targeted map updates process (Section 5.1.3.1).

Section 2: Equipment

All BLAST R6 competitions are played on PC (Section 5.1), so a computer is required to compete.

Equipment is set by the Tournament Organizer. For offline events and competitions, the Tournament Organizer must list all required Players' equipment and state which items it provides and which Players must bring. It must also establish any limits on Players' access to their equipment and maintain a submission and validation process for all equipment used (Section 5.3, "Equipment"). For offline play, it must also set up a submission and validation process for any drivers or third-party programs needed for equipment brought by Players (Section 5.3, "Third party program usage").

Equipment tampering. Equipment tampering, use of unauthorized third-party programs affecting gameplay, and macros are strictly forbidden and are treated as cheating (Section 5.3, "Cheat & Equipment tampering").

LAN restrictions (Section 5.5.2).

  • The PC and peripherals provided by the Tournament Organizer may only be used for their approved function (Section 5.5.2).
  • Use of any electronic device or other non-approved peripherals during competitive play is strictly prohibited and may result in Sanction(s), including telephones, tablets and vaping devices (Section 5.5.2).
  • Players and Coaches may not handle or touch other Players' equipment after a match has started (Section 5.5.2).

In-game cosmetics (Section 5.1.3.5). Players may only use the operator's default skins, Pro R6Share teams' branded cosmetics, Six Major branded cosmetics, and Esports Programs cosmetics, except that the Thermite Legacy Set uniform and headgear may not be used. All other battle dress uniforms and headgears are banned. Weapon skins, weapon charms and operator background cards are not restricted. Drone skins and operator gadget skins are forbidden; only default drone and gadget skins may be used.

Section 3: Playing Area

Play takes place in the Rainbow Six Siege game client on maps from the official competitive map pool (Section 5.1.3.1), in the Bomb game mode (Section 5.1.2).

Hosting (Section 5.1.2). By default the host of the game should be the observer, an admin or a commentator. Tournament Organizers may specify otherwise, but hosting can never fall on a Player or Team Staff member. A game must be hosted on a local server using the Pro League playlist, or in a local custom game with the settings listed in Section 5 below.

Map pool (Section 5.1.3.1). The official map pool consists of 9 maps played on the 5v5 game mode:

  • Bank (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Border (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Chalet (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Clubhouse (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Consulate (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Kafe (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Lair (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Nighthaven Labs (Section 5.1.3.1)
  • Fortress (Section 5.1.3.1)

Ubisoft may change maps in the pool at any point during the Season. Tournament Organizers and Teams are notified at minimum two weeks before a change to the map pool. Ubisoft may also make targeted map updates to any aspect of a competitive map (for example redesigning a site, closing doors, removing windows, relocating walls, adding or removing cover, or changing or adding bombsite locations) at any point, with or without prior notice (Section 5.1.3.1).

Competition area. Players and Coach are strictly forbidden to leave the competition area during Tactical Timeouts, Role Swap Timeouts or Technical Pauses without express approval of a Tournament Official (Sections 5.1.4, 5.1.5, 5.1.6.1).

Section 4: Players & Officials

Roster (Section 4.1). A "Roster" is a group of 5 to 7 Players: 5 Starters and up to 2 Substitutes. Any Roster must have 5 Starters to be validly registered. A Team keeps a Main Roster and may constitute a Secondary Roster.

Minimum Team composition (Section 4.2). A Team must always have one License Holder (Teams with a License) or one Appointed Team Member (Teams without a License), plus five Players who are the Starters of the Main Roster.

Additional composition (Section 4.3). A Team may register up to 2 Substitutes with its Main Roster, or constitute a Secondary Roster with 5 different Players from the Starters of its Main Roster. Two of the Secondary Roster's Starters may be registered as Substitutes of the Main Roster, but not the reverse. A Team may register 2 dedicated Substitutes for its Secondary Roster. The total number of Players registered across both Rosters cannot exceed 12. A Team may also have one Team Manager, one Coach per Roster, and any number of Support Staff.

Eligibility (Section 3.3).

  • Each Player and Team Staff member must be 18 or older on their official government-issued documentation at the time of their first match in BLAST R6 (Section 3.3.2).
  • Players must reside in the Region in which they compete; Specific Rules may add residency requirements. A Coach may work from a different Region for online competitions but must be physically present for offline competitions (Section 3.3.3).
  • Players and Team Staff may not own or use any Ubisoft account not in good standing (Section 3.3.4) and may not be under a competitive suspension (Section 3.3.5).

Substitutions (Section 4.8). A Team may replace one or two Starters with registered Substitutes for a given match. The Coach or Manager must notify the tournament administrator at least 60 minutes before the match for best-of-one, or before the first map for best-of-three or best-of-five series. Fifteen minutes before the match, the administrator notifies both Teams of the substitutions at the same time, and no additional substitutions may be requested afterwards.

Insufficient Players (Section 4.4). If a Team cannot field five Players, including through a Substitute, a Coach or other Team Staff member may request to replace the unavailable Player, subject to Ubisoft and Tournament Organizer approval. Failing that, the Team may exceptionally request a "Stand-In Player" to avoid forfeiting for breach of the composition requirements; this is always subject to approval.

Roster Lock (Section 4.5). A Roster is locked upon registration with the Tournament Organizer, and once locked no new Player or Coach may be added. Teams with a License may make Roster Changes only during Transfer Windows (Sections 4.5, 4.6.1, 4.6.3); Season 2026 windows include May 18, 2026 to June 3, 2026, August 15, 2026 to August 31, 2026, and November 16, 2026 to December 2, 2026 (all 9am CET), and the Off-Season window from February 15, 2027 until March 1, 2027 (Section 4.6.3).

Officials. The Tournament Organizer fields "Administrative Staff" (also "Tournament Officials"), who administer the competition and enforce the Rules, and "Production and Broadcasting Staff" (Section 1.1.2). Tournament Officials decide Technical Pauses (Section 5.1.6.1), and a referee or tournament administrator listens to Coach communication during Tactical and Role Swap Timeouts (Sections 5.1.4, 5.1.5). Each Team has a single Point of Contact (Section 1.2.2).

Section 5: Rules of Play

Match format (Section 5.1.1). A match can be played as best of 1, best of 2, best of 3 or best of 5 maps, depending on the Specific Rules of the competition.

Match settings (Section 5.1.2), applied consistently across all BLAST R6 competitions:

  • HUD Settings: Pro League; Game Mode: Bomb; Ban Phase: Pro League; Ban Timer: 20 seconds (Section 5.1.2)
  • Number of rounds: 12; Attack/Defense Role Swap: after round 6; Overtime Rounds: 3 (or Infinite); Overtime score difference: 2; Overtime role change: 1 (Section 5.1.2)
  • Pick Phase timer: 15; Operator HP: 100; Friendly fire damage: 100; Friendly fire in Prep Phase: Off; Reverse Friendly Fire: Off; Injured: 20; Sprint: On; Lean: On; Death Replay: Off (Section 5.1.2)
  • Plant Duration: 7; Defuse Duration: 7; Fuse Time: 45; Defuser Carrier Selection: On; Preparation: 45; Action: 180 (Section 5.1.2)
  • Tactical Timeout requests available per Team: 1; Timeout Duration: 45; Role Swap Timeout: On, Duration 120 (effective with the first Regional Kickoff match) (Section 5.1.2)

Map bans (Section 5.1.3.2). A match begins with a map ban sequence in which teams "A" and "B" alternate banning and picking from the official pool.

  • Best of 1: coin toss, winner chooses who bans first; A Ban – B Ban – A Ban – B Ban – A Ban – B Ban – A Ban – B Pick (Section 5.1.3.2).
  • Best of 3: coin toss, winner chooses who bans first; A Ban – B Ban – A Ban – B Ban – A Pick – B Pick – A Ban – B Ban – Decider. The team with the highest series round differential receives decider map side selection; ties may use seeding or ultimately a coin flip (Section 5.1.3.2).
  • Best of 5: Single Elimination uses A Ban – B Ban – A Pick – B Pick – A Ban – B Ban – A Pick – B Pick – Decider (highest series round differential gets decider side selection). Double Elimination gives the upper bracket team decider side selection, using UB Pick / LB Ban / UB Ban / LB Pick / UB Ban / LB Ban / UB Pick / LB Pick / Decider (Section 5.1.3.2).

The Team that did not pick a map gets "Side Selection" on it; if the map goes to overtime, the Team that did not get Side Selection gets "Overtime Side Selection" (Section 5.1.3.2).

Operator bans (Section 5.1.3.3). All ban timers are 20 seconds.

  • Before round 1: both teams simultaneously ban 1 operator of the opposite side (attackers ban a defender and vice-versa), twice, for 4 bans in total. Coaches may communicate with players during this sequence (Section 5.1.3.3).
  • Before round 4: each team makes an additional ban, bringing the total to 6. Coaches may not communicate with players (Section 5.1.3.3).
  • Before round 7: bans reset, teams swap sides, and the pre-round-1 sequence repeats (4 bans). Coaches may communicate; this does not count as a tactical timeout (Section 5.1.3.3).
  • Before round 10: the pre-round-4 sequence repeats, for 6 bans. Coaches may not communicate (Section 5.1.3.3).
  • Overtime: no new bans; the 3 bans each team had for a given side are automatically replicated, 6 operators banned at all times, with no Coach communication (Section 5.1.3.3).

Every new operator is available for competitive play upon release. Ubisoft may ban specific operators, gadgets, equipment or attachments to guarantee fair competition (Section 5.1.3.4).

Tactical Timeouts (Section 5.1.4). The Coach notifies the referee or tournament administrator at the end of a round or at the very start of the next operator pick phase. The game pauses for a 45-second timer during which the Coach may communicate with the Players; the communication is monitored and recorded and may be broadcast. Each Team may request one Tactical Timeout per map; unused timeouts do not carry over. A timeout requested before Round 4 or Round 6 starts once the Operator Bans have concluded. When one Team calls a timeout, the other Team's Coach may also communicate with its Players.

Role Swap Timeout (Section 5.1.5). Automatically triggered at the end of Round 6, right before attackers and defenders swap sides, with Coach communication allowed for its duration.

Issues during a match (Section 5.1.6). A Technical Pause interrupts play for an issue preventing normal play and is decided at Tournament Officials' sole discretion; it has no pre-determined duration (Section 5.1.6.1). Once the action phase starts, Players should raise technical issues to Tournament Officials, and play is not paused during the round unless they approve it (Section 5.1.6.1). If a Technical Pause and a Tactical Timeout are called for the same instance, the Technical Pause is issued first (Section 5.1.6.1). The Tournament Organizer may issue a Re-host at any time in the interest of competitive integrity (Section 5.1.6.2).

Bugs and exploits (Section 5.1.3.6). Permitted unintended mechanics include using equipment or defusing through a destructible surface, destroying a hatch with the defuser on it, smoking through walls, and placing Hibana pellets, Ace SELMA charges and Thermite exothermic charges anywhere. Banned ones include standing on a window ledge undetected, shield boosting onto an undetected window ledge, using a Mira black mirror to boost, placing Maestro's Evil Eye on Alibi's decoy, vaulting onto skylight windows, and Vigil boosting that renders Vigil undetectable. Unlisted exploits must be escalated to Ubisoft, BLAST or the Tournament Organizer.

Section 6: Scoring

A map is played over 12 rounds with an attack/defense role swap after round 6 (Section 5.1.2). The Bomb game mode uses a 45-second preparation phase and a 180-second action phase, with 7-second plant and defuse durations and a 45-second fuse time (Section 5.1.2).

Overtime. The match settings specify an overtime score difference of 2, with overtime rounds of 3 (or Infinite) (Section 5.1.2). If a map goes to overtime, operator bans from the previous side are automatically replicated (Section 5.1.3.3), and the Team that did not have Side Selection gets Overtime Side Selection (Section 5.1.3.2).

Forfeits. A team awarded a victory because the opposing team forfeited is credited a 7-0 scoreline, and that scoreline is not taken into account when tiebreakers are applied (Section 5.4).

Tiebreakers (Section 5.4) apply to Regional Leagues, Majors or any format supporting them, unless the Specific Rules state otherwise. They are applied sequentially:

  • Matches played only in best of 1: (1) Round difference, (2) Head-to-head, (3) Match win percentage, (4) Round win percentage, (5) Tiebreaker match (Section 5.4).
  • Some or all matches in best of 3: (1) Map difference, (2) Round difference, (3) Head-to-head, (4) Match win percentage, (5) Round win percentage, (6) Tiebreaker match (Section 5.4).

Global Point System (Section 2.4). Competitive results in Majors and Regional competitions award SI Points toward qualification for the Six Invitational; SI qualification tiebreaker rules are in Section 2.4.2.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Sportsmanship rules (Section 5.2) that may be penalized with Sanction(s):

  • Cheating is heavily penalized. It includes third-party software, tampering with the game's code or servers, and stream sniping (obtaining information from watching the match's broadcast while playing it) (Section 5.2).
  • Match-fixing, meaning pre-determining or tampering with a match's outcome or events, including through bribery, threats or match throwing, is prohibited (Section 5.2).
  • Players may not intentionally forfeit a game, teamkill, or manipulate rankings or brackets (for example, to obtain a lower seed or dodge an opponent) (Section 5.2).
  • Intentional teamkilling is strictly forbidden (Section 5.2).
  • Spawnkilling, defined as killing an opponent within the first 2 seconds of the action phase of a round, is strictly forbidden and results in a penalty (Section 5.2).
  • Players may not curse, taunt or use vulgar language, and may not verbally abuse Tournament Officials (Section 5.2).
  • Discriminatory or threatening conduct, inappropriate names, illegal possession or use of alcohol, drugs or performance-enhancing substances during the tournament, impersonation of officials, theft, intimidation, and harassment are not tolerated (Section 5.2).

In-game penalties. Using a non-permitted battle dress uniform or headgear results in a warning for a first offense and a round loss for any additional offense (Section 5.1.3.5). Using a banned exploit carries, at minimum, an immediate round loss and/or further Sanction(s) (Section 5.1.3.6). Failure to comply with the restrictions applying during a Technical Pause will be sanctioned (Section 5.1.6.1).

Types of Sanction (Section 6.1):

  • Competitive warning: the lowest form, which may precede further disciplinary actions (Section 6.1).
  • Competitive suspension and ban: temporary, or in exceptional cases permanent, barring from BLAST R6 competition; a Player or Team may also be barred during an investigation (Section 6.1).
  • Fines: deducted from prize money or applied as prize pool forfeiture in case of suspension (Section 6.1).
  • Global Points withdrawal: a percentage of a Team's points in the Global Point System (Section 6.1).
  • Competitive penalties: round loss, map loss or match forfeit, depending on severity (Section 6.1).

Sanctions may be combined and escalated for repeated infractions. Ubisoft will not investigate or sanction an infraction if more than twenty-four months elapsed between the alleged occurrence and its notification, unless required by law (Section 6.1).

Enforcement (Section 6.2). Tournament Organizer officials may issue competitive warnings, and may issue round loss and map loss penalties without consulting BLAST or Ubisoft if issued during the match. All other Sanctions are discussed between the Tournament Organizer, BLAST and Ubisoft. Ubisoft and BLAST must be notified of all infractions, which are recorded in the Penalty Tracker (Section 6.6). Ubisoft/BLAST may impose preliminary conservative measures such as temporary suspension during an investigation.

Investigations (Section 6.4). Within 15 business days of notification of a breach, Ubisoft, BLAST and/or the Tournament Organizer will make reasonable commercial efforts to inform the organization involved; the persons involved then have 7 business days to submit supporting elements contradicting the claims.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

This section summarizes the Global Rulebook's provisions on conduct, venue safety and protection of participants.

Protective and supportive measures (Section 6.3). Supportive and protective measures are services and assistance available to any person affected by discrimination, harassment, relationship violence or sexual misconduct ("Misbehavioral Incidents") (Section 6.3.1). On receipt of a report, Ubisoft and BLAST provide reasonable and appropriate measures during the investigation designed to preserve the individuals affected, address safety concerns, and maintain the integrity of the investigation. This includes assistance in contacting law enforcement and in implementing protective orders where practicable and where the affected person asks for assistance (Section 6.3.2).

Conduct affecting health and safety (Section 5.2). Deliberate intimidation, actual or threatened physical or emotional violence, any action endangering the health or safety of any person, stalking, harassment and unsolicited sexual attention are not tolerated and may result in Sanction(s).

Offline events (Section 5.5.2). Players must follow the rules and regulations of the hosting venue and partner. Players and Coaches may not handle other Players' equipment after a match has started. Requests regarding stage lighting or cameras are granted only if justified, for example where lighting unreasonably impacts a Player's capacity to compete fairly.

Eligibility and legal compliance. Participants must be at least 18 years old (Section 3.3.2) and must obtain any visas, work permits or other authorizations required by local law (Section 3.3.6). Ubisoft, BLAST and the Tournament Organizer are not responsible for a Player's or Team's failure to obtain such documentation (Section 3.3.6).
