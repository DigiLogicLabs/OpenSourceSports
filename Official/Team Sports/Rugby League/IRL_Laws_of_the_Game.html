Section 1: Introduction

Rugby League is a full-contact team sport played between two teams of thirteen players. The sport is governed internationally by International Rugby League (IRL), which maintains the IRL Laws of the Game, the codified rulebook applied to all sanctioned international and domestic competitions that operate under IRL jurisdiction. Domestic elite competitions (such as the NRL in Australasia and Super League in the United Kingdom/France) apply the IRL Laws of the Game as their base framework, with limited, competition-specific variations (e.g., interchange limits, the "six-again" restart) permitted under IRL-sanctioned experimental law variations.

The objective of the game is for a team to score more points than its opponent within the allotted match time, primarily by grounding the ball in the opposing team's in-goal area to score a try, or by kicking the ball between the goalposts and over the crossbar.

A defining structural feature of Rugby League, distinguishing it from Rugby Union, is the tackle count (Rules of Play, "Play-the-Ball" provisions): the attacking team is permitted six tackles in possession before it must relinquish the ball to the opposition, absent a score, error, or infringement.

Section 2: Equipment

Ball (IRL Laws of the Game, Law 2 — The Ball): The ball shall be oval in shape, made of four panels, and inflated with an approved bladder. Approved match specifications are:

  • Length (long axis): 280 mm (11.0 in)
  • Long circumference: 740–770 mm (29.1–30.3 in)
  • Short circumference: 580–600 mm (22.8–23.6 in)
  • Weight: 383–440 g (13.5–15.5 oz)
  • Air pressure: approximately 0.67–0.70 kg/cm² (9.5–10.0 psi)

Playing attire: Each player must wear a numbered jersey, shorts, socks, and boots. Studs (cleats) fitted to boots must comply with approved safety standards regarding length and construction (no sharp edges or exposed metal). Players may wear approved protective equipment, including scrum caps, shoulder padding, and mouthguards, provided such equipment does not present a danger to any player and is approved under IRL equipment regulations.

Prohibited equipment: Items that could cause injury to another player — including jewelry, exposed hard braces, and unpadded rigid supports — are not permitted to be worn during play, per the IRL Laws of the Game equipment and player safety provisions.

Section 3: Playing Area

Field of Play (IRL Laws of the Game, Law 1 — The Field of Play): The playing field, including in-goal areas, must not exceed 122 m (133 yd) in length and 68 m (74 yd) in width. The field of play itself (between the two goal lines) is a maximum of 100 m (109 yd) long. Each in-goal area (the scoring area behind each goal line) must be between 6 m and 11 m (6.5–12 yd) deep.

Markings: The field is marked with a halfway line, 10-metre lines on either side of the halfway line, and additional lines at 10-metre intervals across the field, together with a broken line at the 40-metre mark from each goal line, used to indicate distances for restarts such as the 20-metre restart and defensive line positioning.

Goalposts: Goalposts are positioned on each goal line, comprising two uprights not less than 6 m high, spaced 5.5 m (5 yd 8 in) apart, joined by a horizontal crossbar set at a height of 3 m (9 ft 10 in) above the ground, per the IRL Laws of the Game goalpost specifications.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Team composition (IRL Laws of the Game, Law 3 — Players): Each team consists of thirteen players on the field at any time, comprising six forwards and seven backs, with standard numbered positions: fullback (1), wingers (2, 5), centres (3, 4), five-eighth/stand-off (6), halfback/scrum-half (7), props (8, 10), hooker (9), second-row forwards (11, 12), and loose forward/lock (13).

Interchange: Teams are permitted a nominated number of substitute (interchange) players on the bench, with a limited number of interchanges permitted during a match; the exact number of nominated substitutes and permitted interchange count is set by competition regulations operating under the IRL Laws of the Game framework (commonly four substitutes with a fixed interchange limit at elite level).

Match officials: A match is controlled by a referee, who has sole authority to interpret and apply the Laws of the Game during play. The referee is assisted by two touch judges (assistant referees), responsible primarily for signaling the ball or player going into touch and for assisting on foul play and try-scoring decisions. Elite competitions additionally employ a video referee/Bunker official, empowered to review try-scoring and other nominated decisions using video replay, per IRL-sanctioned video referee protocols.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Match duration: A standard match is played over two halves of 40 minutes each, with an interval not exceeding 10 minutes, per the IRL Laws of the Game match duration provisions. The referee has discretion to allow time for injury and other stoppages ("time-off").

The tackle and play-the-ball: When a ball-carrier is held by one or more defenders such that the ball-carrier's progress is stopped and the tackler(s) retain effective contact, a tackle is deemed complete. The tackled player must release the ball and play it under their control by rolling the ball backward with the foot to a teammate (the "play-the-ball"), per the Laws of the Game tackle and play-the-ball provisions. The attacking team retains possession through six such tackles; if a try is not scored and the ball is not turned over by error or infringement, possession transfers to the opposition after the sixth tackle via handover.

Offside: At a play-the-ball, defending players (other than the two acting defenders nearest the tackle, known as "marker" positions) must retire at least 10 metres from the play-the-ball until the ball is played, per the Laws of the Game offside provisions. Offside at the play-the-ball or in general play results in a penalty to the non-offending team.

The scrum: Following certain stoppages (e.g., a knock-on or forward pass), a scrum is formed with six forwards from each team bound together in three rows, with the hooker of the team feeding the ball striking to win possession, per the Laws of the Game scrum provisions.

Knock-on and forward pass: A knock-on occurs when a player loses control of the ball and it travels forward, striking the ground or another player before being recovered. A forward pass occurs when the ball is passed by hand in a direction toward the opposing goal line. Both result in a scrum (or, in some competitions, a goal-line drop-out or handover depending on field position) to the non-offending team.

Touch and 20-metre restart: When the ball or ball-carrier crosses the touchline, play restarts with a scrum unless a kick has traveled directly into touch from outside the kicking team's own 40-metre area, in which case field position is lost to the mark of the kick, per the Laws of the Game kicking-into-touch provisions.

Section 6: Scoring

Per the IRL Laws of the Game, Law on Method of Scoring, points are awarded as follows:

  • Try: 4 points — awarded when an attacking player grounds the ball with downward pressure in the opposing team's in-goal area, or on/behind the goal line, in accordance with the Laws' definition of "touching down."
  • Conversion (goal kick): 2 points — awarded when, following a try, a place kick or drop kick taken in line with the point the try was scored passes over the crossbar and between the goalposts.
  • Penalty goal: 2 points — awarded when a team elects to kick at goal from a penalty award and the kick passes over the crossbar and between the goalposts.
  • Drop goal (field goal): 1 point — awarded when a player, during open play, drop-kicks the ball over the crossbar and between the goalposts.

The team scoring the greater number of points at the conclusion of the match is declared the winner; if points are equal, the match is declared a draw unless competition-specific extra-time or golden-point provisions apply.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Penalty: Awarded to the non-offending team for infringements including offside, obstruction, dissent, ruck/tackle infringements (e.g., not releasing the tackled player, early engagement at marker), and technical scrum infringements, per the IRL Laws of the Game disciplinary provisions. From a penalty, the non-offending team may elect to tap and run, kick for touch, or attempt a shot at goal for 2 points.

Sin bin: For professional foul, repeated infringement, or dissent, the referee may order a player from the field for a period (commonly 10 minutes), during which the offending team plays with one fewer player, per the Laws of the Game disciplinary sanctions.

Sending off (dismissal): For serious foul play, dangerous contact, or foul and abusive conduct, the referee may permanently dismiss a player from the match, with the offending team playing the remainder of the match with one fewer player and the player subject to further review under IRL/competition judiciary processes.

Foul play: High tackles (contact to the head or neck), tackles without arms, dangerous throws (tip/spear tackles lifting a player past the horizontal and dropping them on the head/neck/upper body), striking, and other reckless or dangerous contact are prohibited under the Laws of the Game's foul play and player safety provisions, and are sanctioned by penalty, sin bin, or sending off depending on severity and the on-field or video referee's assessment.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

IRL's Laws of the Game place significant emphasis on player welfare through specific tackle-technique provisions. Tackles making direct contact with the head or neck ("high tackles") are prohibited regardless of intent, with sanctions scaled to the degree of force and whether contact was avoidable, consistent with IRL's head-contact policy and category-based sanctioning framework applied across sanctioned competitions.

Head Injury Assessment (HIA): Competitions operating under IRL jurisdiction apply concussion-management protocols requiring any player suspected of sustaining a head injury to be removed from play for a Head Injury Assessment conducted by an independent or team medical officer before being permitted to return, in line with IRL concussion guidelines.

Dangerous throws: Tackles that lift a player into a position where their head or upper body is driven or dropped toward the ground (commonly termed "tip" or "spear" tackles) are explicitly prohibited under the Laws of the Game as a dangerous throw, carrying mandatory referral for on-field or post-match sanction.

Duty of care: Match officials are directed under the Laws of the Game to prioritize player safety in all interpretations, with authority to stop play, issue medical time-outs, and apply sanctions proactively where continued play would expose players to unreasonable risk of injury.
