Section 1: Introduction

The PlayStation NBA Creator Cup is an NBA-branded basketball exhibition built around social-media creators and digital influencers rather than professional or collegiate rosters. The property has run under NBA branding across multiple annual stagings; the sixth staging was folded, for the first time, directly into the official 2026 NBA Summer League opening-night schedule at The Pavilion at UNLV in Las Vegas on July 9, 2026, per the NBA.com report "The PlayStation NBA Creator Cup Comes to Summer League." A separate "Season 2" staging of the Creator Cup also ran at NBA All-Star weekend on Creator Court inside NBA Crossover at the Los Angeles Convention Center, fielding four creator squads in a traditional 3-on-3 tournament format.

Creator Basketball is a distinct sport-row from the parent Basketball (NBA) entry — the underlying game is basketball, but the format, roster construction, and in-game mechanics diverge from standard NBA play. This entry documents the sixth-edition Summer League staging, which is the current PlayStation-presented format as of the 2026 event; where a rule could not be independently confirmed against NBA or syndicated NBA reporting, it has been left unstated rather than guessed.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Game Structure (Sixth Edition, 2026 Summer League)

  • Per "The PlayStation NBA Creator Cup Comes to Summer League" (NBA.com), the game is played across three eight-minute levels rather than standard NBA quarters.
  • A two-minute "Turbo Mode" segment is built into the levels, described in NBA.com and syndicated reporting as accelerating the pace of play and introducing bonus scoring opportunities; the exact scoring-bonus formula for Turbo Mode has not been published in confirmable detail and is not invented here.
  • After the three regulation levels conclude, the clock is turned off and the game moves to an untimed target-score finish: the winning score is set at 11 points above the leading team's total at the end of the third level, and the first team to reach that target wins. In the 2026 staging, this format produced a 90–69 result for Team Darrick over Team Pierre.

Shooting Fouls — One Free Throw Rule

Per the "2026 NBA Summer League Format Summary & Playing Rules" published on NBA.com, the 2026 Summer League slate — which the Creator Cup's sixth edition was formally folded into as part of opening night — uses a "One Free Throw Rule" on shooting fouls: rather than awarding the traditional one, two, or three free-throw attempts, the shooter takes a single free throw worth the same total point value as the shooting foul it replaces (e.g., a foul on a 2-point attempt is worth two points on a single made free throw; a foul on a 3-point attempt is worth three points on a single made free throw). This rule was adapted from a G League procedure used since the 2019–20 season. Under the general Summer League version of the rule, standard multi-shot free-throw procedure returns in the final two minutes of regulation and in overtime; because the Creator Cup's sixth-edition format uses untimed levels and a target-score finish rather than quarters and overtime, it is not confirmed whether that specific carve-out applies in the same form to Creator Cup games, and no assumption is made here.
