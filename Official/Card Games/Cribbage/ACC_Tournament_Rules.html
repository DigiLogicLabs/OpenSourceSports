Section 1: Introduction

Cribbage is a two-player card game in which score is tracked by pegging holes around a board rather than on paper, and the dealer receives an extra hand — the crib — built from cards both players discard.

In sanctioned play the game is governed by the American Cribbage Congress (ACC), founded in 1980, which publishes the tournament rules and certifies judges to settle disputes at sanctioned events. This page follows the 2020 edition, effective 1 August 2020.

  • A judge is a person authorised by the ACC and appointed by the tournament director to answer questions and settle disagreements relating to the rules of play (Rule 1.6).
  • The playing sequence — mix, cut, deal, discard to the crib, cut the starter, play, then count — is set out in full as an outline (Rule 1.5).
  • Point values for every combination are collected in a single scoring chart (Rule 1.7).

Section 2: The Deck

  • Cribbage uses a standard pack; the composition and ranking of the cards are specified by the rules (Rule 1.3).
  • The game is for two players in the standard tournament form (Rule 1.2).
  • The cribbage board is the scoring device, and its layout is defined by the rules (Rule 1.4).
  • The board is divided into streets of thirty holes each: first street is holes 1–30, second 31–60, third 61–90, and fourth 91–120 (Rule 1.6).
  • Hole number 90 is the stink hole — one point short of avoiding a skunk (Rule 1.6).
  • The starter card is the card cut and placed on the remainder of the pack, used in counting the value of each hand and the crib (Rule 1.6).
  • A hand means either the six cards dealt to each player, or the four remaining after discarding to the crib (Rule 1.6).

Section 3: Setup & Deal

  • The cards must be properly mixed before each deal (Rule 2.1).
  • The pone — the opponent of the dealer (Rule 1.6) — has the right to shuffle once (Rule 2.2), and the dealer has the right to shuffle last (Rule 2.3).
  • The pack is cut by a defined procedure (Rule 3.1), and the cut determines who wins the deal (Rule 3.2).
  • The pack is cut again before each deal (Rule 3.3).
  • Dealing follows a set order (Rule 4.1) and a set method (Rule 4.2).
  • Each player then discards to the crib, whose placement is specified (Rule 5.1); the crib must not be exposed (Rule 5.2).
  • Exposed cards during the deal are handled by rule (Rule 4.3), as is an incorrect number of cards in a hand or the crib (Rule 4.4).
  • An imperfect pack is addressed separately (Rule 4.5).
  • The starter card is then cut (Rule 6.1), and the deck is placed as directed afterwards (Rule 6.4).
  • If the starter is a Jack, the dealer scores two points — "his heels" (Rule 6.3, Rule 1.6).
  • Exposing more than one card on the starter cut is covered by rule (Rule 6.2).

Section 4: Gameplay

After the starter is cut, the players alternate laying cards face up, announcing the running count. This phase is the play, or pegging.

  • The order of play is fixed by rule, beginning with the pone (Rule 7.1).
  • Points earned during the play are scored as they occur (Rule 7.2), using the values in the scoring chart (Rule 1.7).
  • A player who cannot lay a card without exceeding a cumulative count of 31 calls "go" (Rule 1.6, Rule 1.5).
  • Failing to play a card when able is a renege, which carries a penalty (Rule 7.3).
  • Incorrect announcements during the play are handled by rule (Rule 7.4).
  • Points are recorded by pegging on the board (Rule 8.1).
  • A hand or crib worth nothing is pegged as a zero (Rule 8.2).
  • Pegging incorrectly to a player's disadvantage (Rule 8.3) and to their advantage (Rule 8.4) are treated as separate offences with different remedies.

Section 5: Scoring & Winning

Every combination and its value is listed in a single chart, which is the authority for counting both during the play and in the hands (Rule 1.7).

  • A Jack turned by the dealer as the starter card scores 2 points (Rule 1.7, Rule 6.3).
  • A Jack held in hand or crib of the same suit as the starter card scores 1 point (Rule 1.7).
  • Two of a kind — a pair — scores 2 points, both during the play and in the hand or crib (Rule 1.7).
  • A straight (or run) is a sequence of three or more consecutive cards, which during the play may be made in any order (Rule 1.6).
  • Counting order and the pegging of each count are governed by the pegging rules (Rule 8.1).

Winning, Skunks and the Stink Hole

  • To win by 31 or more points is a skunk (Rule 1.6).
  • The stink hole is hole 90, one point short of avoiding a skunk (Rule 1.6).
  • The board's fourth street runs from hole 91 to hole 120 (Rule 1.6).
  • Match structure, including playoff brackets and byes, is defined for sanctioned events (Rule 13.2).

Section 6: Variants & House Rules

The ACC rules above govern sanctioned tournament play. Casual cribbage varies, and the differences are worth naming because they change the game materially:

  • Three- and four-handed cribbage: played outside the standard two-player tournament form defined by the rules (Rule 1.2).
  • Five-card cribbage: the older English game, played to 61 with a different discard count — not the ACC form.
  • Muggins: an opponent may claim points you failed to count for yourself. A house convention, not part of standard sanctioned play.
  • Casual scoring: informal games often ignore skunk and stink-hole conventions entirely, though both are defined terms in the rules (Rule 1.6).
