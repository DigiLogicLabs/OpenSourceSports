Section 1: Introduction

1.1 Overview and History

Snowboarding debuted as an Olympic sport at the 1998 Nagano Winter Games with Giant Slalom and Halfpipe events. Governed by FIS (International Ski and Snowboard Federation) under ICR Book VI — Snowboard, the sport has expanded steadily in the Olympic programme. Snowboard Cross was added in 2006 (Turin), Slopestyle and Parallel Giant Slalom in 2014 (Sochi), and Big Air in 2018 (PyeongChang). The sport bridges athletic competition with progressive action-sport culture, featuring both judged freestyle events and timed racing disciplines.

1.2 Olympic Disciplines

The current Olympic programme includes five snowboard disciplines, each with men's and women's events:

  • Halfpipe: Athletes perform aerial tricks on the walls of a carved superpipe.
  • Slopestyle: Athletes navigate a course of rails, boxes, and jumps, selecting their own line and trick combinations.
  • Big Air: A single massive jump where athletes perform their most technically demanding tricks.
  • Snowboard Cross (SBX): Head-to-head racing over a course featuring banked turns, rollers, and jumps.
  • Parallel Giant Slalom (PGS): Side-by-side gate racing on carved courses with time-based elimination.

1.3 Stance and Riding Direction

Snowboarders ride with either their left foot forward ("regular") or right foot forward ("goofy"). Both stances are equally valid, and many tricks require riding "switch" (opposite to the athlete's natural stance), which increases the trick's difficulty and scoring potential.

Section 2: Equipment

2.1 Snowboard Specifications

  • Halfpipe / Slopestyle / Big Air: No minimum board length. Athletes choose boards suited to their height, weight, and riding style. Typical lengths range from 148–162 cm. Boards are twin-tipped (symmetrical nose and tail) for switch riding.
  • Snowboard Cross: No minimum length. Boards are directional (longer nose, stiffer flex) for high-speed stability. Typical lengths 157–165 cm.
  • Parallel Giant Slalom: Minimum 163 cm (64.2 in) for men, 153 cm (60.2 in) for women. Boards are narrow, stiff, and heavily side-cut for aggressive carved turns. Hardboot (plate) binding systems are standard.

2.2 Bindings

  • Freestyle events (Halfpipe, Slopestyle, Big Air): Soft-boot highback bindings with strap-in or rear-entry systems. The binding must securely retain the boot and allow quick release in emergencies.
  • SBX: Soft-boot bindings similar to freestyle but often with stiffer highbacks for responsiveness.
  • PGS: Hardboot plate bindings that clip into rigid plastic boots, similar to alpine ski bindings. These provide maximum edge-to-edge power transfer.
  • Leash: A safety leash connecting the front binding to the rider's lead leg is mandatory in all disciplines to prevent a runaway board on the hill.

2.3 Protective Equipment

  • Helmet: Crash helmets are compulsory for all FIS snowboard events and must be worn according to the manufacturer's recommendations (ICR 2012.6). Must conform to EN 1077 or equivalent. Full-face helmets permitted in SBX and PGS.
  • Back / impact protector: Mandatory for Halfpipe and Slopestyle at World Cup level. Strongly recommended for all other disciplines. Must meet EN 1621-2 standard.
  • Wrist guards: Recommended for all athletes. Wrist fractures are the most common snowboard injury.
  • Goggles: Impact-rated ski/snowboard goggles required. Lens tint must be appropriate for lighting conditions.
  • Shin guards: Recommended for Slopestyle athletes to protect against rail impacts.

Section 3: Playing Area

3.1 Halfpipe

  • Size: Level A competitions (OWG, WSC, WJC, WC, YOG) use a 22 ft / 6.7 m pipe; Level B uses 18 ft and Level C 15 ft (ICR 3102.2.1).
  • Length: A 22 ft pipe must have at least 160 m of rideable length, 170 m recommended (ICR 3102.2.1).
  • Width: At least 19.0 m from coping to coping on a 22 ft pipe, with 19.0–22.0 m recommended (ICR 3102.2.1).
  • Wall height: 6.7 m from the bottom of the pipe to the coping on a 22 ft pipe (ICR 3102.2.1). The standard "22-foot superpipe" refers to this wall height.
  • Gradient: At least 17° along the centre line of a 22 ft pipe, with 18° recommended (ICR 3102.2.1).

3.2 Slopestyle Course

  • Vertical drop: At least 150 m at Level A, 80 m at Level B and 50 m at Level C, with an average gradient of 10° or steeper (ICR 3102.3.1).
  • Width: 30 m slope width (ICR 3102.3.1).
  • Features: The course must have at least two different feature types (table top jumps, fun boxes, quarter pipes, waves/jumps, rails and ridges, or others). A Level A course needs at least 3 jumps and 6 judged hits, Level B at least 2 jumps and 4 judged hits, and Level C at least 1 jump and 3 judged hits (ICR 3102.3.3).
  • Multiple lines: The course offers a variety of hits, jumps, rails and other features, with two or more lines that competitors may choose between (ICR 3102.3).

3.3 Big Air

  • Kicker: At least 2.0 m high, with a take-off angle of at least 25° and a width of at least 5.0 m (ICR 3102.4.1).
  • Landing: Take-off to landing of at least 15 m at Level A and B (10 m otherwise), a landing gradient of at least 28°, and a landing at least 20 m wide and 20 m long (ICR 3102.4.1).
  • In-run: The drop-in ramp is at least 30 m long, at least 5 m wide and inclined at least 20°. It lets competitors adjust their speed for the jump (ICR 3102.4.1, 3102.4.2).

3.4 Snowboard Cross (SBX)

  • Length: 800–1,300 m at Level A, at least 600 m at Level B and at least 450 m at Level C (ICR 5102.1.1).
  • Vertical drop: 100–250 m at Level A (ICR 5102.1.1).
  • Average course angle: 7°–11° (about 12–20%) at Level A (ICR 5102.1.1).
  • Features: Berms, rollers, jumps and other features, in a course that creates overtaking opportunities from start to finish during heats of 4 or 6 competitors (ICR 5102.1.2.1).
  • Gates: Each gate is a stubby flex turning pole and a long rigid outside pole joined by a triangular gate flag. Gate flags must be in two different colors (ICR 5203, 5203.1).

3.5 Parallel Giant Slalom (PGS)

  • Course: 400–700 m long with 100–200 m of vertical drop and an average angle of 16° (+/- 2°) (ICR 6102.2).
  • Gates: About 22–26 gates are recommended, set 20–25 m apart turning pole to turning pole (22–24 m recommended). The two courses are set 9–12 m apart (ICR 6102.2, 6104.3.2, 6104.3.3).
  • Course preparation: Course setting, terrain and snow preparation on the two courses are to be as identical as possible, with the same profile and difficulties (ICR 6104.3, 6104.3.1.1).

Section 4: Players & Officials

4.1 Competitors by Discipline

  • Halfpipe / Slopestyle / Big Air: Individual runs in heats. The standard World Cup and major-event final has 10–12 men and 6–12 women (ICR 3501.2).
  • Snowboard Cross: Knockout heats of 4 or 6 riders (ICR 5502.1). Qualification can be timed runs, seeding runs, qualification heat rounds, 3-heats qualification or round robin (ICR 5501).
  • PGS: Head-to-head elimination bracket after a two-run qualification. The final runs from the 1/8-finals through the 1/4-finals and 1/2-finals to the small and big finals (ICR 6504.1, 6504.3).

4.2 Judging Panels

  • Halfpipe / Slopestyle / Big Air: At World Cups there are at least 6 scoring judges plus a Head Judge (ICR 3311.7.2), and 6–9 scoring judges plus a Head Judge at the Olympics and World Championships (ICR 3311.7.1). Each judge scores 0–100 without decimals (ICR 3403). With 6 or more scoring judges the high and low scores are discarded and the rest count (ICR 3403.2).
  • SBX and PGS: No judges — results are decided by finish order (SBX) or by racing times (PGS).

4.3 Officials

The competition is run by a Jury that includes an FIS Technical Delegate (TD). A Chief of Competition manages event logistics. Gate or section judges (SBX, PGS), start referees and video officials support the Jury. At the OWG, WSC, WC, YOG and WJC, video judging/control is used for cross competitions (ICR 5408) and is mandatory for snowboard alpine competitions (ICR 6406).

Section 5: Rules of Play

5.1 Halfpipe

Competitions run in heats through Qualification, an optional Semifinal and a Final (ICR 3501). For a phase to count as a result, at least 2 runs must be completed (ICR 3502). The Jury chooses the format: "best of 2" or "best of x" (3 or more runs), ranked on the best single score (ICR 3502.1, 3502.2). A typical run lasts 25–45 seconds and includes 5–7 "hits" (tricks performed on the pipe walls). Athletes drop into the pipe from the deck and alternate between the frontside and backside walls. Judges evaluate execution, difficulty, amplitude, variety and progression (ICR 3402).

5.2 Slopestyle

The same heat and run formats as halfpipe apply: at least 2 runs per phase, ranked on the best score (ICR 3502). Athletes choose their own line through the course, selecting which features to hit and in what order. A balanced run demonstrates mastery on both jib features and jumps. Judges score overall impression on a 0–100 scale (ICR 3403, 3403.2). Alternatively, section-by-section judging splits the panel into trick and composition judges (ICR 3403.3).

5.3 Big Air

Big Air can use a "best 2 out of 3" format in which the two counting tricks must be different (ICR 3502.3.1). At the Team Captains' meeting the Jury chooses which variety version applies. In the Clockwise / Counterclockwise version, clockwise and counterclockwise rotations count as different tricks. In the 2 of 4 Directions version, snowboard tricks are classed as frontside, backside, switch backside and switch frontside (ICR 3502.3.1). This rule prevents athletes from simply repeating their best trick and rewards versatility.

5.4 Snowboard Cross (SBX)

A qualification phase is followed by a knockout final (ICR 5500.1). Finals are run in 4-person or 6-person heats. The top 2 (4 per heat) or top 3 (6 per heat) advance from phase to phase (ICR 5502.1). Rank in each heat is decided by the first part of the body or the snowboard to cross the finish line (ICR 5407.1.1). Contact is common in snowboard cross, but the Jury sanctions interference such as pulling, pushing or blocking with hand or arm, contact from the side or behind, and line deviation (ICR 5404.1, 5404.1.2). A rider who does not finish but is ranked in the top 2 (4-person heats) or top 3 (6-person heats) still advances (ICR 5407.3.1).

5.5 Parallel Giant Slalom (PGS)

Qualification has two runs. In the first run odd numbers ride the red course and even numbers the blue course, and the top 16 per course advance. Riders swap courses for the second run, and the total time of both runs decides the top 16 for the finals (ICR 6504.1.1, 6504.1.2). In a two-run knockout final, each pair races twice, switching courses after the first run. The time differences of the two runs are added, each run capped at the penalty time (4% of the best qualification time, max. 1.5 s), and the rider with the lower total advances (ICR 6504.3.2, 6504.3.2.3). A single-run knockout format may be used instead (ICR 6504.3.3). The bracket runs through the 1/8-finals, 1/4-finals, 1/2-finals, and small and big finals (ICR 6504.3).

Section 6: Scoring

6.1 Judged Events (Halfpipe, Slopestyle, Big Air)

Each judge scores each run 0–100 without decimals. Any calculated score is truncated to two decimal places (ICR 3403). In the overall impression system, if there are 6 or more scoring judges the high and low scores are discarded and the remaining scores count (ICR 3403.2). The judging criteria are (ICR 3402):

  • Difficulty: Complexity of tricks — number of rotations, inversions, and grabs. A 1440° (4 full rotations) scores higher than a 1080° (3 rotations).
  • Execution: Clean takeoffs, controlled flight, solid landings (no hand drags, reverts, or wobbles). Grab quality — tweaked, held, and stylish versus quick or sloppy.
  • Amplitude: Height achieved above the lip (halfpipe) or gap cleared (slopestyle/big air). Greater height demonstrates control and power.
  • Variety: Mixing frontside and backside rotations, different grab types, regular and switch takeoffs. Repetition is penalized.
  • Progression: Innovation — performing tricks new to competition or increasing difficulty from previous runs.

6.2 Big Air Multi-Run Scoring

In the best 2 out of 3 format, the two counting tricks must be different. If a competitor performs the same type of trick two or more times in the same phase, only the highest-scored one counts. If only two jumps are performed in a phase, the highest individual score counts (ICR 3502.3.1).

6.3 Race Events (SBX, PGS)

SBX: finish order in each heat decides who advances — no scoring (ICR 5407.1.1, 5502.1). PGS: in two-run finals the time differences of the two runs are added, each capped at the penalty time (ICR 6504.3.2).

6.4 Tiebreaking

In judged events, tied competitors receive the same rank. The competitor with the higher start number is listed first (ICR 3503). If riders tie for the last qualifying place, all of them advance (ICR 3503.1, 3503.2). In SBX, an undecidable finish before the small and big finals is broken by qualification rank (ICR 5407.2.1). Ties in the small or big final remain tied (ICR 5407.2.2). In PGS two-run finals, if the summed time differences are tied, the winner of the second run advances (ICR 6504.3.2).

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

7.1 Snowboard Cross Contact Rules

  • Official warning (WRG): For an intentional action with involuntary interference, or an unintentional action with accidental interference, where the consequence is not severe (ICR 5404.2.1.3). A second warning in the same competition becomes a Yellow Card (ICR 5404.3.1).
  • RAL: For an unintentional action causing accidental interference with severe consequences. The rider is ranked last in the heat and of the round (ICR 5404.2.1.4).
  • Yellow card (RAL): For an intentional action with intentional or involuntary interference where the consequence is not severe. The rider is ranked last in their heat and relegated to the last position of the round, and the card stays for the season in that category (ICR 5404.2.1.5). Two yellow cards in a season mean suspension from the next competition in the same category (ICR 5404.3.2).
  • Red card (DSQ): For intentional action and intentional interference with severe consequences. The rider is disqualified from the whole competition, listed as DSQ without a ranking, and suspended from the next competition in that category (ICR 5404.2.1.6).
  • Review: The Jury may use section judges' recommendations and video evidence (ICR 5404.1). A post-race panel may mitigate a sanction, but the result and ranking stay unchanged (ICR 5404.3.5).

7.2 Gate Violations

  • SBX: A rider who does not pass a gate correctly receives a DNF and may not continue through further gates (ICR 5401.3).
  • PGS: A gate is passed correctly when at least the front foot is fixed in the bindings and the entire board crosses the gate line outside the turning pole (ICR 6401.1). In a two-run final, a rider disqualified or not finishing in the first run starts the second run with the penalty time. A rider disqualified or not finishing in the second run is eliminated (ICR 6504.3.2.4).

7.3 Equipment Violations

The Jury may disqualify or sanction a competitor who does not wear an acceptable helmet or the official start number, or who violates any FIS equipment rule (ICR 2025.4). Boards shorter than the minimum (PGS) and missing leashes are equipment violations.

7.4 General Infractions

  • Not being ready to start on time, or making a false start, can be sanctioned by the Jury (ICR 2024.5, 2024.6).
  • Unsportsmanlike conduct or verbal abuse: yellow card, disqualification, or suspension at the jury's discretion.
  • Doping: per FIS Anti-Doping Rules and WADA Code. Violations result in disqualification and suspension.
  • Protests: Protests must be submitted in writing with a CHF 100 deposit, which is returned if the protest is upheld (ICR 2026.4.1, 2026.4.4). Jury decisions on SBX in-race interference cannot be protested or appealed (ICR 5613).

Section 8: Safety Considerations

8.1 Course Approval

The Jury must formally approve the course before the first Official Training. A Jury member inspects the course before each day's training and periodically during the competition (ICR 3605).

8.2 Snow and Course Conditions

  • The halfpipe must have compact, consistent snow in the walls and be ready for training at least one day before the first scheduled session (ICR 3102.2.2).
  • Park and pipe courses must be entirely closed off by barriers. In critical places, the Jury may require safety installations such as mattresses, pads and nets (ICR 3102.1).
  • SBX courses must be entirely closed off by barriers and secured with suitable safety material in agreement with the Jury (ICR 5102.2.1, 5102.2.2).

8.3 Athlete Course Inspection

Before the first official training on each competition day, a course inspection is held for coaches and competitors, sliding down the course without riding the features. Teams can then raise concerns with the Jury (ICR 3606).

8.4 Night Events and Artificial Lighting

  • Big Air and Halfpipe events are frequently held under artificial lighting for spectator appeal and broadcast scheduling. The light level anywhere on the course may not be less than 80 lux (ICR 2034.2.1).

8.5 Anti-Doping

FIS enforces anti-doping rules per the WADA Code. In-competition and out-of-competition testing is conducted at all World Cup, World Championship, and Olympic snowboard events. Athletes in the FIS Registered Testing Pool must provide quarterly whereabouts information. Violations result in provisional suspension, a formal hearing, and potential multi-year bans with disqualification of results.
