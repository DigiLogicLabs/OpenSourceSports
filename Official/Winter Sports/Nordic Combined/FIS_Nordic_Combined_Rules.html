Section 1: Introduction

1.1 Overview and Governing Body

Nordic combined has been part of the Olympic program since the inaugural Winter Games at Chamonix in 1924, making it one of the oldest winter Olympic disciplines. Governed by FIS under ICR Book VII (Nordic Combined), the sport uniquely demands that athletes excel in two fundamentally different physical disciplines: the explosive power and aerial technique of ski jumping, and the sustained aerobic endurance of cross-country skiing.

1.2 History and Olympic Evolution

Originally a single event (18 km cross-country + jumping), Nordic combined has expanded over the decades. The Individual Normal Hill/10 km was joined by the Team event (1988) and the Individual Large Hill/10 km (2002, replacing the original Sprint format). The Gundersen method, introduced in the 1980s, revolutionized the sport by converting jumping points into time gaps, creating dramatic pursuit-style cross-country finishes where the first athlete across the line wins.

1.3 Women's Nordic Combined

Women’s Nordic combined has been contested at FIS World Championships since 2021. At World Ski Championships women contest Individual competitions on the normal and large hill and a Team/Team-Sprint, plus the Mixed Team (ICR 528.2); the Olympic program in ICR 528.1 lists men’s events only. The growth of women’s ski jumping (Olympic since 2014) provided the foundation for expanding combined disciplines to women.

1.4 Olympic Events

  • Individual Gundersen Normal Hill / 10 km (men): One competition jump from the normal hill, followed by a 10 km cross-country race (ICR 528.1)
  • Individual Gundersen Large Hill / 10 km (men): One competition jump from the large hill, followed by a 10 km cross-country race (ICR 528.1)
  • Team-Sprint Normal Hill / 2×7.5 km (men): Team ski jumping, then a 2×7.5 km cross-country race in which each athlete skis 5×1.5 km alternately (ICR 528.1, 544.1)

Section 2: Equipment

2.1 Ski Jumping Equipment

  • Jumping skis: Maximum length 145% of body height, adjusted by BMI (lower BMI = shorter maximum ski length, per FIS BMI-to-ski-length table). Minimum ski width 95 mm at the binding. Maximum weight per ski not to exceed half the ski length in grams (e.g., max 1350 g for a 270 cm ski).
  • Jumping suit: Maximum material thickness 6 mm (0.24 in). Air permeability minimum 40 l/m²/s (tested by FIS gauge). No inflation or aerodynamic aids. Suit must be measured and stamped by FIS equipment controllers before competition.
  • Jumping boots: Maximum sole height 45 mm at the heel; boot must be compatible with the approved binding system
  • Helmet: FIS-approved helmet mandatory for the jumping phase; must meet EN 1077 or equivalent certification
  • Goggles: Recommended during the jumping phase; must not provide aerodynamic advantage

2.2 Cross-Country Equipment

  • Skating skis: Free technique is standard for the cross-country phase. Minimum length = athlete’s height minus 10 cm. Skis must comply with all FIS cross-country equipment rules.
  • Skating poles: Maximum length 100% of body height. Pole baskets maximum 100 cm².
  • Skating boots: High-cuff design with ankle support; must be commercially available and FIS-approved
  • Wax: Glide wax only (no kick wax for skating). Fluorinated wax and fluorine-containing tuning products are prohibited for all FIS disciplines and levels (ICR 222.8).
  • Race suit: Standard cross-country race suit; bib number worn during the cross-country phase

2.3 Equipment Transition

  • Athletes carry or transport two complete sets of equipment: jumping gear and cross-country gear
  • Equipment is pre-positioned in the transition area before the jumping phase begins
  • Rapid transition between jumping and skiing equipment is essential; teams practice transition to minimize time
  • Equipment controllers may inspect cross-country skis for fluorocarbon wax during the transition period

Section 3: Playing Area

3.1 Ski Jumping Hill

  • Normal Hill: Hill Size (HS) 85–109 m, with the construction-point distance (w) 75–99 m (ICR 511.1). In-run length, takeoff table angle, and landing slope profile must meet FIS homologation standards.
  • Large Hill: HS 110–149 m (ICR 511.1). Full FIS homologation certificate required.
  • Wind screens: netting or barriers along the in-run and landing hill to reduce crosswind effects
  • Judges’ tower: positioned at the K-point level, perpendicular to the landing slope, housing 5 style judges with clear sightlines
  • Speed measurement at the takeoff edge and distance measurement (video-based or manual)

3.2 Cross-Country Course

  • Individual Gundersen course: 5 to 15 km raced on 2.0 or 2.5 km laps (ICR 512.1). Course terrain one-third uphill, one-third undulating rolling terrain, one-third varied downhills (ICR 512.4).
  • 5 km course (team relay / women): 2.5 km laps; total climb 150–210 m (ICR 512.1, 512.5.3)
  • Course must be FIS-homologated with a valid certificate
  • Course marked with directional arrows, distance markers every 1 km, and competitor information boards

3.3 Transition and Stadium Area

  • Transition area between jumping hill and cross-country start: athletes change from jumping to skiing equipment. Minimum size to accommodate all competitors simultaneously.
  • Stadium area includes: cross-country start zone (staggered start positions), finish area, exchange zone (a rectangle 30 m long for the Team Gundersen relay, ICR 515.2.11), electronic timing mats, and spectator viewing areas
  • Results display board visible from the stadium showing jumping scores and cross-country time gaps
  • Warm-up area adjacent to the cross-country start with groomed snow lanes

Section 4: Players & Officials

4.1 Athlete Qualifications

  • Athletes must hold a valid FIS license and be registered by their national federation
  • Athletes must meet minimum FIS points requirements in both ski jumping and cross-country to qualify
  • Minimum age: at OWG, World Ski Championships, World Cup and Continental Cup, competitors must reach their 16th birthday within the calendar year; 18 applies only to World Cup ski-flying events (ICR 521.1.1)
  • The same athlete must compete in both phases: start right in the cross-country race requires having taken part in the jumping competition round (ICR 540.1)

4.2 Team Composition

  • Individual events: Each athlete competes alone in both phases
  • Team event: 4 athletes per team who must participate in and complete both disciplines: all 4 jump, then race a 4×5 km relay (ICR 543.1, 512.1)
  • Team jumping order and relay leg order are declared by the team coach before competition
  • After the draw, a competitor who cannot start because of force majeure (injury, illness) may be substituted if the Jury permits, certified by a medical doctor and communicated to the Jury until 2 hours before the start (ICR 522.4.2); a team substitute must take the same Team/Team-Sprint position (ICR 522.4.5)

4.3 Officials and Jury

  • Competition jury: FIS Technical Delegate, Chief of Competition, Assistant TD; oversees both phases
  • Jumping officials: 5 style judges, distance measurers, equipment controllers, wind/gate compensation committee
  • Cross-country officials: Timekeepers, technique controllers (not typically needed as free technique is used), course controllers, exchange zone judges (relay)
  • Equipment controllers: Inspect jumping suits, ski lengths, boot heights, and BMI compliance before the jumping phase; inspect cross-country skis for fluorocarbon before/after the skiing phase
  • Separate timing systems for jumping (distance/style) and cross-country (elapsed time); electronic transponders on athletes’ ankles

Section 5: Rules of Play

5.1 Competition Sequence

All Nordic combined events follow the same fundamental sequence: ski jumping first, then cross-country skiing. The jumping phase determines the start time gaps for the cross-country phase using the Gundersen method. This creates a pursuit-style cross-country race where the first athlete across the finish line wins, making the result immediately clear to spectators.

5.2 The Gundersen Method

  • Jumping results (total points from distance + style + wind/gate compensation) are converted into time differences relative to the leader
  • Conversion factor (Individual): On 5–15 km courses, 15 points = 1 minute, so each point difference = 4 seconds (e.g., an athlete 10 points behind the jumping leader starts 40 seconds behind) (ICR 527.4.1)
  • Conversion factor (Team): In the Team Gundersen (4×5 km), 60 points = 1 minute, i.e. 1 second per point (ICR 527.4.1)
  • The winner of the jumping competition starts first; all other athletes start at their converted time gaps, rounded to full seconds (ICR 525.2.1)
  • This system rewards consistency across both disciplines; a strong jumper who is a weak skier can be caught, and vice versa

5.3 Jumping Phase Rules

  • One competition round (one jump per athlete), except the Individual Gundersen 15 km with two scored rounds; one trial round must be included in the program, and using the trial jump is up to each jumper (ICR 525.1.1)
  • Scored identically to standalone ski jumping: distance points + 5 style judges (highest and lowest dropped, 3 remaining scores summed) + wind/gate compensation (ICR 527.2.3.1, 525.1.2)
  • At OWG, WSC, World Cup and Continental Cup, the jumping start order is the reverse of the current Cup standings; athletes without Cup points are drawn in groups before those with points (ICR 523.2.1)
  • Gate and wind compensation: points added or subtracted based on in-run gate position and wind conditions to ensure fairness
  • Telemark landing (one foot ahead of the other) is essential for full style marks; a two-footed landing results in deductions

5.4 Cross-Country Phase Rules

  • The cross-country race is skied in free technique, which includes all cross-country techniques (ICR 527.4.2)
  • Individual Gundersen: 5 to 15 km (10 km at the Olympics) on 2.0 or 2.5 km laps through the stadium (ICR 512.1, 528.1)
  • Team relay: 4×5 km; the exchange is a tap of the hand on any part of the next competitor’s body while both are in the 30 m exchange zone (ICR 525.2.17, 515.2.11)
  • At OWG, WSC, World Cup and Grand Prix, competitors who have been lapped are pulled from the race and ranked at the place where they were removed (ICR 525.2.3)

5.5 Compact Format (World Cup)

FIS has introduced compact formats for World Cup events to increase spectator appeal. The Compact Individual format uses a 5 or 7.5 km cross-country race (ICR 512.1) whose starts follow a fixed interval table rather than a point conversion: the jumping winner starts at 0:00, second at 0:06, third at 0:12, and so on (ICR 541.1.4). Mass Start events reverse the order, with a mass-start cross-country race first and one round of jumping second; the race winner receives zero points and the rest of the field minus points under the conversion table (ICR 542.1, 542.2.1). These formats are used at select World Cup events but not yet at the Olympics.

Section 6: Scoring

6.1 Jumping Score Calculation

  • Distance points: The meter value depends on the K-point: 2.0 points/m for K 80–99 m, 1.8 for K 100–134 m, 1.6 for K 135–164 m. Landing at the K-point = 60 points (ICR 527.2.3.2).
  • Style points: 5 judges each award 0–20 points based on flight form, body position, landing technique, and outrun. Highest and lowest scores are discarded; remaining 3 are summed (maximum 60 style points) (ICR 527.2.3.1).
  • Wind compensation: Points added for headwind (disadvantage), subtracted for tailwind (advantage). Calculated from real-time anemometer data on the hill.
  • Gate compensation: Points added if the in-run gate is lowered (shorter in-run = less speed), subtracted if raised. Jury adjusts the gate for safety based on conditions.
  • Total jumping score: Distance + Style + Wind/Gate compensation

6.2 Final Results

  • In Gundersen competitions, final ranking is determined by cross-country finish order, confirmed by the photo finish system (ICR 514.3)
  • The first athlete to cross the finish line wins, regardless of their jumping score
  • Jumping scores serve solely to determine the cross-country start time gaps via the Gundersen method
  • In case of identical finish times: the photo finish video or the finish referee decides; if no difference can be seen, the athletes share the rank (ICR 540.2)

6.3 World Cup Points

  • World Cup points awarded for top 30 finishers: 100-80-60-50-45-40-36-32-29-26-24-22-20-18-16-15-14-13-12-11, then 10 through 1 for places 21–30
  • Overall World Cup standings determine the season champion; separate standings for individual and team events
  • Nations Cup standings determine team quotas for the following season’s World Cup

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

7.1 Jumping Phase Violations

  • Equipment non-compliance: Suit thickness, air permeability, ski length, or boot height outside FIS limits — DSQ from the entire competition (both phases)
  • BMI manipulation: If an athlete’s BMI is found to be artificially altered (e.g., wearing weights), DSQ and potential FIS disciplinary action
  • Improper landing: Two-footed, hand-touch, or fall landings incur style deductions but are not violations per se; falls may trigger safety holds
  • False start: Starting before the official starting signal, or intentionally delaying readiness, as a rule causes disqualification (ICR 525.1.12, 531.7.6)

7.2 Cross-Country Phase Violations

  • Obstruction: Deliberate blocking of an overtaking athlete — time penalty or DSQ depending on severity and whether it influenced the final result
  • Unauthorized assistance: Receiving pushing, pacing, or physical aid from team personnel or spectators — DSQ
  • Course cutting: Deviating from the marked course to gain an advantage — DSQ
  • Fluorinated wax: use in competition results in disqualification (ICR 222.8, 531.7.2)

7.3 General Violations

  • Unsportsmanlike conduct in either phase: warning, time penalty, or DSQ at the jury’s discretion
  • Missing mandatory equipment control checks: athlete may not start the respective phase
  • Late arrival at the start: in jumping, the athlete forfeits their jump; in cross-country, the athlete starts at their designated time regardless
  • Doping violations: handled per WADA code; immediate provisional suspension and results invalidation

Section 8: Safety Considerations

8.1 Jumping Phase Safety

  • FIS-approved helmet mandatory for all jumps; chin strap must be fastened
  • Competition jury monitors wind conditions continuously and may stop the round if conditions are unsafe
  • In-run gate adjusted by the jury based on wind, snow, and athlete safety; athletes cannot request gate changes
  • Landing area maintained with groomed and shaped snow; minimum snow depth requirements enforced
  • Medical team with stretcher positioned at the base of the landing hill; ambulance on standby
  • Fall protocol: if an athlete falls during landing, competition is paused to clear the outrun before the next jumper

8.2 Cross-Country Phase Safety

  • If the temperature is below -20 °C (-4 °F), measured at the coldest point of the course, the Jury postpones or cancels the competition (ICR 502.1.2.4)
  • Athletes showing signs of hypothermia, exhaustion, or collapse are removed by medical personnel

8.3 Transition Area Safety

  • Transition area must be fenced and controlled to prevent unauthorized access
  • Non-slip surfaces or mats at the equipment change area to prevent falls
  • Team support personnel limited to designated areas
  • Equipment laid out in pre-assigned positions to avoid collisions between athletes during simultaneous transitions

8.4 General Safety Provisions

  • Competition may be cancelled, postponed, or shortened at any time if conditions become unsafe for either discipline
  • If the jumping phase is completed but the cross-country cannot be held (e.g., extreme weather), the competition may be rescheduled or the jumping results may stand as provisional (jury decision)
  • All venues must have an emergency action plan reviewed by local emergency services before competition
  • Anti-doping controls conducted per WADA and FIS Anti-Doping Rules; selected athletes tested after competition
