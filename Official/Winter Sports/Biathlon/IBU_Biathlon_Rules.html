Section 1: Introduction

1.1 Overview and Governing Body

Biathlon combines cross-country skiing and precision rifle marksmanship into a single gruelling competition. The sport has been on the Olympic programme since 1960 (men) and 1992 (women). It is governed by the International Biathlon Union (IBU) under the IBU Event and Competition Rules (ECR). The IBU, headquartered in Salzburg, Austria, oversees the World Cup circuit, World Championships, and Olympic qualification. Biathlon's roots trace to Scandinavian and northern European military patrol traditions dating to the 18th century.

1.2 Olympic Events

  • Individual: 20 km (men), 15 km (women) (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). The original and longest biathlon event with 4 shooting stages and a 1-minute time penalty per miss (Rule 1.4.10.4).
  • Sprint: 10 km (men), 7.5 km (women) (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). Two shooting stages with penalty loops for misses (Rule 1.4.10.4).
  • Pursuit: 12.5 km (men), 10 km (women) (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). Staggered start based on the results of the qualifying competition (Rule 1.7.6). Four shooting stages (Rule 1.4.10.4).
  • Mass Start: 15 km (men), 12.5 km (women) (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). All athletes start simultaneously (Rule 6.1.4); the field is 30 competitors (Rule 13.4.1). Four shooting stages (Rule 1.4.10.4).
  • Relay: 4×7.5 km (men), 4×6 km (women) (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). Each team member completes 2 shooting stages (Rule 1.4.10.4).
  • Mixed Relay: 4×6 km, with all four legs 6 km; the order of men and women can be either way round (Rule 1.4.3).

1.3 Historical Development

Biathlon evolved from the military patrol event, which appeared at the 1924 Winter Olympics but was not included regularly until 1960. The sport transitioned from large-calibre military rifles to standardised .22 LR small-bore rifles in 1978, making the shooting component safer and more precise. The introduction of electronic target systems in the 1980s replaced manual target marking, enabling real-time scoring and television-friendly presentation. Women's events were first contested at the World Championships in 1984 and entered the Olympics in 1992.

Section 2: Equipment

2.1 Biathlon Rifle

  • Calibre: .22 Long Rifle (LR) rimfire, 5.6 mm bore diameter. This is the only calibre permitted in biathlon competition.
  • Action: Bolt-action (straight-pull bolt is standard for speed). No automatic or semi-automatic actions permitted.
  • Minimum weight: 3.5 kg (7.7 lb) including the carrying harness and magazine. Rifle specifications are stipulated in the IBU Materials Catalogue, Annex A (Rule 4.1).
  • Magazine: Capacity of 5 rounds. The five rounds required in each bout may be loaded with the use of a magazine (Rule 8.4.2). Relay spare rounds must be hand-loaded one by one, not from a magazine (Rule 8.2.2).
  • Sights: Open/iron sights only. No optical sights, telescopic sights, lens systems, or red-dot sights permitted. Diopter rear sight and ring/post front sight are standard.
  • Trigger: The rifle trigger resistance must be at least 0.5 kg (Rule 4.2.3).
  • Stock: Custom-fitted to the athlete. May include adjustable cheekpiece and buttpad. No electronic components permitted in the stock.

2.2 Ammunition

  • Type: .22 LR rimfire cartridges. Athletes select and test their own ammunition for optimal accuracy and reliability in cold conditions.
  • Quantity per stage: 5 rounds per bout. In relay events, athletes have 3 spare rounds per shooting stage, hand-loaded one by one after the first five are fired (Rule 8.2.2).
  • Cold weather performance: Athletes often warm ammunition in body-heat pockets before loading.

2.3 Skis and Poles

  • Technique: All skiing techniques are permitted (Rule 7.1.1). In practice athletes use free technique (skating).
  • Ski specifications: Per FIS cross-country regulations. Minimum ski length based on athlete height.
  • Wax restrictions: Skis are checked for prohibited preparation materials, which are specified in Annex A (Rule 4.2.2.1).
  • Poles: Standard cross-country skating poles. No restrictions beyond FIS specifications for pole length and construction.

2.4 Carrying Harness and Shooting Mat

  • Rifle harness: Custom-fitted harness worn on the back. Rifle must be secured with the barrel pointing upward at all times during skiing. Quick-release mechanism for rapid transition to shooting position.
  • Shooting mat: Mats are placed with the front edge on the firing line in the middle of the lane for both prone and standing shooting; at IBU events they must be 200 cm × 150 cm and 1 to 2 cm thick (Rule 3.4.3.2).

Section 3: Playing Area

3.1 Shooting Range

  • Distance: 50 m (164 ft) ± 1 m from the firing line to the targets (Rule 3.4.2.1).
  • Lanes: The range must have 30 shooting lanes at all IBU competitions (Rule 3.4.4.1). Each lane must be at least 2.75 m but not more than 3 m wide (Rule 3.4.3.1).
  • Prone targets: 5 circular metal plates, each 45 mm (1.77 in) in diameter. The target falls (closes) when hit, providing immediate visual feedback.
  • Standing targets: 5 circular metal plates, each 115 mm (4.53 in) in diameter. Larger targets compensate for the inherent instability of standing shooting after intense physical exertion.
  • Electronic target system: Only metal and fully electronic targets may be used for competition; at OWG, WCH, WC and other televised events, targets must have remote reset and electronic target scoring data (Rule 3.4.4.1).
  • Wind indicators: Wind flags must be installed beside every second shooting lane, 20 m from the target (Rule 3.4.6). There is no wind compensation in scoring — athletes must adjust their aim based on real-time conditions.

3.2 Cross-Country Course

  • Sprint course: 3 loops of 3.3 km (men) or 2.5 km (women) (Rule 1.4.10.4), with the course passing through the stadium and shooting range area between loops.
  • Individual course: 5 loops of 4 km (men) or 3 km (women) (Rule 1.4.10.4), with 4 passes through the shooting range.
  • Terrain profile: The maximum height difference on the course is 80 m, the maximum single climb is 50 m and no climb may exceed a 25 percent grade (Rule 1.4.10.1, Rule 1.4.10.2, Rule 1.4.10.3).
  • Course width: At least 6 m of groomed snow surface; unavoidable narrow sections may not be less than 4 m wide for not longer than 50 m (Rule 3.3.2).

3.3 Penalty Loop and Stadium

  • Penalty loop: 150 m (± 5 m) oval, 5–6 m wide, no further than 60 m skiing distance from the range (Rule 3.3.11). Athletes ski one loop for each missed target immediately after the shooting bout (in Sprint, Pursuit, Mass Start, and Relay events) (Rule 7.1.5). Completing one loop typically adds roughly 20–25 seconds to the athlete's total time.
  • Stadium layout: Located in the stadium area are the start/finish areas, shooting range, penalty loop, relay hand-over zone and spectator areas (Rule 3.1).
  • Altitude: No part of the course may be higher than 2,000 m above sea level unless an exception is authorized by the IBU Executive Board (Rule 3.3.2).

Section 4: Players & Officials

4.1 Competitors

  • Individual events: The Olympic national quota is based on the World Cup Nations Cup ranking, the IBU Qualifying Points list and the host nation quota, with the final system agreed between the IOC and IBU (Rule 13.4).
  • Mass Start: Limited to 30 competitors, based on the World Cup Total Score, medal winners and points from the current championship (Rule 12.6.1.3, Rule 13.4.1).
  • Pursuit: The top 60 from the qualifying competition are entitled to start (Rule 12.6.1.2, Rule 15.6.4). Start times are based on time gaps in the qualifying competition (Rule 1.7.6).
  • Relay: Each nation fields a team of 4 athletes (Rule 1.4.1, Rule 1.4.2). Names must be entered in writing by the stated deadline (Rule 1.6.2).

4.2 Competition Jury and Officials

  • Technical Delegate (TD): Appointed by the IBU Technical Committee (Rule 2.6.1) and chairs the Competition Jury (Rule 2.3.1.2). Oversees rule compliance, course homologation, and equipment controls.
  • Chief of Competition: Manages the organising committee and event logistics.
  • Range Officer: Controls access to the shooting range, manages firing lane assignments, and ensures safety protocols are followed at all times.
  • Course Chief: Responsible for course preparation, grooming, and safety inspections.
  • Shooting Judges: Monitor electronic target displays and verify hit/miss registration. May be called upon to resolve disputed shots (e.g., simultaneous hit from adjacent lane).
  • Timing and Data: Competition times are registered to 1/10 (0.1) of a second (Rule 9.3.2). Photo-finish data is used to separate close finishes in Pursuit, Mass Start and Relay at top events (Rule 9.2.5).

4.3 Start Procedures

  • Individual/Sprint: Single interval start, normally at 30-second intervals; shorter or longer intervals are permitted if best for the competition (Rule 6.1.2).
  • Pursuit: Time-gap start based on the qualifying competition. The winner starts first; subsequent athletes start with their time deficit, rounded to the nearest second (Rule 1.7.6, Rule 6.1.3). Athletes more than four minutes behind start together four minutes after the first start (Rule 1.7.6).
  • Mass Start: Simultaneous start for all athletes (Rule 6.1.4). Each start number designates the start position (Rule 1.7.7).
  • Relay: The first-leg athletes start simultaneously; subsequent legs begin with a tag in the hand-over zone (Rule 6.1.5).

Section 5: Rules of Play

5.1 Individual Event (20 km Men / 15 km Women)

The longest and most traditional biathlon event. Athletes ski 5 loops (4 km each for men, 3 km each for women) with 4 shooting stages in alternating order: prone, standing, prone, standing (Rule 1.4.10.4). Each missed target adds a 1-minute time penalty to the athlete's total ski time (Rule 1.4.10.4, Rule 9.2.1). There is no penalty loop — the time addition is applied to the final result.

5.2 Sprint Event (10 km Men / 7.5 km Women)

Three skiing loops with 2 shooting stages: first prone, then standing (Rule 1.4.10.4). Each missed target requires the athlete to ski one 150 m penalty loop (Rule 3.3.11, Rule 7.1.5). The Sprint is the usual qualifying competition for the Pursuit (Rule 1.7.6).

5.3 Pursuit Event (12.5 km Men / 10 km Women)

The top 60 finishers from the qualifying competition start at intervals corresponding to their time gaps from the winner (Rule 1.7.6, Rule 15.6.4). Five skiing loops with 4 shooting stages: prone, prone, standing, standing (Rule 1.4.10.4). Penalty loops for misses. The first athlete across the finish line wins (Rule 9.2.2.2) — it is a head-to-head chase format where the qualifying advantage carries forward.

5.4 Mass Start (15 km Men / 12.5 km Women)

All 30 athletes start simultaneously in a mass start (Rule 6.1.4, Rule 13.4.1). Five skiing loops with 4 shooting stages: prone, prone, standing, standing (Rule 1.4.10.4). Athletes must shoot from the lane designated by their start number in the first bout and fill the range sequentially from the right in order of arrival in subsequent bouts (Rule 8.2.1). Penalty loops for misses. The first across the finish line wins (Rule 9.2.2.2).

5.5 Relay (4×7.5 km Men / 4×6 km Women)

Each team of 4 athletes skis one leg consisting of 3 skiing loops and 2 shooting stages (prone, standing) (Rule 1.4.10.4). In relay, athletes have 3 spare rounds per shooting stage in addition to the first five (8 total per stage) (Rule 8.2.2). Spare rounds must be hand-loaded one by one, not loaded directly from a magazine (Rule 8.2.2). After all 8 rounds are fired, any remaining misses result in penalty loops. The hand-over takes place in a designated hand-over zone, where the incoming athlete must touch the outgoing athlete's body or rifle with a hand (Rule 6.3.5).

5.6 Mixed Relay

The Mixed Relay is 4×6 km, with all four legs 6 km; the order of men and women can be either way round (Rule 1.4.3). Same relay shooting rules apply: 5 rounds + 3 spare per stage (Rule 8.2.2). The Mixed Relay is typically one of the shortest biathlon events, making it spectator-friendly and fast-paced.

5.7 Shooting Procedure

Upon arriving at the range, athletes enter their lane, remove the rifle from the harness, assume the shooting position (prone or standing), and engage the 5 targets. In prone shooting, the rifle may only be in contact with the athlete's hands, shoulder and cheek, and the wrist of the supporting arm must be distinctly raised from the ground (Rule 8.3.1). In standing shooting, the athlete must stand without any support (Rule 8.3.2). After firing, athletes return the rifle to the carrying position before resuming skiing (Rule 8.5.4) and ski any penalty loops immediately (Rule 7.1.5).

Section 6: Scoring

6.1 Time-Based Results

All biathlon events are determined by total elapsed time, and the competition time always includes any penalties or adjustments (Rule 9.2). Times are recorded to 1/10 of a second (Rule 9.3.2). In Pursuit and Mass Start, the first competitor to cross the finish line wins (Rule 9.2.2.2).

6.2 Individual Event Penalties

In the Individual event, each missed target adds 1 minute (60 seconds) to the athlete's total time (Rule 1.4.10.4, Rule 9.2.1). With 20 possible targets across 4 stages, a perfect shooting score adds 0 penalty minutes, while a worst-case scenario adds 20 minutes. Elite athletes typically miss 0–3 targets.

6.3 Penalty Loop Time Cost

In Sprint, Pursuit, Mass Start, and Relay events, each missed target requires skiing one 150 m penalty loop (Rule 3.3.11, Rule 7.1.5). Elite athletes typically complete the loop in roughly 20–25 seconds, depending on course conditions and fatigue, so misses accumulate rapidly across 2–4 stages per event.

6.4 Relay Scoring

The team's total time is the elapsed time from the start of the first member to the finish of the last member (Rule 9.2.4). All penalty loop time is included. The spare-round system in relay means that misses cost time (hand-loading individual rounds is slower than magazine shooting) but may avoid the even greater cost of a full penalty loop.

6.5 World Cup Points

World Cup points are awarded based on finishing position: 1st = 90 points, 2nd = 75, 3rd = 65, decreasing down to 40th = 1 point (Rule 15.8.2.1). Mass Start and Super Sprint use a separate scale down to 30th place (Rule 15.8.2.1). Points accumulate over the season to determine the World Cup standings, and Nations Cup points are awarded separately (Rule 15.8.2).

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

7.1 Range Safety Violations

  • Muzzle direction: The rifle muzzle must be over the firing line from the beginning to the end of the shooting bout, and an athlete may never deliberately point it at themselves or others (Rule 8.5.1). Violating shooting safety regulations results in disqualification (Rule 11.3.4).
  • Loading and unloading: After starting, the bolt may not be opened or the magazine removed except on the shooting mat, and rifles must be unloaded after each shooting bout (Rule 8.5.2, Rule 8.5.2.1).
  • Aimed shots: All shots must be aimed and fired at the targets only (Rule 8.5.3).

7.2 Competition Violations

  • Early start: An athlete who starts early must return and start again, and the lost time counts against them (Rule 6.4.5); in single-start events, starting outside the start window without returning brings a 30-second penalty (Rule 11.3.1). In the Pursuit, starting more than three seconds early is a disqualification (Rule 11.3.4).
  • Cross-fire: Hits on the wrong target are not scored (Rule 8.7.3.2) and a 2-minute penalty applies (Rule 11.3.3).
  • Rounds not fired: Recommencing skiing before firing all five shots (or all eight in a Relay) without hitting all targets brings a 2-minute penalty for every round not fired (Rule 11.3.3).
  • Equipment violation: Appearing at the start with non-compliant equipment leads to a start prohibition (Rule 11.2); modifying inspected equipment in a prohibited way is a disqualification (Rule 11.3.4).
  • Course cutting: Deviating from the marked trail, skiing a wrong trail, or skiing loops in the wrong sequence or direction — DSQ (Rule 11.3.4).

7.3 Sanctions

  • Sanction types: Penalties and disciplinary measures are reprimands, start prohibitions, time penalties, disqualifications, suspension and fines (Rule 11).
  • Time penalties: Thirty seconds, one minute or two minutes depending on the offence (Rule 11.3.1, Rule 11.3.2, Rule 11.3.3).
  • Suspension and fines: Gross violations may lead to suspension (Rule 11.5) and fines (Rule 11.6).

Section 8: Safety Considerations

8.1 Range Safety Protocols

  • Barrel direction: When carrying the rifle on the back, the barrel must point up (Rule 7.1.2). Rifles may be loaded and unloaded only with the barrel pointing toward the targets or up (Rule 8.5.2).
  • Rifle inspection: Rifles are checked before the start for weight, trigger resistance, dimensions and shape (Rule 4.2.2.1).
  • Forward of the firing line: When the range is open for shooting, no one is permitted to be forward of the firing line (Rule 8.5.1).
  • Backstop: The range must be surrounded by adequate safety berms on the sides and behind the targets (Rule 3.4.1).

8.2 Course Safety

  • Course marking: The course must be so clearly marked that competitors are at no time in doubt as to how to follow it, and each loop length has its own colour (Rule 3.3.6, Rule 3.3.6.1).
  • Temperature limits: Competitions must not be started if the air temperature is colder than −20°C (−4°F) at the coldest part of the site (Rule 6.6.1); below −15°C, wind chill and humidity must be considered (Rule 6.6.2).

8.3 Ammunition Handling

  • Ammunition removal: At the end of training and competitions, all ammunition must be removed from both the stock and the magazines (Rule 8.5.2.1).
  • Misfires: Misfired or lost rounds may be replaced from the athlete's own spares, or from a range official on request (Rule 8.6.1).
