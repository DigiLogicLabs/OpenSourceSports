Section 1: Introduction

Karate Combat is a US-based professional stand-up karate promotion founded in 2018. Unlike Olympic-style WKF Karate (which uses point-stop scoring with light contact), Karate Combat is full-contact, continuous-action — closer in pacing to MMA/kickboxing — but restricted to stand-up striking only (no ground fighting). Fights take place in the distinctive "Pit" arena: a sunken, slanted-wall enclosure with no cage and no ropes, designed to keep action centered. Karate Combat has grown rapidly via streaming + crypto-partnership distribution, attracting traditional karate practitioners alongside kickboxers and Muay Thai fighters. All fights are conducted per the Karate Combat Rules and Regulations published at karate.com/en/rules-and-regulations, which govern equipment, conduct, scoring, and safety across every sanctioned event.

The Rules and Regulations note that Karate Combat's rules are always subordinate to, and may be revoked or modified by, the applicable laws and ethical standards of the country or state where an event is organized, and to the requirements of the supervising Athletic Commission, Sanctioning Organization, or other approved Regulatory Body.

Section 2: Equipment

  • Karate Combat mitts: per the Rules and Regulations, contestants shall wear Karate Combat mitts with a net weight of four ounces — open-palm, fingerless design for grip-and-clinch defense.
  • Mouthguard: mandatory.
  • Groin protector: mandatory (men); chest protector required for women.
  • Shin guards / instep pads: not standard equipment (more karate-style than kickboxing).
  • Karate gi top + black shorts: required for branding and competition consistency.
  • Competitors fight barefoot.

Section 3: Playing Area

  • "The Pit": Karate Combat's proprietary sunken-floor arena (~25 ft diameter circular floor, slanted walls).
  • No cage, no ropes — sloped walls keep action centered and prevent fighters from resting on a cage or ropes.
  • Padded floor and walls; lighting and camera positions are configured for live broadcast.

Section 4: Players & Officials

  • Two competitors per match.
  • Per the Rules and Regulations' weight-class provisions, events feature single-match contests between well-matched, skilled karate fighters in the same weight division. Men's divisions: Strawweight, Flyweight, Bantamweight, Featherweight, Lightweight, Welterweight, Middleweight, Light Heavyweight, Heavyweight. Women's divisions: Flyweight, Bantamweight, Featherweight.
  • Bouts may also be contracted at a Catchweight with the permission of the presiding Commission.
  • Officials: 1 in-Pit referee, 3 judges, a ringside physician, and a commission representative where a state athletic commission applies.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Match Length

  • Per the Rules and Regulations, each non-title Karate Combat contest is 3 rounds of 3 minutes each, with a 1-minute rest period between rounds.
  • Title/Championship bouts are 5 rounds of 3 minutes each — two additional 3-minute rounds beyond the standard non-title format.
  • If the judges' scorecards result in a Draw or a Split Decision, one additional 3-minute Sudden Victory round is contested to determine the winner — including in Championship bouts, for a maximum of six 3-minute rounds.

Permitted Strikes

  • Punches (closed fist, gloved)
  • Kicks (legs, body, head)
  • Knee strikes (body + head, including jumping knees)
  • Spinning techniques fully legal

Prohibited Techniques

  • No elbows (since the early seasons; subject to change by edition)
  • No takedowns (any attempt = referee reset)
  • No ground fighting (if both fall, immediate stand-up)
  • No clinch beyond ~5 seconds (referee separates)

Knockdowns + Knockouts

  • Standing 10-count rule
  • Three-knockdown rule per round = TKO
  • KO = total victory; TKO via referee/doctor stoppage

Section 6: Scoring

  • 10-Point Must scoring (boxing-style), based on effective fighting.
  • Judges weight clean technical strikes (kicks heavier than punches by tradition), aggression, ring/Pit generalship, and defensive technique.
  • Title fights produce championship rankings (Karate Combat World Title per weight class).
  • Crypto-partnered scoring system: fan-voted "$KARATE" governance influences event selection (separate from in-fight scoring).

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

  • Illegal techniques (elbows, takedowns, eye-poke, throat strikes, back-of-head, spine): warning → point deduction → DQ.
  • Excessive clinching: warning + referee reset.
  • Hitting after the bell: point deduction.
  • Faking injury: point deduction.
  • Unsportsmanlike conduct: DQ.
  • Anti-doping: state athletic commission (where applicable) plus Karate Combat's own anti-doping policy at championship level.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Karate Combat is full-contact stand-up combat with KO/concussion risk comparable to professional kickboxing. Mandatory pre-fight medical screening (neurological, cardiac, ophthalmological, MRI for major fights), a ringside physician with stoppage authority, on-site EMT and ambulance, and an AED in the arena are all standard. Post-fight medical assessment is mandatory. KOs trigger automatic medical suspension (minimum 30-day rest, longer for severe KOs, with neurological clearance required to return). State athletic commission oversight applies at events held in commission-regulated jurisdictions. The Pit floor is padded, and its slanted walls absorb falls more safely than a vertical cage. Concussion protocol includes SCAT-5 assessment and a medical-clearance requirement before return to competition.
