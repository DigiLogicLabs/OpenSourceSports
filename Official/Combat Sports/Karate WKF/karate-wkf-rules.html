Section 1: Introduction

Karate is a striking martial art of Okinawan/Japanese origin, governed internationally by the World Karate Federation (WKF), the sport's IOC-recognized governing body. Competitive WKF karate is organized under the WKF Kumite Competition Rules and WKF Kata Competition Rules, published together as part of the WKF's official Rules and Regulations. These rules govern the two disciplines contested at WKF-sanctioned events, including World Championships and (as of the Tokyo 2020 Olympic programme) Olympic competition: Kumite (sparring, a semi-contact combat discipline in which two competitors face off) and Kata (a solo or team performance of a prearranged, codified sequence of offensive and defensive techniques, judged for technical and athletic execution).

Under WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 1 defines karate as a discipline in which competitors must exercise control (Article 8, "Prohibited Behavior") to avoid injuring an opponent while still demonstrating decisive, well-formed technique — the concept of controlled contact is the philosophical core of the ruleset, distinguishing WKF karate from full-contact striking sports.

This rulebook summarizes the core competition regulations for both Kumite and Kata as codified by the WKF, current for the 2025–2026 competitive season.

Section 2: Equipment

Karategi (uniform): Per WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 2, competitors must wear a plain white karate uniform (karategi) bearing no design, piping, or writing other than the competitor's national/club emblem and, where applicable, WKF-approved manufacturer markings within specified size limits. The jacket, when belted, must reach a minimum of hip length but not exceed three-quarters thigh length; sleeves must not extend past the wrist crease or be shorter than half the forearm. Pants must reach at least to within 5 cm of the ankle bone.

Belt (obi): One competitor wears a red belt ("Aka") and the other a blue belt ("Ao") to allow officials and electronic scoring systems to distinguish competitors; belt color/rank grading is otherwise indicated by the standard kyu/dan color system.

Mandatory protective equipment (Kumite): Per WKF equipment regulations, the following WKF-approved gear is compulsory for Kumite competitors: WKF-approved hand mitts (Aka/Ao colored), a mouthguard, and shin and instep guards. A groin guard is compulsory for male competitors (worn under the gi). A chest protector is compulsory for female competitors in most age categories. Gloves, mitts, and pads must bear the WKF approval logo; equipment lacking approval markings may be rejected by the Kansa (arbitrator) prior to the match.

Prohibited items: Hard or metal-framed eyewear, jewelry, and any object capable of injuring an opponent are forbidden. Fingernails and toenails must be cut short. Contact lenses are permitted at the competitor's own risk.

Kata equipment: No hand or foot protective gear is worn for Kata; competitors perform in the karategi only, and may use a bladed weapon (for Kobudo-style Kata events, where sanctioned) subject to separate WKF weapons regulations.

Section 3: Playing Area

Per WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 1 ("Competition Area"), the competition area (tatami) is a flat, hazard-free surface measured 8 metres by 8 metres (approx. 26 ft 3 in x 26 ft 3 in), measured from the outside of the boundary line, plus an additional 1-metre (approx. 3 ft 3 in) safety area extending outside the competition area on all sides. The total area including the safety zone therefore measures approximately 10 metres by 10 metres (approx. 32 ft 10 in x 32 ft 10 in). The floor may be a raised platform up to 1 metre high.

The area is marked with lines indicating the starting positions of both competitors (approximately 1.5 m either side of the center point) and the position of the Referee (Shushin). The Kata competition area uses the same 8m x 8m core dimensions per the WKF Kata Competition Rules, Article 1, to standardize the performance space across both disciplines.

Section 4: Players & Officials

Kumite competitors compete individually or as part of a team, organized by gender, age category (e.g., Cadet, Junior, U21, Senior), and weight category. WKF Senior Kumite weight categories are periodically reviewed by the WKF Executive Committee; current WKF Senior divisions include multiple men's and women's weight classes ranging from lightweight to a heavyweight ("+"; over-weight) division, contested individually, plus an open-weight Team Kumite event.

Match officials (per WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 4, "Judging Body"): Each match is controlled by a Shushin (Referee), who stands inside the competition area, directs the match, calls scores, and has final authority on scoring decisions and penalties. The Shushin is assisted by corner judges — historically called Fukushin — under the modern electronic flag/scoring-table system, along with a Kansa (Match Area Controller) who monitors the safety zone and timing, a Scorer, a Timekeeper, and a Recorder. Decisions on scoring are made via an electronic scoring system operated by the judging panel.

Kata judging panel (per WKF Kata Competition Rules, Article 7): Kata matches are scored by a panel of judges — five judges in preliminary rounds and seven judges in final rounds of major WKF events — who independently score each performance.

Section 5: Rules of Play

Kumite match duration (WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 5, "Duration of Match"): Senior Individual Kumite matches last 3 minutes for both men and women; Team Kumite matches also last 3 minutes. Cadet and Junior category matches are typically shortened to 2 minutes per WKF youth category regulations. The clock stops whenever the Shushin calls "Yame" (stop).

Starting and restarting: Competitors bow to each other and to the officials at the start and end of the match. The match begins on the Shushin's command "Shobu Hajime" and is paused with "Yame" following a scoring action, a boundary exit (Jogai), an injury, or any situation requiring the Shushin's judgment. Competitors return to their starting lines to resume with "Tsuzukete Hajime."

Match outcome (WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 6): A match is won by the competitor who: (a) achieves a clear lead of 8 points or more at any time, ending the match immediately ("mercy rule"); (b) has the higher score when time expires; or (c) is awarded the decision by Hantei (judges' majority decision) if scores are tied at time expiration. A match may also be won by disqualification (Hansoku), withdrawal (Kiken), or the opponent being declared unable to continue (Shikkaku for the offending party).

Kata performance (WKF Kata Competition Rules, Articles 3–5): Competitors perform a Kata selected from the official WKF Kata list ("Tokui" list for the Shitei/Tokui system), announcing the Kata name to the panel before beginning. Team Kata additionally requires synchronized performance by three competitors, followed by a Bunkai (practical application) demonstration in elimination and medal rounds at major events. Judges evaluate technical performance (stances, technique, transitional movements, timing, correct breathing, focus/Kime) and athletic performance (strength, speed, balance, rhythm) per Article 4.

Section 6: Scoring

Kumite scoring categories (WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 6, "Scoring Criteria"):

  • Yuko (1 point): Tsuki (punch) or Uchi (strike) delivered with correct form to a scoring area (Jodan or Chudan).
  • Waza-ari (2 points): Chudan Geri — a kick delivered to the body/trunk.
  • Ippon (3 points): Jodan Geri — a kick delivered to the head/face/neck — and any scoring technique delivered against a competitor who has been knocked down or thrown.

To be awarded, a scoring technique must meet six criteria set out in Article 6: good form, correct attitude, vigorous application, awareness/zanshin (remaining alert and in control after the technique), correct timing, and correct distance (delivered to a scoring area with accurate control, generally with skin contact permitted to Jodan only when contact is light and controlled).

Scoring areas: Jodan (head, face, neck) and Chudan (abdomen, chest, back, side) are valid target areas. Attacks below the belt, to the throat, or to the joints/groin are not valid scoring targets and are treated as violations under Article 8.

Kata scoring (WKF Kata Competition Rules, Article 5): Each judge awards a score typically on a scale from 5.0 to 10.0 in increments of 0.1 (or as configured by the Judging Committee), reflecting technical performance (weighted 70%) and athletic performance (weighted 30%). The highest and lowest of the panel's scores are discarded, and the remaining scores are summed to produce the competitor's or team's final result, used to rank performances head-to-head in a flag/majority-based bracket system at major events.

Section 7: Violations & Penalties

Categories of prohibited behavior (WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 8): Violations are grouped into two categories:

  • Category 1 (C1): Techniques that display excessive or uncontrolled contact, contact to the throat, attacks to the arms, legs, groin, joints, or instep, attacks to the face with open-hand techniques, and dangerous or forbidden techniques that risk injury.
  • Category 2 (C2): Feigning or exaggerating an injury, exiting the competition area (Jogai) without cause, self-endangerment through repeated exposure to injury (e.g., turning the back on an opponent), simulated attacks or passivity avoiding engagement, grabbing/seizing without immediately executing a scoring technique or throw, and unsportsmanlike conduct.

Penalty progression: Within each category, penalties escalate on repeated offenses through four stages: Chukoku (first-level warning, no direct point award), Keikoku (warning accompanied by an equivalent point award to the opponent), Hansoku-chui (a more severe warning with a larger point award to the opponent), and Hansoku (disqualification from the match, with victory awarded to the opponent). A single sufficiently serious act (e.g., a technique causing injury through excessive, uncontrolled contact) can result in an immediate Hansoku without progressing through the lower stages, at the Shushin's discretion in consultation with the judging panel.

Shikkaku: A separate, more severe disqualification sanction (Article 9) applies to conduct that violates the spirit of the competition — for example, ignoring the instructions of the Shushin, acts of extreme unsportsmanlike conduct, or deliberate dangerous technique intended to injure. A competitor issued Shikkaku is removed from the entire tournament, not merely the match.

Section 8: Safety Considerations

Controlled contact principle: WKF Kumite is built around the principle of "control" (Article 8): competitors must demonstrate the ability to stop or pull a technique short of injurious contact, particularly to the head. Techniques executed with excessive force or that make uncontrolled, heavy contact are penalized as Category 1 violations, regardless of whether the technique would otherwise have scored.

Injury procedure (WKF Kumite Competition Rules, Article 10, "Injury and Accident"): When a competitor is injured, the Shushin calls "Yame" and consults the ringside doctor (or event medical staff) as required. A competitor unable to continue after examination may be declared the loser via Kiken-gachi (win by withdrawal/default) awarded to the opponent, unless the injury was caused by the opponent's rule-violating technique, in which case Hansoku may be declared against the offending competitor and victory given to the injured competitor.

Mandatory protective equipment (hand mitts, mouthguard, shin/instep guards, and groin/chest protection as applicable — see Section 2) is required specifically to mitigate injury risk during permitted, controlled-contact exchanges, and referees may halt a match to check equipment compliance at any time.

Medical oversight: WKF-sanctioned events require an accredited medical team on-site with authority to examine any competitor at the request of the Shushin or Kansa and to recommend suspension of a competitor from further competition in the tournament following a concussive or otherwise serious injury.
